MY STORY


On the 9th of December 2004, I was diagnosed with Stage IIIb Testicular Cancer. On the 10th December 2004, amongst obvious discussions about my forthcoming and required treatment, I was told that if no treatment were to be given, then I would have... "just less than a year to live, possibly only months" This website covers my journey throughout the fight with my Cancer, not only my initial suspicions but my day to day battle both in hospital and out, it covers the complete story. If you're in doubt about anything that might be wrong with you, don't hang about, make sure you get seen to and seen to properly, not once but time and time again until you are happy with the result. As you will see from my experience, things can be easily missed in our busy lives... The Chemotherapy treatment I am to receive will most likely drain my energy, so I'm using this as an outlet for you all to find out how I am and save me telling the story a hundred times, I hope you understand. Throughout the site I have links referencing particular sites to explain things further, these will be underlined. Thank you for all of your support and kind wishes, it is much appreciated. The story begins here...

June 2003

After having my suspicions of a slight irregularity down in my nether regions I decided to pay a visit to my GP for a physical examination, my GP being quite dismissive claimed that a physical examination would not be necessary and instead lifted my arms and legs and asked if I had any pain anywhere??? I am not a doctor but surely after asking for a physical examination I should have at least been given one and after my peculiarly fashioned GP examination I was left with no answer to my original suspicions. So I pondered on this for 2 weeks and decided to go to my local walk in GUM Clinic (Genito-Urinary Medicine) to request a physical examination, which I was promptly given. The lady that examined me deliberated for a bit and finally said all felt fine and my initial fears were laid to rest and I worried no more.

January/February 2004

A slight swelling in the right hand side of my nether regions had occurred and I thought that I had simply knocked myself as it wasn't that painful or uncomfortable, after a week and a bit it had subsided and no difference in shape or size was noticed than from before. I did wonder if I should go for another checkup to be sure and either became too busy or I simply wasn't that concerned as my fears of a problem had previously been dispelled.

October 18th - 22nd 2004

I had a weird pain in my back on the left had side around my rib cage, almost like I had pulled a muscle it went away after a couple of days and I really thought nothing of it.

Late October 2004 through to the end of November 2004

I slowly developed a cough, dry but irritable and producing no flem. A few more of the strange pulled muscle feelings popped up around the front and back of my ribcage both on the left and right which were a little painful at times in terms of general movement and coughing/breathing. Again they subsided but I certainly thought something was up. The most noticeable symptom of illness was an intense sweating at night, so much so that from the base of my head to the bottom of my back both front and back would literally leak like a tap and soak my sheets so much so I could not sleep on that side of the bed anymore.

November 29th 2004 - Monday

I decided that a visit to the GP was needed for at least some antibiotics to attack what I thought was a nasty infection or flu that needed to be cleared. I was seen by the same dismissive GP and prescribed over 2000mg of antibiotics to take a day. I asked as I left that I thought perhaps a chest Xray would be needed if the antibiotics did not work and was told very quickly that it "might be considered" if there was no change. I went straight to the chemist that same afternoon. The following 7 days I took my antibiotics religiously, ate extra healthily and drank no alcohol to get over what I thought was a nasty flu. That's everything leading up to my diagnosis. My diary continues with December 2004 and the treatment that followed.


DECEMBER 2004


6th December 2004 - Monday

Antibiotics course finished and no real change noticed.

8th December 2004 - Wednesday

Growing concern, booked another appointment with a different GP at my surgery for Friday 10th at 5pm.

9th December 2004 - Thursday

After yet another sweaty night (these had become such a regular occurrence now over the past few weeks) I awoke with what felt like yet another pulled muscle pain in my ribcage area, this time around on my back on the left hand side.

I was becoming increasingly fed up with my symptoms and decided that the GP would only prolong any further treatment I may need, and a visit to the A&E for a routine check-up and X-ray would be a better option. So, without further delay or cancelling my GP appointment, I paid a visit to West Middlesex Hospital's brand new A&E department.

I waited just under an hour at A&E to see someone, a brilliant waiting time for an NHS A&E.

Some routine checks were carried out and a cautionary X-ray was ordered immediately without any real concerns or suspicion attached.

I was sent for an X-ray some 20 minutes later. The radiographer asked after zapping me if I had ever had a chest X-ray before, I noticed an air of concern come over her face and I answered candidly "no".

Upon my return to A&E I was given a blood test. I noticed several doctors looking at a computer screen and then looking over at me in amazement. Now I was really starting to sense something wasn't quite right, but kept my cool.

A doctor approached me and led me into a curtained cubicle. He pulled the curtains closed after our entrance; the look on his face told me he had bad news before a single word had passed his lips. "We've found some dark patches on your X-ray" he said cautiously. I asked for a straight explanation and, after an initial shock and a cup of water, the facts started to sink in.

It was explained that I had up to 8 or 9 lesions the size of 20p pieces on each of my lungs and the chances of it being an infection was 20% versus the 80% chance of it being CANCER!!!

I was admitted to West Middlesex Hospital that evening for an overnight stay in the Medical Assessment Unit (MAU), a CT scan and Ultrasound scan scheduled for the morning.

10th December 2004 - Friday

The morning came and my fears worsened as a plethora of doctors entered my room and each and every one of them looked as worried as the doctor the night before. What was dawning on me was that this was something quite serious. More bad news was given to the effect that if it was a Cancer, as they now suspected, then what was showing on the chest X-ray was not its primary source and was likely to have started somewhere lower in my body, eventually reaching my lungs. The CT scan results confirmed that the Cancer was also in my para-aortic lymph nodes located by my abdomen, and gave a much clearer picture of my lungs with depth and clarity to confirm its spread. A further Ultrasound scan on my testicles also confirmed that the most likely cause and primary location of the Cancer was in my right testicle. Something I had suspected some 18 months back! I was transferred to Charing Cross Hospital by wheelchair in an ambulance as an emergency case (no blue lights, though!). All this time, I still looked as healthy on the outside as I had before, a young man with a nasty cough and absolutely no other symptoms whatsoever from any single part of my body. I called my GP's surgery to inform them that I wouldn't be turning up at 5pm! Once at Charing Cross, I was wheeled in my chair to the 6th floor. There was an immediate difference in newness between West Mid and Charing Cross but comfort was coming from the fact that I was now going to be treated in a Hospital that had years of experience and expertise - over and above any of the other hospitals in the area. I waited a while to be seen by my consultant and, to put a 40 minute conversation very briefly, I was told that my prognosis was good, a 4 out of 5 chance of a complete and full recovery from Testicular Cancer. Some 4 months of Chemotherapy in and out of hospital and getting ill to get better lay in wait for me, but it's my only choice.

I had to ask a final, but obvious, hypothetical question...

"As I feel fine and could walk out of here now, what if I didn't want any treatment whatsoever?"

"You'd have less than a year to live"

So that really kicks the facts home I guess, Chemotherapy treatment or die, a simple one to work out!!! So, whatever it takes, however long, and whatever side effects I have to put up with, they pale into insignificance compared to the alternative. A room was allocated to me and I decided to stay in hospital to try and keep the room as my Chemotherapy treatment would start the following week. Although the consultant was happy for me to retire home until the following week, I felt more comfortable being in this environment, considering the news I was still trying to take in and believe.

11th December 2004 - Saturday

I had to return home to refill my Mum's Parkinson's medication schedule to cover her whilst I was in hospital. Leaving the hospital was all cleared with the powers that be, so I had a shower and a good freshen up and left for Hounslow in order to be back to Hospital for dinner at 6pm. Being out of Hospital after the news I had been given was a weird experience, almost like I shouldn't be out of my comfort zone in case anything was to happen to me, so I felt a little anxious to say the least. Although sorting my Mum out was a priority, I now also had my own welfare to be concerned about. Upon returning to hospital at 6pm I discovered that dinner was served at 5pm so my best friend Ken Clark who, so far, has been through this whole daunting experience with me, popped over to Pizza Express which I can see from my window, to pick up some dinner.

12th December 2004 - Sunday

The day passed with relative calmness although anticipation was building for my Sperm Banking, EDTA and MRI tests over the next couple of days.

13th December 2004 - Monday

A visit to Hammersmith Hospital over lunchtime for some Banking (I'm not going into detail here!) and discussions about how, when and who I consent to using it in the future. All consents are far from set in stone and can be changed as and when needed, but if something happened to me and I hadn't changed them, the laws surrounding consents can be very strict and would stick to the last consent I had given.

14th December 2004 - Tuesday

Down to the 1st floor for my EDTA test at 11am which requires me to be back at 2 hour intervals, 1pm and 3pm which gave me a brief chance to get over to Hammersmith between 1pm and 3pm for another session of Banking! Increasing my future chances of having a family is a nice insurance policy to add to but on the scale of things my recovery comes first. Back to the ward for my dinner at 5pm then a short wait for my first round of Chemotherapy, this was all explained to me in full before treatment began. Due to the lateness of its start, and the schedule of drugs they feed me intravenously, it made for a disturbing night with visits to the loo dragging a machine along with me and getting out of bed to be weighed in the early hours.

15th December 2004 - Wednesday

Chemotherapy didn't finish on time this morning today, so I had to break away from it to rush down for my MRI scan. I was fast asleep and luckily Ken arrived early to wake me and get me down to the 1st floor for the scan, something the nurses should have done!! The MRI was pretty straight forward, a small injection in my arm and into the scanner I slid. I wasn't afraid; it was a little noisy and I stayed as still as possible so the resulting image would be as clear as it could be. Back to the ward for the final blast of Chemotherapy and then I was free for the day. After one night of Chemotherapy I didn't feel much different. The night came quickly and I was soon connected up to the Chemotherapy drip again. They were very busy on my ward and when it came to changing my dosage bag I was left for quite some time with no through flow, this caused quite a bit of discomfort in my hand and a burning sensation. Another disturbing night with dosage changes, wee'ing and weighing! I did sleep better though, probably fatigued from the night before.

16th December 2004 - Thursday

Morning came and the Chemotherapy treatment again finished a little late. Today I was to be free of any lines or taps in my hand, just another blood test, that's all. Again I was feeling fine and ate everything that was put in front of me. I got a look at my X-ray and MRI scans today. Thankfully the MRI was clear - still to be officially confirmed by my consultant - and the X-ray was an obvious indication that this disease was inside me and meaning business. The day went swiftly, although I tired towards the end, I still found the energy to set up Milton's (my ward buddy) new iMac G5, which gave him great pleasure. He's now even busier with his crusade to offer a positive approach to dealing with serious diseases like ours.

17th December 2004 - Friday

Slept well, albeit a little sweaty during the night. Today a brief chat about my test results with my consultant confirmed that the MRI test was clear, the blood test clear, the kidney test (EDTA) was good and all in order. My X-ray was alarming but the CT showed I still have plenty of lung space to breathe with comfortably and fight this disease. Discharge day today, home for the weekend. A bit of a wait around for my medication to take home and I was off the ward and home in no time, a surreal experience since going in to A&E over a week ago!

18th December 2004 - Saturday

Slept well in my own bed last night, felt sweaty again during the night. Ate breakfast, ate lunch, went to see my Mum briefly then back home for dinner. I was asked by the doctors to monitor my temperature, matched with how I am feeling whilst at home. I didn't feel any different and my temperature was reasonable at all times (normal should be 36.5 C).

19th December 2004 - Sunday

Slept a bit more awkwardly, woke up wide eyed, sweating, with a slight pain in my left lung during the night and couldn't sleep again for a bit for thinking about all that has gone on so far. Bounced myself off the walls in the dark and found the loo, back to bed and I was asleep in no time. This morning I'm OK, had a bit of a lie in (what's new!). The Sun is shining brightly out there today. Relaxed all day at home, away from all the Xmas bustle outside that I keep hearing about from my friends that have phoned or visited. Back to hospital tomorrow. I'll be in for a while for my first phase of full blown BEP Chemotherapy. Getting a bit nervous.

20th December 2004 - Monday

Slept really well although doing the usual sweating thing. It's hospital day today, not so daunting as I think the reason I'm going in today is just for NHS bed logistics, either that or 2 days away is all they are going to give me, considering the condition inside my body. I almost don't want to go back to hospital, but know that I have no choice. Back at hospital now, a new Ward (6 West, F7). Nervous at first, but now settled, oh and of course they had to give me a blood test didn't they!! Big day tomorrow, as far as I know. See you then.

21st December 2004 - Tuesday

Slept well although didn't go to bed until 3am as I was chilling out with Milton. This new ward I'm on doesn't even bring you breakfast, cheek! My 2 registrars (Dr's) came round to see me (they are under supervision by my consultant, the only one from whom I will take medical orders/instructions). They seemed pretty upbeat and happy to see me, and my initial blood results from yesterday all looked good. It seems my body is as ready as it's going to be for this Chemotherapy and they are preparing to give it to me this afternoon. They reiterated that it would be a much bigger dosage than I was given last week. Let's hope I can tough the effects out, I'm going to have a bloody good go. Breakfast done and they are taking my blood again, although the experience is becoming easier as my new Dr's assistant/phlebotomist (blood sucker), Sylvia, is a master at taking blood and does a sterling job with me - 'the big baby' when it comes to needles. Another blood test this afternoon, what are these people after? My life and soul? I must admit I might be getting used to these now, either that or my mind and body has given up worrying about it so much. Chemotherapy time is fast approaching and the nasty experience of having a Cannula fitted to my arm has to be overcome first. Well it took them 3 goes to get the big Cannula needle in my arm; I hated this experience. Now I'm connected up and have a full 15 hours of intravenous drip Chemotherapy ahead, this will take me well into the morning tomorrow. Had a few visitors tonight, been sitting in the TV room relaxing and chatting. Chemotherapy still going strong and making me wee a lot!! Off to bed soon, catch up with you all again tomorrow.

22nd December 2004 - Wednesday

Slept quite well considering 15 hours of Concorde fuel has been pumped in to me (thanks Rog). I'm awake and feeling fine, just waiting to be disconnected so I can go for yet another pee and get myself some breakfast. Breaking news... I'm potentially moving wards today - I pulled some strings ;) - back to South ward where I started all this. I think they miss me!! I'm 33 years and 4 months old today, exactly. Yup, it makes you think about your age too! Getting some great inspirational emails from you all, even from people I have never met, from the furthest corners of the earth, saying it's making them go get themselves checked out. What a surprise that my site is not only being used by those of you that wish to keep up to date with me, but also by people who may benefit from my experience. Moved wards now, back to the original place where everyone knows me. Although I might be annoying at times, I think they like me! Back on the Chemotherapy now, another 9 hours dragging an IV machine about, but feeling fine. Hearing more and more of people's Cancer stories is quite frightening, especially people who have a similar Cancer to myself. My only consolation is that EVERYBODY IS DIFFERENT and I have to keep saying that to myself to stay positive. Good test results are a good indication too, I'm chasing after some blood test results at the moment, tell you all about it later... The Consultant, with both his Registrars, found me loitering on the ward. They asked if I could follow them in to a quiet room (you know the feeling I was getting now, one of anticipation and worry). Sat me down and asked all about this website, which they'll be looking at soon. Also, mentioning the results of my HCG blood test almost in passing, a test they will be taking regularly throughout my treatment cycles. The HCG markers in my blood are an indication of my Cancer and its current level. When I first came in to the Charing Cross Hospital before any treatment, the reading was 3,900. It has now lowered itself to 3,300 which suggests an improvement and my body's positive reaction to the treatment so far. Although the Consultant would have been equally happy if these figures had mildly risen, although at a slow rate. No parties yet, however much it is good news; these levels can go up as well as down. Originally I was told that 80 to 90 percent of the Cancer in my body should be an easy conquest, it's the last 10 percent that is the hard part to eradicate. So, all in all, so far we are heading in the right direction. Ate double dinner tonight, one hospital meal and then a takeaway Pizza from Pizza Express across the road, appetite not gone yet, in-fact it's a little more furious at times. Feeling a little more fatigued tonight, especially in the legs, possibly an indication of some Chemotherapy side effects. Still on the Chemotherapy now, should be finished by midnight and then I can get an unrestricted night's sleep.

23rd December 2004 - Thursday

Awoke early today at 6am to have my blood pressure checked etc. Usual routine but I awoke with a startle and nearly karate chopped (like Inspector Clueso) the nurse. I warned her to be a little kinder when waking me or her life might be on the line! Got back to sleep for a bit, despite a lot of activity on my ward. Breakfast out the way now, I usually have 2 Weetabix with warm milk. Got my Christmas dinner menu today, hospital Christmas dinner whoopee! The doctors and nurses really pity you when then hear that you are in for the Xmas period, I'm so not bothered they cannot believe it. Honestly, when faced with something like this, nothing else should matter but your recovery (I was never really an Xmas person anyway, I'll save all my Xmas joy for the day when and if I'm given the chance to have my own family). Third day of Chemotherapy today, another 9 hours connected to a drip machine. That should be around 2pm I guess so making the most of my unattached freedom is a must. Ahh, I saw the blood test trolley come round just now and yes they wanted some... I must be getting used to these now as it went OK although I still didn't look! The Registrars came through to have a quick word, nothing major just a friendly 'how you feeling' and I think they can see I'm doing just fine so far. Lunch out the way, a quick bath, shave and dressed to impress. I'm on top form for another 9 hour round with the Chemotherapy IV machine. Today marks 2 weeks since I was first diagnosed with Cancer. How about that? I've just witnessed the inception of Milton's Xmas single which should be available on his site for download tomorrow or Xmas day. Please support him and go get yourself a copy from his site. He's a fine musician indeed. Ken went out for Kebab Kid for us all tonight (another 4th meal, appetite is still good). I've really fancied one since being here and being not so far away from my favourite kebab shop (very unlike your traditional drunken kebab shop, this has been voted one of the best in London by Time Out). Regular takeaway food is not advisable while on Chemotherapy but I would say KK passes the tests! It certainly went down fast enough with a bag of chips to follow. A friend is here taking pictures of Milton and I, for our web-sites (providing his Kebab with chillies stops repeating on him!); they'll be up on the website soon. Only a few hours of Chemotherapy to go tonight; running a bit behind and should be finished tonight by 1am. I'm sure I'll be suitably tired by then. Signing off for now... until tomorrow...

24th December 2004 - Friday (Xmas Eve)

Chemotherapy day 4 today, should start around 2pm'ish! Didn't sleep brilliantly last night, not for want of it though! Bleeps and beeps from other patients' machines kept me in a light sleep and also a few visits to the loo. I keep mentioning my visits to the loo because it's actually a good sign that my kidneys are flushing correctly and quickly, especially with all this Chemotherapy I am having, it's all fluid given by IV and if I retained it all I'd probably drown!! Breakfast all done, I had the usual. Just going to relax a bit and reply to a few more of the many supportive emails I've received from you all. One of the nurses was humming "Here comes the Sun" by George Harrison whilst making my bed, a song close to my heart. It was like a little message to me as I've always come across it at emotional or special times in my life. I'm guessing she heard it in Milton's room earlier and now I cannot get it out of my head (I'm not really trying to either!). I hope you enjoy the new images section (thanks Ken), I'll be adding to them frequently now, mainly throughout each phase of my Intense Chemotherapy. IF there's a change in the way I look over time, which there may well be, then I don't want you to become alarmed - it's all part of the healing process, I'm suitably informed (or at least the side effects of Chemotherapy). Mind you, for those of you that don't know me, I'm not that good looking anyway ;-) !!! Had a visit from my consultant and one of his registrars again. They're still happy with my progress and will be taking another blood test today! I also have to have an injection whilst at home called a G-CSF which should help me stay healthy over and above any side effects of Chemotherapy. Can you see me injecting myself, I don't think so! Any volunteers, a nice nurse perhaps? I've just completed a massive update in the Links section of my site for those of you that are interested to read more detail about some of the tests and medical names I have mentioned. Blood test done, I'm definitely getting used to these or I have found the best nurse there is at doing them; I want to give her a hug. Finally back on the Chemotherapy, it's been manic here this afternoon with patients being discharged and new patients arriving. They just haven't had time to get to me, so I had to go and beg them at the ward reception for my treatment to start. 4pm start time means I will not be free of the IV machine until just before 2am tonight, another late night! Some of the patients around me are in quite a bad way, a permanent reminder that Cancer is a potential killer and not to be ignored in our lives. A vicar has visited the patient opposite me, it really opens your eyes when it comes to that. Chemotherapy schedule gone a bit haywire tonight, I'm still waiting for my final 4 hour bag at 22.45 which means I will not finish until 3am now. Another disturbed night, I'll not be rested well for Santa. Couldn't resist a Pizza Express blow-out tonight so Ken went down to get the takeaway order for us all. Definitely tiring for bed now and not looking forward to my noisy and delirious neighbour (a new addition to the ward), been seriously thinking about sleeping in the TV room under the Xmas tree! I hope that tonight Santa brings you all you wished for.

Merry Christmas to all of you. I don't expect many of you will be checking in on me today with all your presents and festivities. Not so much for me, as I'm doing just fine and Xmas doesn't bother me, but please spare a thought for those who are less fortunate than yourselves today. After all, Xmas is a time of giving, so a little kind thought wouldn't do you any harm! As I haven't had much time to organise Xmas cards for you all, Ken has kindly decorated my site for your Xmas enjoyment! What a cheapskate I am, one Xmas card for you all!!! Well I did manage a couple of hours on the sofa in the TV room whilst my Chemotherapy drip finished which, as I suspected, finished around 3am, the bleeper alarm woke me up. This also gave ample time for some medication to take effect on the delirious and noisy man that had moved next to me in my ward. I had to ask the nurses to do something; although I did feel sorry for him, I needed my sleep time too!! They've moved my noisy neighbour just now and the chap on the other side is not doing so well, in-fact he's just been sick so I'm putting off my breakfast until I get up to go get it, I'm having a cup of tea for now. This morning I feel rested but I hope to get an early night again tonight. Waiting for my friend Ken to arrive before I open any of my presents, I expect something Xmas related will be going on in the ward today. Humbug! Noisy neighbour is back. Chemotherapy is due to start again soon, it looks like it'll be another late one tonight. Milton has launched his alternative Christmas Single today "That's Christmas". Produced in 2.5 days from his hospital room whilst undergoing Chemotherapy, the (alternative) Christmas Single is all about what's crap about Christmas but great about people! At last Chemotherapy has restarted at 15.50, which means approximately another 9 hours on before I'm finished tonight. 26th December 2004 - Sunday (Boxing Day)

So much for the sleep, although what I did manage to get was reasonable, managed the 2am bedtime and went off to sleep almost immediately, only to be woken be the moans and screams of the man next to me at around 6.45am. I pressed my nurse call button to get him some attention but I think that there is nothing more they can do for him, he is delirious, confused, rambling and most of all sounds scared that he will be left alone to die. It may sound harsh but I cannot do anything for him apart from call the nurse, I have detached myself from the situation now and have taken up residence in the TV room to spend the rest of my early morning in the peace and quiet of my own company (and a little TV!). It hurts quite a bit when I see others suffering around me, mainly terminal in my section of the ward. I must stay positive about my own condition, so some isolation in this room by myself was my only option. It's starting to get busy now out in the corridors, it'll be breakfast time soon. The usual breakfast out the way and it turns out I did need some more sleep as I dozed off on the sofa in the TV room. Woke up about an hour later. Just had a Blood Test, definitely easier to cope with but I'm still not watching! The result of which will determine both my requirement for the HCG injections and my current HCG counts. Lunch approaching and I'm getting hungry, methinks it'll be quite a wait today before they give me their decision on my discharge and or injection schedule. Discharged at 15.45 without the need for an immune boost (HCG) as my blood test results have shown that I tolerated the first phase of Chemotherapy very well. My body is still able to fight off infections by itself, although possibly mildly dampened, so I have to be careful of my exposure to germs etc. So this ends phase 1 of my particular Chemotherapy regime. I'm not sure if the strength of the Chemotherapy in the following 3 major phases will be increased in strength (this entry is going to serve as a reminder to me to get this answer as soon as possible). I will need more power and strength to overcome greater doses. At the beginning of each major phase, I will be X-rayed to have some visual results as to the increase, or more positively, and what I will be hoping for the most... the demise of the Cancer that is showing on my lungs. Home for dinner and an undisturbed night's sleep.

27th December 2004 - Monday

Good morning! I managed 8 hours of good sleep, I don't want to overdo the lie in as I still need to stick to a regular eating regime, so I'm about to go have my Weetabix but this time at home! It occurred to me last night that my consultant and doctors may be occasionally looking in on this diary, so not only is it acting as a means of communication for me and a means of awareness for others but also as a kind of Bio Monitor for my doctors, as they can just check in to see how I'm feeling!! Well this morning I feel fine, apart from a little bit of a throat (probably from snoring after my comfortable sleep) and a snotty nose, can't explain that! I'll stick to my given tablet regime 1 Allopurinol 100mg in the morning along with 2 Domperidome 10mg 3 times a day. I also have a mouth wash to keep any nasty germs at bay, although my immune system result was good and I should be able fight off infection myself, I'll try not give it too much work to do. No feeling of sickness at all. Wouldn't it be a good idea for temporarily discharged patients to wear a bio monitor to analyse statistics, the results of which would be available securely online to the doctors, and if anything went wildly wrong alarms would be raised etc. They put curfew monitors on prisoners these days, why not use similar technology? I'll shut up before I get carried away. Been keeping myself busy all day; healthy eating and drinking very well indeed! Tiring now, ready for another good night's sleep!

18th March 2005 - Friday

CT Scan day today. I must eat something this morning to keep me going. As of 12pm I am not allowed to eat anything until after my scan at 4pm. During these 4 hours I have to drink a colouring agent called Gastrografin at 12pm, again at 2pm, then again when I arrive for the scan. I'm allowed to mix it with 300ml of orange squash, however it's not meant to taste that nice, I'll let you know! The drink wasn't too bad and I hope I suitably coloured my insides for a good clear scan. Traffic was awful getting to the hospital, got there just after 4pm. Waited in the CT reception until about 5pm, when they started to prepare me for the scan. I think I tired my brain out with stress and almost fell asleep waiting. At this stage I am frightened about what they are going to find inside my body. I'm being looked at through a magnifying glass and the result will determine what, if any, other operations are to be performed on my body. Apprehensive, nervous and worried; I couldn't think straight at all. Another small cup of colouring fluid, clear in colour with not much taste, then I was cannulated ready to be connected or 'interfaced' with the CT scanner. I have some really good pictures of me inside the CT scanner which will be up in the gallery soon. Fortunately the CT operator owns the same digital camera as me and she didn't need any instruction on how to use mine! The scan took just under 10 minutes to complete, there is a recorded voice that asks to 'hold your breath' and 'breathe normally' during the scan. Halfway through, the CT scanner begins pumping yet another colouring fluid in to my veins, warning just beforehand that 'the injection is starting'. My only thoughts were to stay as still as possible during the scan, in the hope that the stiller I could be, the clearer the picture they would have of my insides. It was all over quite quickly, my cannulation was removed almost painlessly and yet another hurdle in my journey has been overcome. The next is this Tuesday, the day I find out the results of my scan and how much cancer they need to cut out from inside my body. Milton was in at the hospital for another blood test and we managed to catch up with each other. We all went across the road for a Pizza Express. Traffic was quite bad being yet another Friday rush hour, so spending a little time catching up with Milton was much better than sitting in stationary traffic on the way home. Once home, I felt quite tired again and managed about an hour's sleep on the sofa. These tired spells aren't normal for me at all, however I still feel ok before and after sleeping, it's just pure tiredness. Spent a little time on front of the TV and managed a big bowl of fruit and ice cream as a late snack. Didn't get to bed too early as I just didn't feel tired enough after my nap earlier; once I did get to bed I soon drifted off to sleep.

If my health and blood results are on the up, then the operation will be arranged for a date quite soon after Tuesday's meeting. Dropped my friend off at Heathrow Airport for a flight that I would have most certainly been on with him, if I hadn't been ill. A little heart wrenching, hopefully I will be on that flight one day. Went over and spent some time with my Mum. The nice weather today helped her good spirits, which was added to with a fair amount of mobility. Got home in good time for a big dinner before my first evening outing for what seems like an absolute age. Another friend is having a birthday party upstairs at my favourite bar in Richmond and I really wanted to show my face. Got there at 22.00, lots of people were pleased to see me after such a long time. It was nice to be out although I did take a back seat from the partying and drank only cranberry juice all night. It was quite tiring being out and, once home, I was glad to get to bed.

21st March 2005 - Monday

Woke up at 09:00 today, I must stay awake in order to get to bed early tonight in preparation for my Tuesday meeting. Breakfast out of the way, I am going to do a few chores around the house and then set off to take my Mum to an important meeting for her at the local hospital. Hospital, hospital, hospital, my life at the moment seems to revolve around hospital and will do for sometime to come! My Mum's test results have also been playing on my mind for quite some time. After a little potter around at some shops with my Mum, I took her home and then made for home myself. Not much time left before I find out now. I wonder if I will be able to get to sleep tonight!? Two helpings of dinner and I was soon tired out enough to get to bed. Eventually I got to sleep at around 01:00.

22nd March 2005 - Tuesday

Woke up at about 07:00 this morning. Anxiety about today's meeting no doubt! I felt rested enough so was up and about by 09.00. Had a big breakfast cooked for me this morning. I'm 33 and 7 months old today! Ken arrived back after being away for a few weeks and we all set off for the hospital. Arrived in good time for my meeting and was soon seen to by my Doctor and Registrar. They had examined my CT scan results and I prepared myself nervously for the results. I will try to explain what was said to me as best I can, I've just got home and am typing this whilst the news sinks in and I can understand and accept it all myself. During the course of my treatment for Testicular Cancer, the chemotherapy has had an outstanding effect on what, by all accounts, was quite a serious and large spread of cancer. My blood results (tumour markers) have shown an outstanding response to the treatment, with the cancer showing no signs of recovery for over a month now. However, I still have tumours showing in my lungs and abdomen. A few very small tumour scars, along with two sizeable tumours in my lungs and one tumour almost 2cm in size on my abdomen. Without an operation, there is no way of telling if any of these tumours, or any tiny part of them, are still alive. All the signs so far show that they have died and may just be shrinking slowly over time as my body repairs itself. So without an operation the gamble is to wait and find out if they are still alive. This could take some time and could mean that, if they are alive, they would mutate into another form of cancer which could put me at further risk in the future. With an operation, the tumour would be entirely removed and unable to mutate or grow even if it was still alive. Bearing in mind that these tumours in my body could just be dead tissue which would fade over time. The conclusion is that the obvious and most important operation is to remove the testicle that started all of this off; that is a dead cert and relatively easy operation, which will be scheduled soon. The outstanding decision is the slightly more serious operation on my abdomen to remove the 2cm tumour, a decision that will be taken tomorrow by my specialist Doctor along with another specialist Doctor, a Surgeon and a Radiologist. As I understand it, my Doctor likes to err on the side of caution and may decide to wait and see if the abdomen tumour shrinks any more before any surgery is required. However, the others in the team will most likely favour the operation being carried out as soon as possible, along with the testicle removal. At this stage I think there is a 60-70% that the abdomen operation will be carried out along with the testicle removal. Tomorrow, I will know for sure after the experts have had their patient meeting at the hospital. Operations on lungs are a more serious issue and the general practice is to let them be and keep a close eye on anything that remains. My lungs have shown a massive improvement and, like the abdomen, the hope is that the tumours showing are dead tissue and will recover over time. The likelihood is that I will not be having an operation on my lungs; instead they will be monitored over time with more X-rays and CT scans. If, after some time, they have not improved then my lungs will have to be operated on. This may change! I went to the hospital today for some news and, on a scale of 1 to 10, 1 being brilliant news, I probably came away with a 6. I will hear more tomorrow, but the general picture has been explained to me and my interpretation of it is above. I had a blood test after the meeting and await the results of this over the next few days. This and future blood tests are mainly to monitor my general health and recovery from chemotherapy, along with another tumour test to monitor the status of my cancer (currently non active or dead). How do I feel? Physically... quite good. Mentally... in shock (a little), nervous, externally; quiet and thinking. I have a choice and can make decisions about my operations. Given the dilemma, I think that it is best to follow the advice of the experts in charge. I really feel that I cannot make any decisions in isolation regarding my treatment as they are based on odds and are quite simply a gamble! Managed to get over to my Mum's in the evening to explain face to face what I have learned so far. Home for dinner and I was fast asleep by 01:00. Tomorrow's another day!

23rd March 2005 - Wednesday

Woke up early today, just one thing on my mind. My first thoughts as I opened my eyes were about my operations and how much of this cancer is actually dead inside my body. I just have no way of knowing. However, all the signs so far (including medical) suggest that it is. I'm beginning to get very scared, I could really cry at times and am probably crying inside already. My demeanour has changed somewhat, from being jovial to quiet contemplation. I'm certainly not in a chatty mood at the moment. The registrar at the hospital had a bit of trouble getting hold of me today, a mix up with my actual contact telephone number. Before finally getting hold of me, the hospital phoned my Mum and then a friend of mine, which worried them somewhat. The hospital isn't allowed to discuss anything with anyone but the patient, so calling someone else and saying "it's the hospital, we need to speak to Tim" can easily be taken the wrong way. My bloods have recovered almost to normal, which is a very good sign that my body is getting better quickly from the chemotherapy. I'm a healthy young man as the doctors put it (apart from the obvious). The news is that my surgeon has decided to also operate on the abdomen and wishes to discuss this with me before he goes away for a week tomorrow. The hospital simply wanted me to get in for a meeting with him this afternoon to discuss my imminent surgery (within the next 2 weeks). Hence the urgency with which they were trying to get hold of me. Once I arrived at the hospital, I was soon seen by not only my surgeon but also my doctor, the registrar and the Macmillan nurse. Everyone at hand for me and my questions. The surgeon came across very confident about the operation and explained to me in detail how it would be carried out. Dr Tim Christmas' reputation precedes him (he's my surgeon). These particular operations are certainly not unfamiliar to him. I have been told that people are sent to him from all over the country and sometimes all over the world. It appears that Charing Cross Hospital and its patients are very lucky to benefit from his expertise. A somewhat comforting thought. I am still nervous about my pending operation; who wouldn't be? I will end up with a sizeable scar in my groin and an even more sizeable scar down the centre of my stomach. There will be before and after pictures on here for all to see (no rude bits, I'm afraid)! The date of my operation is now posted in the schedules area of this website. They have not been confirmed to me in writing as yet, so may be subject to change. In the mean time, I just have to keep myself busy. My blood health and results have returned to normal now, this was confirmed to me not only on the phone earlier but also in the meeting. I am lucky to have my health and be able to recover quite quickly by normal standards. Not so much in terms of fitness but general health I am in pretty damn good shape apart from the obvious! On the way home I was still trying to soak up all that I had been told, hopefully I will be able to expand on it more once I have digested all the information myself. Very bad traffic on the way home; went to Sainsbury's to top up on some food shopping. After I had my dinner, I popped over to spend the latter part of the evening with my Mum to expand further on my call to her earlier in the day. As you can imagine, each and every time I get more news I am immediately passing it on to my Mum & Dad by phone. Once home, I finally got to sleep at about 2am.

24th March 2005 - Thursday

Woke up at 11am. A good deep sleep. Soon after I had eaten breakfast the registrar called to confirm the last part of my blood results. My HCG tumour marker still remains at below 2 or untraceable. So good news that potentially none of this cancer showing inside me is alive and what isn't going to be removed will hopefully disappear over time. A question that I have been meaning to ask for some time about my cancer was answered today. As I have shown particularly good recovery so far, does this mean that my cancer will also recover too? After all, the cancer was created in my body, I did wonder if it would recover as well as my body has. The answer is no. Generally in healthier people the battle is hard but the clear up rate is much better than with people who have poor health when they are admitted with cancer. Another lesson, look after yourselves and make sure you are in good health! Keeping myself busy today, trying not to think about things too much. I have a bit of time away from hospital now before any further tests are carried out. Got to bed quite late again. I must get back in to a normal sleeping pattern.

Easter weekend! Woke up feeling good. Nice to see the sun shining. Not much to report. I'm feeling in good health and eating well. Apart from the obvious stress of my pending operations I'm going to continue to lead as normal a life as possible and feasible under the current circumstances. I'll not bore you with mundane daily reports of my movements! If anything happens, I'll be back here to tell. Otherwise see you all again on the 5th April 2005.


APRIL 2005


5th April 2005 - Tuesday

Arrived at the hospital just before the blood testing room closed at Clinic 8 for the morning. Ordered up my own blood tests as no reference to what was needed could be found. Full Blood Count, U&E's inc. Magnesium, Tumour Markers (HCG, LDH, AFP). How do I remember all that? After some 4 months of treatment and blood tests, you can pretty much become an expert in knowing what's required at times! I will not have any results from the blood tests until tomorrow at the earliest. I've been feeling pretty healthy over the past week or so. Still catching my breath a little if I do too much; this is probably more to do with the fact that I'm out of condition than in poor health. The strangest feeling is knowing there is still cancer inside my body (hopefully dead) and thinking ahead to my operation next week, asking myself "why do I need it, I feel OK". Obviously I know the real answers but it's a weird feeling knowing that soon I will be undergoing major surgery for what appears from the outside to be for no reason at all. I get the odd twinge in my lungs, nothing painful or awkward and perhaps I only perceive it mentally. It certainly serves as a sobering reminder of what may be in store for me with regard to my lungs and any operations that may be required of them in the future. I have accepted the fact that next week I will be on an operating table. I will be undergoing a major procedure called an RPLND as well as an Orchiectomy, all in the same sitting. Quite an ordeal for my body: it will be approximately a 1 hour operation, 3-5 days recovery in hospital and a further 2-3 weeks at home, doing not much at all. I have been told that, in my case, the RPLND will be quite a quick and easy procedure for the surgeon to complete due to the small size of the tumour he is removing (approx. 2cm in diameter). However, it still means that I will have a substantial scar down the centre of my stomach, some 6-10 inches in length. Believe me, I'm scared of what lies ahead for me. I'm trying to stay positive and keep my mind off my forthcoming agenda but time is getting thin now, the operation and its undeniable results are looming over me. I haven't been getting to sleep very quickly or easily at night. My mind races with apprehensive thoughts, although when I do finally sleep, I sleep well and deeply (I've never had a problem there!) My phone has been quiet, which I thank you all for, the more I talk about "how I am" and "how I feel" worries me more and more each day. The more I keep my mind off things and just get on with it, the better I'll be. Your messages of support are still greatly appreciated; it's comforting to see all the support I have out there. I'll be back to report on my blood results as soon as I receive them. I'll probably repeat myself a bit as everything's going over and over in my mind, more and more with each passing day.

7th April 2005 - Thursday

Managed to get up early after another late night last night. Had to get myself to the garage early to sort some things out for my car. The tiredness caught up with me this afternoon while visiting my Mum and I had a quick snooze. The stress of this forthcoming operation is beginning to catch up with me; I almost got myself quite upset today whilst talking to my Mum, a few tears showing. The additional operation (RPLND) I am to have is not exactly the ideal scenario I had hoped for, but I have to keep positive, strong and, most of all, keep moving forward. Something I don't always find it easy to focus on. Understandable, given the circumstances, I guess. My blood results came through by phone late this afternoon. I'm still not 100% (probably about 60% - 70%) in terms of blood health, but it's good enough to go for my operation next week. I will have another blood test on the day before my operation and, if required, may need another blood transfusion to top things up a little. This explains my unfit feelings recently, it's not that apparent but I have noticed a reduction in performance from my body, at least from what I am used to being able to do (pre diagnosis). My HCG tumour marker still remains below 2 or untraceable, which is positive news. I have added the latest test date to the HCG results section of the site.

It's about time I updated my symptoms... Tiredness (infrequently) Tinnitus (chemo side effect, sometimes annoying) Hair loss (a light fluff appearing) Itchy face (due to hair growth returning) Bruised fingers , small sores appearing (long gone) Oral Thrush (long gone) Tiger stripes on skin (still apparent, a chemo side effect) Bowel Movements (very occasional pain, pretty much back to normal) Night Sweats (gone) Cough (very occasional, slight, different to before) Temperature and chills (none) Nausea (none).

Spoke to my Dad today - as I do most days - to update him with the latest news. He is coming on the 13th when I'm admitted to hospital, and will be with me before I go in to theatre for my surgery the following day. He asked if there was anything I needed him to bring up; I just said "some courage". Until the 12th of April then... which is when I have a quick and very easy visit to the hospital for an ultrasound. The date that is playing on my mind the most is Thursday the 14th April, the day of my operation. I somehow feel that I will never be the same again after that day.

9th April 2005 - Sunday

Today marks 4 months exactly since diagnosis. It has been an unpleasant 4 month lesson in human mortality. There is light at the end of the tunnel but this is far from over yet, I'm still nervous about my forthcoming operation and the results that will transpire. I have received some really supportive emails recently, for which I am eternally grateful. One of which read... "I know there is probably no telling you right now, but I have been there recently and you shouldn't worry. I bet you, like me will be checking out of hospital on the Monday morning following your op, feeling nothing but a little sore and you like me, now 3 months on, will feel exactly the same as you did before, and you will be cancer free." Very comforting to read, amongst many other emails, it's something I will most certainly keep in mind to help me through the coming days. I managed to spend the day with my Mum today. We went to Richmond Park for some fresh air, tea and cakes! I hope to be spending many more days out with my Mum in the future. Just one 'whole' day left for me now, before I start my hospital business.

12th April 2005 - Tuesday

I woke up quite early and suddenly this morning. I immediately felt my stomach wondering if there was a scar there or not. I cannot remember dreaming and slept very well, however I guess I must have 'lived' the operation in my sleep and awoke to find no scar! Just goes to show that this is really starting to play on my mind now. I cannot pinpoint exactly what is worrying me so much, probably the aftermath and results of this forthcoming operation. Also, it is my first ever major operation under general anaesthetic; I have some reservations about that too! I'm off to have an Ultrasound scan today at 11.45. I'm also going to try to find my Doctor for a quick word and to pop up and have a look at the ward I am scheduled to stay in. ...back home now. Had a good look at my Ultrasound scan while it was being carried out. The doctor was very helpful and showed me all that there was to see, pointing out on screen all the bad bits in my right testicle. I'll be rid of it on Thursday! Afterwards, Ken and I went for a bit of a search about in the hospital for my Doctor and also had a look at the 8th floor ward which I am to be recovering in. Managed to catch up with my Registrar and Doctor, both of whom again tried to allay my fears about my forthcoming operation. There just seems to be no telling me at the moment, but I guess every bit of reassurance I get will slowly chip away at the barriers and worries I have built up for myself. I introduced myself to the sister in charge of 8 North; she filled me in on some of the things I am to expect once I'm admitted tomorrow and also some of the tubes and machines that will be around me once I come out from surgery. It doesn't sound pleasant in the slightest. I will know more tomorrow once I'm at the hospital which is tentatively arranged for 14.30. My Tinnitus is much more apparent today, perhaps connected to the increasing stress I am feeling. I'm staying at my Mum's house tonight. I will not be able to see her again for quite some time after tomorrow's admission to hospital. Although, once I'm home there will, of course, be times when she can come to see me while I recover.

13th April 2005 - Wednesday

Woke up suddenly at 05:00 this morning but managed to get back off to sleep shortly after. Again, no dreams apparent, but there's certainly something going on up there in that brain of mine! Finally got up at around 09:30 this morning, got myself together and then helped my Mum get herself together. For me, saying goodbye to my Mum this morning felt like a final farewell. I know it's not, but I found it hard and was quite emotional. My Mum is full of hope and encouragement for me. I called the Hospital admissions department as instructed at 12:00, only to be told that instructions from the bed manager on my intended ward had not been relayed as yet and it would be best if they called me when they were ready. I hope this delay does not have a knock on or rushed effect with regard to my preparation for surgery which is supposed to be taking place tomorrow. I returned to my house where my Dad was waiting for me. All that I can do is await the call now so I'm trying to keep busy in the meantime. 15:10 - Got the call; now I'm doing my final bits, getting ready to set off for the hospital. There should be a picture of me in the Gallery shortly, one of an intact 'warranty valid' un-scarred torso. This was taken just before leaving for the hospital. Soon you will see the results of my operation in glorious colour and Dr Tim Christmas' handiwork. I arrived at the hospital just after the admissions department had closed, so I made my way up to the ward where I was to be admitted. There was a bit of confusion on the ward, as I was supposed to collect my notes from admissions upon arrival. I spent the rest of the evening worrying about tomorrow. I had to take some Bowel prep in order to clear me out before the operation. There were plenty of tears, mostly apprehension on my part. This was my first operation ever and a major one at that.

14th April 2005 - Thursday

Woke up early, the Bowel prep from the night before soon did the trick! I was told that the operation was scheduled for 10:30 and was visited by the Anaesthetist. Apparently pre operation and some time ago, normal procedure is to be admitted to a pre operative clinic. This was something that perhaps the schedule of my operation's urgency did not allow. A lot of things that fortunately I had taken the time to find out about a few days ago were sprung on me. More trauma for my ever depreciating mood to cope with! It was suggested that I take a tablet to calm my nerves. I'm not a big fan of suppressive tablets or medication, however, on this occasion, I went with the advice of everyone around me and was given a Temazepam. I'm not so sure it worked much but everyone around me said I was a little calmer. Once dressed in gown and surgical stockings, I was ready to go when called upon. I climbed on to my hospital bed, the bars locked in to place and off I was wheeled. As you can imagine, I was VERY nervous and very emotional. What felt like my final good-byes were said over the phone to my Mum and by the lift door to my Dad and Ken. Once on the 14th floor, it was just a short journey and I was in the Anaesthetic room. I was comforted and further prepared for surgery. The last words I remember saying through my tears as the anaesthetic worked its cold way up my arm and over my face were "please look after me..." Today's entry also written by Ken... Tim's Dad and I arrived early this morning in preparation for today's events. The Surgeons, Senior Registrar and the Anaesthetist came round early to explain a few things and get Tim's consent signature for the ops. It was a bit up in the air as to whether he would require a Central Line. This is where they feed a pipe directly into a main artery, allowing for easy drug and fluid intake. He was told that he would also be fitted with a catheter as well as being cannulated in his hand for other fluid and drug intake. The final two tubes to be fitted were a tube up his nose and into his stomach to drain off fluid and a tube inserted into his side to drain fluids around the operated area. He was given a tablet around 09:30 to take the edge off his nerves, basically a 'Chill Pill' and standard pre-op procedure. He was then taken up to Surgery on the 14th floor around 10:30, and we gave our best wishes to an emotional Tim as he was wheeled into the lift stretched out on his bed. We were then told to enquire in around two hours as to his condition; we weren't allowed up to the 14th floor. The Sister rang up for us at around 12:30 and we were told he was still in theatre and to check again in an hour. We weren't overly concerned as to the amount of time that had passed, as we didn't know exactly when the operations started so couldn't gauge anything. Sister rang again at 13:30 and was told he had been out of theatre for 45 minutes. Also that he was in the recovery area on the 14th floor and was expected back down to us in under an hour. After some pacing, plenty of clock watching and numerous phone calls to his Mum and brother to keep them updated, he arrived back into the ward and we followed. The curtain had been pulled round as the surgery nurses checked his dressings and handed him over to the ward's care. I peeped through the curtains to find him asleep, looking very pale, pipes and tubes seeming to come out of every part of him, and being fed oxygen through a mask. As if sensing someone other than the nurses was looking on, he opened his eyes. I gave him a questioning thumbs up, and he replied with a reassuring nod. He was back. He was then pounced upon by the observations nurse and was told he would have his vitals taken every half hour for the first two hours and then every hour for the following two hours. He managed a few groggy words to us just to say he was fine, but in pain. One of the tubes going in to him is a Morphine drip, which is in his control. He has a button that he can push to feed in Morphine as and when he needs it, but it will lock out if pushed too much in quick succession. So far the lock hasn't been enforced. I asked him on a scale of 1 to 10 how bad the pain was, he thought about it, then said 7, promptly followed by a squeeze of the button! He was complaining that his mouth was very dry, especially as he hadn't drunk any fluid since midnight. I went off to see if he was allowed to drink anything, but the Sister explained that he can't eat or drink for a few days depending on how it goes. What was allowed was to moisten his mouth and lips using ice cubes and sponge sticks dabbed in water; a willing female happily played Nurse. Now able to speak slightly better, Tim made a call to his Mum to reassure her that he was okay. We then waited for the Surgeon's Senior Registrar to appear to explain how the Ops went. He arrived around 16:30. After asking how Tim was feeling, he told us that the Ops went better than expected as they didn't need to insert the drain tube into his side, and the central line wasn't needed. He went on to say that the tumour from the abdomen and cancerous testicle had been removed without any problems, and to expect pathology results in two weeks. He said the scar on his stomach seems also to be smaller than expected, about 7 inches, so another piece of good news. He's now in and out of sleep and not really any less groggy than when we first saw him, but apparently this is to be expected for a few hours yet. I'm not sure in his current condition if he's going to be tortured by the smell of dinner about to be served to all the other patients, but based on the past, he's not missing much. That's about it I think. All in all it's gone really well, the tumour and cancerous testicle are gone, and he's got fewer tubes and shorter scars than expected. Hopefully that's the last piece of treatment he will need, and it's just recovery from here on in. I doubt Tim will be able to manage more than one day without using his Mac, so expect tomorrow's entry to come from the patient himself.

17th April 2005 - Sunday

Briefly got out of bed today. I sat in my chair for a bit this morning. Later on I managed 2 very slow laps of the ward accompanied by my IV machine. One of my cannulations tissued today. I felt the bandage on my arm swelling and called the nurse to stop the IV machine and check my arm. I am so used to cannulations and what they should feel like this was old hat to me. Although uncomfortable and mildly painful, I waited for the sister to come and re-cannulate me in another vein (as you know, an experience I do not relish). Once the sister arrived, I discussed the possible use of Emla cream with her, she told me they did not have access to any. Now I wish I had brought my own! She attempted and failed at a cannulation, not willing to take any feedback from me 'the patient' she continued to attempt to flush what I said had failed and my arm swelled further, only commenting to me that if it had failed then she should be the judge of it! I waited a over 1 hour more for a doctor to arrive and have another go. Although an uncomfortable experience, he was successful. I went back to sipping water today, as I felt the advice given yesterday was a bit hasty and I didn't want to suffer with the stomach cramps again. The rest of the day soon passed quickly, I watched a couple of DVDs in bed before getting my head down for the night.

18th April 2005 - Monday

Only today was I able and well enough to quickly post a progress notice here with a little help from Ken. Mr. Christmas came to visit with his team of Registrars and Doctors very early this morning. He removed the bandages from my scars that I was reluctant to look at, and commented in jest that he had a team of plastic surgeons working on me in surgery to make them as unnoticeable as possible. I must admit my first impressions of what must be some quite angry scars are pretty good indeed. As I had heard before, the Operation had gone very well. Of course, it was more comforting to hear it come from the person that actually performed it! Mr. Christmas also commented that the tumour from my abdomen was most likely to be dead now he had seen it and removed it, stating that obviously he didn't have microscopic eyes. Another piece of positive news. The jury will still be out on that one until I get conclusive pathology results back in a couple of weeks' time. I've had my catheter removed today, a very unpleasant experience. I now have to pee in a bottle until further notice, as my output has to be regularly monitored. I've had my operation wounds cleaned, sprayed and redressed today although the larger bandages are to be left off in favour of airing the wounds for them to heal better. Managed another 2 very slow laps of the ward aided only by my IV machine to walk with.

19th April 2005 - Tuesday

Today's entries bring you more up to date from the 15th April onwards. As my memory returns and I can type more info, I'll fill in more detail regarding today and the past few days. Not much sleep at all last night, what with wanting to go to the toilet, having to 'try' and go in a bottle, constant noise from surrounding patients and an awful back ache from being in bed too long. When I did finally get comfortable or at least exhausted enough to sleep, I was suddenly awoken by Mr. Christmas' Registrar who rattled off some information and promptly left my bedside. I was a bit put out, considering my condition and that I had just woken up, there was no chance to reasonably question or answer his comments. Then shortly after it was regimental 'out of bed', 'we want to make it' time. After hardly any sleep, some pain and a lot of discomfort, it really starts to grind at you. Managed 1 cup of water and 2 cups of warm milk so far today. Stomach seems more settled. I have been asked to eat some soup this afternoon. I've had a full wash today, brushed my teeth and had a shave. After which I managed 3 laps of the ward accompanied by my trusty IV machine. A friend brought in some soup for me to eat this afternoon. I have slowly worked my way through a whole tin so far. I've certainly been a bit more active and able to move about today. Soup again for dinner. My 3 units of blood are due to be transfused tonight. Once under way, it meant for a disturbed night's sleep. One unit transfused at a time, each over 3-4 hours with very regular observation and temperature checks on me. This meant waking up almost every hour of the night.

20th April 2005 - Wednesday

Up early today and out of bed. Thought I'd beat them to it, before I'm instructed to "get up and out of bed", plus I didn't get much sleep either, so I was awake anyway. Feeling better after my lengthy 3 unit blood transfusion which is just about finished now. I'm due a blood test today then, based on the results, perhaps 1 more unit of blood later on. Just had a cannulation removed from my hand, so only one left in my arm now. Also I'm free of my IV machine for the first time since last Thursday. Hopefully I will be a lot more active and lively today, although I must make sure I do not overdo it or over stretch myself (I don't want to tear open!). Going to try some Weetabix with warm milk for breakfast today! Managed to get breakfast down but sending food down in to my stomach still hurts a little. Just a few stomach cramps, I'm sure it'll all be back in place soon. Had a visit from the Registrars and the ward Sister this morning, much more informed and pleasant to talk to. They've started reading my website, too! I didn't have to say much! Finally I get to pee normally again, no more going in a bottle every time. Now I just have to make it to the loo when I need to go!! Previously, I had tried to make the effort to get to the loo, just from a mental point of view and for the exercise. Going in a bottle wasn't a pleasant experience but far more favourable than using a catheter! Blood test taken (Full Blood Count and U&E's), noticed it was marked 'urgent' so if things move quickly, I'll know my HB level soon enough and either be totally free or transfused another unit of blood later today. Had a bit of an anomaly occur down below, apparently something to do with the catheter I'd had. Anyway I called the Doctor to double check. Had a good informative visit from both the Registrars and, later on, my Surgeon. My HB is back to 11.3 now, so no need for the last unit of blood and the final cannulation in my arm was removed. All that is required of me now is that I go to the loo... (no.2)! I haven't been since last Thursday morning and it's pretty much a medical requirement after the type of major operation I've had. Thing is... hospitals, toilets and me don't quite go together very well. I cannot strain either!! I might have to opt for one of the 'techniques' my surgeon joked about earlier. So all being well I should be going home tomorrow. I can't wait. Late this evening I was due another intravenous antibiotic injection, the final one of a 3 day regime (twice a day). As my cannula had been removed I was reluctant to have this and asked if it could be given in tablet form. Unfortunately, this was not possible so I very nicely refused to take it. A bit of an oversight and hasty removal of the final cannula on the Doctors' part earlier. I just couldn't see the point of going through the discomfort of what I particularly dislike for just one small injection of something that I could probably live without. Especially going on an earlier experience in this ward. (Still, I respect I'm no medical expert so I hope it isn't detrimental to my treatment).

21st April 2005 - Thursday

Slept a lot better last night as no needles or wires were attached to me. Up early today. First thing on my mind: going home today as long as I can get some action down below! It has been a whole week since the morning of my operation. I remember the feelings of anxiety and trepidation like it was yesterday. Something I don't relish remembering. Hopefully in time the memories will fade, just as the scars will. 07:35 - The gates are open. I had a movement, small but satisfying!! This means I'm now on track for home today without having to undergo any of the 'techniques' that Mr. Christmas said they have here to help make your bowels move!! Phew! Never has such importance been placed on such a sensitive subject. 08:10 - A brief visit from Mr. Christmas' team. Nothing much to discuss. I'm free to go! I'll have to return to clinic in about 3 weeks time for a follow-up on my operation, scars etc. Had some Weetabix for breakfast. Time soon passed, despite the morning traffic, Ken soon arrived to pick me up. I had managed to get myself packed and ready to leave. At home I have done nothing but relax on the sofa and catch up with a few things that needed doing. I've carefully eaten some solid foods since I've been back home, methodically and in small amounts, being careful not to give my settling stomach too much to do. Getting around is still difficult and uncomfortable but should ease more and more as the days go on. I am most certainly looking forward to a good solid undisturbed night's sleep in my own bed! It's all about recovery now; I found a good link on the internet for the basic care of scars, here. I intend to look after them as best I can.

22nd April 2005 - Friday

Finally had what seems like my first good sleep in ages. Although movement for me is restricted (I prefer to sleep flat on my back to avoid the scars), I managed a good 6-8 hours solid sleep. Haven't done much at all again today. Looks like this will be the order of the day for at least a few more days as I get more and more mobile each day. Ken very kindly went over to my Mum's house to pick her up as I'm not up to driving yet. This is the first time I've seen my Mum since before the operation. Whilst she was here I had a surprise delivery of some lovely flowers and exquisite nibbles from a friend who has been nothing but very supportive and thoughtful throughout this whole journey of mine. So it's sofa and good food for a bit. Looking forward to a good sleep again tonight. Almost forgot... I'm 33 and 8 months old today!

23rd April 2005 - Saturday

Another good night's sleep. Again a little more comfortable than the previous night (slowly but surely). I've put a couple more pictures up in the gallery today. The first, one I know a lot of you have been looking out for, is a first glimpse at my scars just over 1 week on from the operation. The second showing my hair growth so far and the tiger marks on my shoulder that are still just as prominent. I'm cutting ALL my hair off tomorrow, only once, just to promote its growth and make it stronger. Looking at a picture of a rather skinny me leads me to contemplation about all that has happened to me since diagnosis. Certainly a lot indeed. I have been through quite a battle, a battle that for the time being is far from over. I still await results which could mean more operations (potentially disabling) and more hospital visits for sometime to come. I hope this is not the case as, after nearly 5 months of treatment and hospital visits, I am keen to return to normal life. The centre of my back still hurts quite a bit and I'm beginning to think that it's related to my insides healing up; there's been quite a bit cut away from inside me as I understand it. It could also be to do with the fact that I'm sitting around a bit more than usual. Also the Tinnitus is becoming more annoying and apparent, especially in the evenings. I hope this subsides soon. For the back pain last night I resorted to 1000mg of Paracetamol for the first time since I've been home. With a little help from some more Paracetamol (1000mg), I managed to get out to an important Masonic meeting this afternoon. Something I wanted to achieve and had focused on. Only 9 days after major surgery, a lot of people were very surprised to see me indeed. On returning home I enjoyed a lovely dinner and soon retired for the night.

24th April 2005 - Sunday

Again last night's sleep was a little better than the previous night. Movement is a lot less restricted now and standing up/sitting down isn't as awkward anymore. Settling down to watch the Formula 1 today, looking forward perhaps one day I will actually go to a race somewhere in the world. I've certainly found recently I've been thinking of a lot of things I'd like to do once this is all over, so perhaps I can see beyond this after all. Apart from that I've sat around surfing the internet and watching TV. I'm starting to get itchy feet for things to do, although not always easy I'm beginning to get bored of sitting around! I'm going to leave the hair shaving ceremony until tomorrow.

Up early today. I've added some more content to the days surrounding my operation so if you feel like a re-read you can start off on the 13th April. Although mainly related to my battle, I hope it can be of some assistance to those of you that may be embarking on similar treatment or a hospital stay. 26th April 2005 - Tuesday

Didn't sleep brilliantly last night, much to do with my back aching although mobility in bed has greatly improved and I can change sleeping positions quite easily. After having read yesterday's entry in full I wonder if you asked the question, "what was the interesting call about, from the hospital?" Well, as the results of the Pathology are now in, my Doctor and his Registrar decided not to make me worry unnecessarily until next Tuesday's meeting, hence the call from the Registrar, Dr. Dancey. The Cancer that was so skilfully removed from my body is ALL DEAD. This means the likelihood of an operation being required on my lungs is now very slim indeed; the chances are practically next to nothing. The battle is almost and practically over now, a battle that has lasted almost 5 months. I mentioned on the phone that we have won this triumph together, we being the skilled and professional hospital care I have been given under the watchful eye of my Consultant, Dr. Savage, the surgical skills of Mr. Christmas and the care of their respective Registrars. The response was that I too was a very big part of this battle and without a good spirit, a positive attitude and some serious fight in me, it may never have turned out like this at all. Of course thanks goes to all of you who have supported me and kept me in the right frame of mind. I still have a meeting next Tuesday to discuss this further but a great weight has been lifted from my mind. This is FANTASTIC NEWS. I am not shouting from the roof tops yet but you can probably tell I'm pretty happy about it. Of all the things that could have happened at this stage, this is the best outcome I could have wished for. All that should remain now is a CT Scan in the not too distant future. This will hopefully show that the tumours in my lungs (now very likely to be dead as the pathology evidence is in) have shrunk and are slowly fading away. Then regular blood tests for many years to monitor any (hopefully none of course) future Cancer that may appear in my body. I will be in remission from Cancer for the rest of my life, a life that soon -once I recover from surgery and come back to full health- I can return to and put this part of far behind me. My Dad is arriving soon to help me out for about a week. Unless anything else exciting happens between now and next Tuesday (3rd May), I'll leave you all with the good news until then. Enjoy the rest of your week and have a little smile on your face for me!

Congratulations to Dr. and, of course, Mrs. Savage, who are proud parents of their new arrival 'Lucy', born on Wednesday 4th May. Perhaps one day in the future, when the time is right and granted the opportunity by the powers of fate and all that controls it, I may be able to introduce Dr. Savage to a new-born of my very own! Although, after all the chemicals that have been pumped into my body over the past 5 months, my chances have been reduced somewhat. So, back to my meeting today... 9th May 2005 - Monday

Today marks 5 months since diagnosis. As the weeks and months pass by, I am gaining strength all the time. I hope I can soon put all of this behind me; into the distant realms of my memory. Undeniably an awful lot has gone on. I face a future of relative uncertainty for quite some time, and side effects which could possibly stay with me forever. Still, I am grateful for the life my treatment and the professional care surrounding it has granted me thus far and, hopefully, well into the future. My bodily functions have changed in comparison to what I was used to before diagnosis: different feelings, different twinges and different schedules. It may be purely because I am eating a far healthier diet now or might be combined with the fact that my body has been to hell and back with chemotherapy and then, on top of that, my intestines repositioned during surgery. Some of the obstacles that face us all on a daily basis are but a small price to pay, considering. I have most certainly been taught an eye-opening lesson over the past few months.

10th May 2005 - Tuesday

Arrived at the Hospital on time for my appointment today. One of those days when the traffic was 'just' quick enough to get there in time and parking was available right outside the correct entrance. The Urology clinic at Charing Cross was very busy and I was seen within about 40 minutes of arriving. Mr. Christmas wasn't in attendance at the meeting, however, I was seen by his Registrar, who gave me all the answers I needed. They are very happy with my progress following surgery and the symptoms and soreness I am experiencing are all normal and to be expected. It will take a while for my body to come back to normal from surgery, however. Currently I have no major complaints or pains and all my bodily functions are working as expected. My scars are healing well and all appears completely normal with them. I went over every thing I could think of with the Registrar; the meeting was over and done with quite swiftly. He had also read up on how I was, here at the web site, so a lot of his questions had been answered already. I asked about exactly what was removed from me. Obviously, the right testicle has gone along with its plumbing in that area, then the right side para-aortic lymph nodes or 'retroperitoneal lymph nodes' have been removed along with all of the plumbing leading to the testicle. Quite a bit of stuff then, no wonder I'm lighter! It's all bits I just don't need anymore. He also confirmed that the tumours located in both areas were 'necrotic' tumours (evidence of cancer tissue but DEAD). I am now effectively signed off from the care of the post surgery clinic and fully back in to the hands of the oncology clinic. Unless anything happens, see you back here on the 19th May and more importantly again on the 27th May. Please see the schedule for appointment updates.

19th May 2005 - Thursday

Blood test today. Charing Cross Clinic 8 almost had tumbleweed blowing through the chairs in the waiting room. I was seen to very quickly indeed. Bloods were taken Full Blood Count, U&E's, Tumour Markers (HCG, LDH, AFP). After a short wait, my Consultant Dr. Savage was ready to see me. Much of what I have learnt before was gone over in more detail. My upcoming CT Scan and its results are still a hurdle of importance and will determine if any further treatment is required. Based on existing results, further treatment is unlikely to be the case. During the meeting my Full Blood Count results came back (this is the quickest I have ever experienced!). Pretty normal and although I am feeling good, things still aren't quite up where they should be (UK average values in brackets)... Platelets 165 (120 - 400) HGB 11.4 (12.5 - 17.0) WBC 5.4 (4.0 - 11.0) RBC 3.69 (4.16 - 5.57) Neutrophils 2.5 (2.0 - 7.5) My health is certainly showing good signs of recovery now, unaided by blood transfusion I'm pleased to say. Still suffering with a few symptoms from Chemotherapy, which could possibly go on for a long time to come, however this is certainly a small price to pay in the grand scheme of things. Here's an update... Tiredness (infrequently) Peripheral Neuropathy (apparent at all times) Tinnitus (apparent at all times, I'm getting used to it) Hair loss (now more like a number 2 crewcut, looks quite good!) Tiger stripes on skin (still apparent, should fade with time).

I am in almost financial ruin, the government don't seem to be able to help much as I've recently found out at a welfare rights meeting. I'll just have to get back to work when I can manage it. Being self-employed doesn't make it easy; it will be tough to recoup and pay back debts of some 5 months so far without work. Anyone got the winning lottery numbers? Onwards and upwards then, making sure I don't make myself ill with worry! I suppose I still have my health! Unless anything happens, see you back here on the 27th May and, more importantly, again on the 2nd June. Please see the schedule for appointment updates.

27th May 2005 - Friday

I'm sleeping really well these days. Just one night recently where I think I was exhausted, I slept a little bit awkwardly and had a crick in my neck all day. Symptoms are pretty much the same although I have noticed a shooting pain in my left leg from my hip down. I hope this is the final one for a long time. I'm about to embark on a breakfast binge as, from 11.00 onwards, I am not allowed to eat prior to the scan at 15.00. I'm also going to try and get to the hospital a little earlier than I'm expected in order for them to put some Emla cream on before I get yet another Cannulation in my arm (this is used to pump the final colouring agent into my blood stream while I'm connected to the CT Scanner). Guess what... one last time, it had to tissue (didn't go into the vein properly), there was quite a bit of blood coming out of my arm too. At this stage, after all that has been done to me, I've given up feeling stuff like this and, quite honestly, it didn't really bother me! After mopping me up, another Cannula was successfully put in, after all that, the Emla wasn't much use for me today. It did look slightly different to the others I had been in. It was all over very quickly and, after I was dressed, I headed home. It has got to be the hottest day today, traffic was a nightmare, everybody heading away early for their bank holiday weekend! So that should be it, the final scan for now. Just the wait for the results now. Hopefully I will be given and shown these on the 2nd June. Until the next instalment...

EXCELLENT !!

Essentially and at first I was quite mad/worried as nobody I asked seemed to know anything. I would have loved to have been told this before hand, wouldn't you? Surgery is scary enough being out of your control. Hmmm, not exactly sure what I am going to do about this but I need to find out more to put my mind at rest. That's all I can think of for now. 9th June 2005 - Thursday

23.30... Today is 6 months to the day since I was diagnosed with my Cancer, a disease that had spread throughout my body unbeknown to me apart from a niggling cough. This time on the 9th of December 2004 I was lying in a medical assessment unit in West Middlesex Hospital, riddled with disease. My friends had gone home and I was left to wonder, alone in my room, what the future would hold for me. So, half a year later, I have been through 4 cycles of extensive BEP chemotherapy followed by major surgery and today I'm in remission from a Cancer that almost killed me. My story has been told and you've all been here to share it with me. Strangely on this anniversary, a documentary was aired this evening called "Bollocks to Cancer". A documentary about a young Testicular Cancer sufferer and survivor. Although a different person and a short production to pack a lot of experience in to, it really brought back some vivid memories and experiences for me. February 2007 9th February 2007 - Friday

Today it was confirmed that I am officially 2 years clear of Cancer. Every year further away from it all, the better. What fantastic news for me. I feel my diary must come to an end now and I must move on with my life. Has it changed me. Honestly? Yes and No! Life has not changed one bit, just the way I perceive things have. If anything life is a little harder but I now have a coping mechanism to help guide me through the bad times. I must say though when the good times are good, they are even more appreciated than before. There probably isn't a day that goes by that I don't see something in the world that lights up my day, mostly it is the person closest to me and other times it's something in the world that you have come to appreciate, more so since the illness. Every single day I think about the fact that I have had Cancer, the tinnitus I get reminds me of all of the chemicals pumped through my body and the twinges and occasional -normal- coughs remind me of the Cancer more so than anything else. The scars I have are fading, like memories fading in to the distance. The times I remember the most whilst in hospital are the days of my illness that will stick with me forever and are some of the most intimate in my life, those that I shared them with will certainly not forget. Something I never spoke about in my diary was the fact that the patients I met in the first male ward I was admitted to, either passed away around me or shortly after I had made their acquaintance. That was tough for me to deal with as at that stage I did not fully understand the likely outcome of my illness, so being surrounded by other people dying of a disease similar to mine was terrifying. I have come to understand Cancer is a very broad term! I think the night and morning before my operation were the most terrifying moments for me, I could not control my emotions and they were laid bare for all to see. The moment the lift doors closed to take me up to surgery was quite honestly one of the hardest things I have ever experienced as I thought I would never see anyone ever again. I still get upset now if I think about it too much. Financially I still find things hard, scrambling back to financial health has been a battle in itself and has certainly pulled me down. Testosterone levels and low feelings at times also have affected the way I handle my life. In fact right now I am going through a sudden and difficult personal time and finding it very hard to deal with. Sometimes we just cannot understand what awaits us in life. Still, I'm a survivor, am thankful for it and you can be too! 2008

THOUGHTS ALONG THE WAY


This is where I post anything that I feel I need to say that doesn't fit within the diary, some of it nonsense, some of it helpful... Over the past year and half (mid 2003 - 2005) I've dealt with a great deal of personal upset and some stressful times (none of which I will go in to as that is not what this site is about, those of you that know me well, will know enough), so all in all I've had a pretty shitty couple of years just pass by, which in some instances doctors suggest can bring on poor health especially if it's lying dormant there in the first place ready to attack. It might be tough at times, but we must all remember to take stock of life and try not to get too stressed or upset about situations that cannot be avoided or are inevitable. Easy to say, much harder to do. Time is the greatest healer of all. I hope we can all look forward to a brilliant New Year in 2005 with many more to follow. I'm not ashamed, embarrassed or afraid of my Cancer, this web-site is for all to see from whatever walk of life, gain strength from and be aware for themselves... pass the word around. Each and everyone of us is made different, so each and everyone of us has a different reaction to everything. Chemotherapy reacts with everyone differently, my experience is here for all to see, if you face it I hope you can stay positive about fighting your condition and not worry too much about the possible side effects from any treatment or surgery. I really hope this site will not only provide a link for all my friends to keep up to date with what's going on with me but also to provide inspiration, positivity and drive to others who need that extra kick. Just go for what you want. Males... and believe me I speak from experience! If YOU think you have an irregularity down below (yes YOU and YOUR BALLS ) or are unsure EVEN AFTER getting yourself checked out, insist on having an Ultrasound Scan, in retrospect if I was offered one I would have known sooner and my Cancer spread would not have been so bad, thus increasing my percentage of making a full recovery from 80 percent to 99 percent!! AND... KEEP CHECKING YOURSELF AT LEAST ONCE EVERY OTHER MONTH. General advice for all of you... If YOU are unsure about a health issue EVEN AFTER getting yourself checked out, insist on having it checked again, do some research on the internet. DON'T LEAVE IT, IT MAY NOT GO AWAY. Mortgages... Critical Illness Cover, do you have it, it might be worth your while thinking about it! Medical Insurance... if you have it then good on you, but do you know how much it will ACTUALLY cover you in the event that something goes wrong? I pay a fair amount each month and will only get £50 a night for my hospital stays (up to a max of £2000) and NO private care or attention at all, so long as the NHS can treat me within 6 weeks, which in this instance is guaranteed as my life is on the line. Quite a few people have suggested that I read the Lance Armstrong story or Bob Champions story, they have both suffered from Testicular Cancer and recovered. Of course it's a comfort to hear that people survive and an inspiration that they live on to achieve many wonderful things, but from my discussions with other patients who are fellow sufferers they would rather experience things in their own way not through the eyes and thoughts of someone else. Perhaps after this whole experience has ended I will invest in a copy and be able to relate to their stories, but right now and for example I do not want to be reading about a particular side effect that I just don't want to hear about or will simply not touch me. Merry Christmas 2004, if Santa brings me anything for this coming year lets hope its my good health and full recovery from this Cancer. I hope he's brought you just what you wished for.
