The diary

May 2005

Recovery and waiting for scan results

Personal diaryAbout 9 min read

3rd May 2005 - Tuesday


My hospital appointment has been cancelled today. It has been postponed until Thursday the 5th of May.

A combination of a very busy Hospital Clinic and my Consultant is otherwise engaged so I would not be able to meet with him.

Day by day, I have been gaining strength and ability in movement. I'm able to get about now and do most things apart from lifting heavy things.

Until Thursday, then. I will report my latest blood results and the outcome of the meeting.

5th May 2005 - Thursday


Woke up early today in readiness for my hospital meeting. Traffic was bad en route to London and parking was atrocious at the Hospital so I ended up arriving about 15 minutes late for my meeting.

Clinic 8 at Charing Cross Hospital is a busy place most of the time. Taking blood tests seems to take forever, utilising a numbered ticket system to keep your place in line.

It turned out that my Consultant wasn't in attendance today either! Much to his delight I found out why. Congratulations to Dr. and, of course, Mrs. Savage, who are proud parents of their new arrival 'Lucy', born on Wednesday 4th May.

Perhaps one day in the future, when the time is right and granted the opportunity by the powers of fate and all that controls it, I may be able to introduce Dr. Savage to a new-born of my very own! Although, after all the chemicals that have been pumped into my body over the past 5 months, my chances have been reduced somewhat.

So, back to my meeting today... I didn't really learn much more than what I had been told on the phone a couple of weeks ago (April 26th). The next notable date for me will be my forthcoming CT scan on the 27th May. The results of which will be provided to me at a meeting soon after. To recap, as the Cancer removed from my body recently was found to be all dead, the chances of the tumours in my lungs being alive are even slimmer than they were before. Therefore, further surgery is unlikely to be required. Still, I have my fingers crossed that these mild twinges and pains I get from my lungs on occasion are just part of the healing process. They certainly serve as a stark reminder!

Also had a brief chat about Peripheral Neuropathy, of which amongst other side effects I have now confirmed that I am suffering from very slightly.

I'm still taking it easy although I am becoming increasingly active as the scars hurt less each and every day. Still, it will take at least 6 weeks from the date of my operation before I can begin to go at full steam!

See you back here on the 9th May, which will be exactly 5 months from diagnosis.

9th May 2005 - Monday


Today marks 5 months since diagnosis. As the weeks and months pass by, I am gaining strength all the time. I hope I can soon put all of this behind me; into the distant realms of my memory.

Undeniably an awful lot has gone on. I face a future of relative uncertainty for quite some time, and side effects which could possibly stay with me forever. Still, I am grateful for the life my treatment and the professional care surrounding it has granted me thus far and, hopefully, well into the future.

My bodily functions have changed in comparison to what I was used to before diagnosis: different feelings, different twinges and different schedules. It may be purely because I am eating a far healthier diet now or might be combined with the fact that my body has been to hell and back with chemotherapy and then, on top of that, my intestines repositioned during surgery.

Some of the obstacles that face us all on a daily basis are but a small price to pay, considering. I have most certainly been taught an eye-opening lesson over the past few months.

10th May 2005 - Tuesday


Arrived at the Hospital on time for my appointment today. One of those days when the traffic was 'just' quick enough to get there in time and parking was available right outside the correct entrance.

The Urology clinic at Charing Cross was very busy and I was seen within about 40 minutes of arriving.

Mr. Christmas wasn't in attendance at the meeting, however, I was seen by his Registrar, who gave me all the answers I needed. They are very happy with my progress following surgery and the symptoms and soreness I am experiencing are all normal and to be expected.

It will take a while for my body to come back to normal from surgery, however. Currently I have no major complaints or pains and all my bodily functions are working as expected. My scars are healing well and all appears completely normal with them.

I went over every thing I could think of with the Registrar; the meeting was over and done with quite swiftly. He had also read up on how I was, here at the web site, so a lot of his questions had been answered already.

I asked about exactly what was removed from me. Obviously, the right testicle has gone along with its plumbing in that area, then the right side para-aortic lymph nodes or 'retroperitoneal lymph nodes' have been removed along with all of the plumbing leading to the testicle.

Quite a bit of stuff then, no wonder I'm lighter! It's all bits I just don't need anymore.

He also confirmed that the tumours located in both areas were 'necrotic' tumours (evidence of cancer tissue but DEAD).

I am now effectively signed off from the care of the post surgery clinic and fully back in to the hands of the oncology clinic.

Unless anything happens, see you back here on the 19th May and more importantly again on the 27th May. Please see the schedule for appointment updates.

19th May 2005 - Thursday


Blood test today. Charing Cross Clinic 8 almost had tumbleweed blowing through the chairs in the waiting room. I was seen to very quickly indeed. Bloods were taken Full Blood Count, U&E's, Tumour Markers (HCG, LDH, AFP).

After a short wait, my Consultant Dr. Savage was ready to see me. Much of what I have learnt before was gone over in more detail. My upcoming CT Scan and its results are still a hurdle of importance and will determine if any further treatment is required. Based on existing results, further treatment is unlikely to be the case.

During the meeting my Full Blood Count results came back (this is the quickest I have ever experienced!). Pretty normal and although I am feeling good, things still aren't quite up where they should be (UK average values in brackets)...

Platelets 165 (120 - 400)
HGB 11.4 (12.5 - 17.0)
WBC 5.4 (4.0 - 11.0)
RBC 3.69 (4.16 - 5.57)
Neutrophils 2.5 (2.0 - 7.5)

My health is certainly showing good signs of recovery now, unaided by blood transfusion I'm pleased to say. Still suffering with a few symptoms from Chemotherapy, which could possibly go on for a long time to come, however this is certainly a small price to pay in the grand scheme of things.

Here's an update...

Tiredness (infrequently)
Peripheral Neuropathy (apparent at all times)
Tinnitus (apparent at all times, I'm getting used to it)
Hair loss (now more like a number 2 crewcut, looks quite good!)
Tiger stripes on skin (still apparent, should fade with time).

I am in almost financial ruin, the government don't seem to be able to help much as I've recently found out at a welfare rights meeting. I'll just have to get back to work when I can manage it. Being self-employed doesn't make it easy; it will be tough to recoup and pay back debts of some 5 months so far without work. Anyone got the winning lottery numbers?

Onwards and upwards then, making sure I don't make myself ill with worry! I suppose I still have my health!

Unless anything happens, see you back here on the 27th May and, more importantly, again on the 2nd June. Please see the schedule for appointment updates.

27th May 2005 - Friday

I'm sleeping really well these days. Just one night recently where I think I was exhausted, I slept a little bit awkwardly and had a crick in my neck all day.

Symptoms are pretty much the same although I have noticed a shooting pain in my left leg from my hip down. I'm putting it down to increased activity; after all, I have been lying around not doing much at all in terms of movement for the past 5 months.

CT Scan day today. I hope this is the final one for a long time. I'm about to embark on a breakfast binge as, from 11.00 onwards, I am not allowed to eat prior to the scan at 15.00.

During these 4 hours, I have to drink a colouring agent called Gastrografin at 11.00, again at 13.00, then again when I arrive for the scan. I'll be mixing it with the permitted 300ml of water/orange squash, as the colouring agent doesn't taste very nice at all!

I'm also going to try and get to the hospital a little earlier than I'm expected in order for them to put some Emla cream on before I get yet another Cannulation in my arm (this is used to pump the final colouring agent into my blood stream while I'm connected to the CT Scanner).

Arrived at the hospital in good time and applied the Emla cream in two locations.

After changing into a gown, I was soon taken to have my Cannula put in. Guess what... one last time, it had to tissue (didn't go into the vein properly), there was quite a bit of blood coming out of my arm too. At this stage, after all that has been done to me, I've given up feeling stuff like this and, quite honestly, it didn't really bother me!

After mopping me up, another Cannula was successfully put in, after all that, the Emla wasn't much use for me today. They decided to use another vein location, different from the secondary position I had prepared.

I was then ushered in to the CT Scanner room; I was told it was a relatively new one. It did look slightly different to the others I had been in.

It was all over very quickly and, after I was dressed, I headed home.

It has got to be the hottest day today, traffic was a nightmare, everybody heading away early for their bank holiday weekend!

So that should be it, the final scan for now. Just the wait for the results now. Hopefully I will be given and shown these on the 2nd June. Until the next instalment...

This is my personal experience, not medical advice.
