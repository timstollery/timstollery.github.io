The diary

April 2005

Surgery and the days around it

Personal diaryAbout 30 min read

5th April 2005 - Tuesday


Arrived at the hospital just before the blood testing room closed at Clinic 8 for the morning.

Ordered up my own blood tests as no reference to what was needed could be found. Full Blood Count, U&E's inc. Magnesium, Tumour Markers (HCG, LDH, AFP). How do I remember all that? After some 4 months of treatment and blood tests, you can pretty much become an expert in knowing what's required at times!

I will not have any results from the blood tests until tomorrow at the earliest.

I've been feeling pretty healthy over the past week or so. Still catching my breath a little if I do too much; this is probably more to do with the fact that I'm out of condition than in poor health. The strangest feeling is knowing there is still cancer inside my body (hopefully dead) and thinking ahead to my operation next week, asking myself "why do I need it, I feel OK". Obviously I know the real answers but it's a weird feeling knowing that soon I will be undergoing major surgery for what appears from the outside to be for no reason at all.

I get the odd twinge in my lungs, nothing painful or awkward and perhaps I only perceive it mentally. It certainly serves as a sobering reminder of what may be in store for me with regard to my lungs and any operations that may be required of them in the future.

I have accepted the fact that next week I will be on an operating table. I will be undergoing a major procedure called an RPLND as well as an Orchiectomy, all in the same sitting. Quite an ordeal for my body: it will be approximately a 1 hour operation, 3-5 days recovery in hospital and a further 2-3 weeks at home, doing not much at all.

I have been told that, in my case, the RPLND will be quite a quick and easy procedure for the surgeon to complete due to the small size of the tumour he is removing (approx. 2cm in diameter). However, it still means that I will have a substantial scar down the centre of my stomach, some 6-10 inches in length.

Believe me, I'm scared of what lies ahead for me. I'm trying to stay positive and keep my mind off my forthcoming agenda but time is getting thin now, the operation and its undeniable results are looming over me. I haven't been getting to sleep very quickly or easily at night. My mind races with apprehensive thoughts, although when I do finally sleep, I sleep well and deeply (I've never had a problem there!)

My phone has been quiet, which I thank you all for, the more I talk about "how I am" and "how I feel" worries me more and more each day. The more I keep my mind off things and just get on with it, the better I'll be. Your messages of support are still greatly appreciated; it's comforting to see all the support I have out there.

I'll be back to report on my blood results as soon as I receive them.

I'll probably repeat myself a bit as everything's going over and over in my mind, more and more with each passing day.

7th April 2005 - Thursday


Managed to get up early after another late night last night. Had to get myself to the garage early to sort some things out for my car.

The tiredness caught up with me this afternoon while visiting my Mum and I had a quick snooze. The stress of this forthcoming operation is beginning to catch up with me; I almost got myself quite upset today whilst talking to my Mum, a few tears showing. The additional operation (RPLND) I am to have is not exactly the ideal scenario I had hoped for, but I have to keep positive, strong and, most of all, keep moving forward. Something I don't always find it easy to focus on. Understandable, given the circumstances, I guess.

My blood results came through by phone late this afternoon. I'm still not 100% (probably about 60% - 70%) in terms of blood health, but it's good enough to go for my operation next week. I will have another blood test on the day before my operation and, if required, may need another blood transfusion to top things up a little. This explains my unfit feelings recently, it's not that apparent but I have noticed a reduction in performance from my body, at least from what I am used to being able to do (pre diagnosis).

My HCG tumour marker still remains below 2 or untraceable, which is positive news. I have added the latest test date to the HCG results section of the site.

It's about time I updated my symptoms...

Tiredness (infrequently)
Tinnitus (chemo side effect, sometimes annoying)
Hair loss (a light fluff appearing)
Itchy face (due to hair growth returning)
Bruised fingers, small sores appearing (long gone)
Oral Thrush (long gone)
Tiger stripes on skin (still apparent, a chemo side effect)
Bowel Movements (very occasional pain, pretty much back to normal)
Night Sweats (gone)
Cough (very occasional, slight, different to before)
Temperature and chills (none)
Nausea (none).

Spoke to my Dad today - as I do most days - to update him with the latest news. He is coming on the 13th when I'm admitted to hospital, and will be with me before I go in to theatre for my surgery the following day. He asked if there was anything I needed him to bring up; I just said "some courage".

Until the 12th of April then... which is when I have a quick and very easy visit to the hospital for an ultrasound.

The date that is playing on my mind the most is Thursday the 14th April, the day of my operation. I somehow feel that I will never be the same again after that day.

9th April 2005 - Sunday


Today marks 4 months exactly since diagnosis.

It has been an unpleasant 4 month lesson in human mortality. There is light at the end of the tunnel but this is far from over yet, I'm still nervous about my forthcoming operation and the results that will transpire.

I have received some really supportive emails recently, for which I am eternally grateful. One of which read...

"I know there is probably no telling you right now, but I have been there recently and you shouldn't worry. I bet you, like me will be checking out of hospital on the Monday morning following your op, feeling nothing but a little sore and you like me, now 3 months on, will feel exactly the same as you did before, and you will be cancer free."

Very comforting to read, amongst many other emails, it's something I will most certainly keep in mind to help me through the coming days.

I managed to spend the day with my Mum today. We went to Richmond Park for some fresh air, tea and cakes! I hope to be spending many more days out with my Mum in the future.

Just one 'whole' day left for me now, before I start my hospital business.

12th April 2005 - Tuesday


I woke up quite early and suddenly this morning. I immediately felt my stomach wondering if there was a scar there or not. I cannot remember dreaming and slept very well, however I guess I must have 'lived' the operation in my sleep and awoke to find no scar!

Just goes to show that this is really starting to play on my mind now. I cannot pinpoint exactly what is worrying me so much, probably the aftermath and results of this forthcoming operation. Also, it is my first ever major operation under general anaesthetic; I have some reservations about that too!

I'm off to have an Ultrasound scan today at 11.45. I'm also going to try to find my Doctor for a quick word and to pop up and have a look at the ward I am scheduled to stay in.

...back home now. Had a good look at my Ultrasound scan while it was being carried out. The doctor was very helpful and showed me all that there was to see, pointing out on screen all the bad bits in my right testicle. I'll be rid of it on Thursday!

Afterwards, Ken and I went for a bit of a search about in the hospital for my Doctor and also had a look at the 8th floor ward which I am to be recovering in.

Managed to catch up with my Registrar and Doctor, both of whom again tried to allay my fears about my forthcoming operation. There just seems to be no telling me at the moment, but I guess every bit of reassurance I get will slowly chip away at the barriers and worries I have built up for myself.

I introduced myself to the sister in charge of 8 North; she filled me in on some of the things I am to expect once I'm admitted tomorrow and also some of the tubes and machines that will be around me once I come out from surgery. It doesn't sound pleasant in the slightest. I will know more tomorrow once I'm at the hospital which is tentatively arranged for 14.30.

My Tinnitus is much more apparent today, perhaps connected to the increasing stress I am feeling.

I'm staying at my Mum's house tonight. I will not be able to see her again for quite some time after tomorrow's admission to hospital. Although, once I'm home there will, of course, be times when she can come to see me while I recover.

13th April 2005 - Wednesday


Woke up suddenly at 05:00 this morning but managed to get back off to sleep shortly after. Again, no dreams apparent, but there's certainly something going on up there in that brain of mine!

Finally got up at around 09:30 this morning, got myself together and then helped my Mum get herself together. For me, saying goodbye to my Mum this morning felt like a final farewell. I know it's not, but I found it hard and was quite emotional. My Mum is full of hope and encouragement for me.

I called the Hospital admissions department as instructed at 12:00, only to be told that instructions from the bed manager on my intended ward had not been relayed as yet and it would be best if they called me when they were ready. I hope this delay does not have a knock on or rushed effect with regard to my preparation for surgery which is supposed to be taking place tomorrow.

I returned to my house where my Dad was waiting for me. All that I can do is await the call now so I'm trying to keep busy in the meantime.

15:10 - Got the call; now I'm doing my final bits, getting ready to set off for the hospital.

There should be a picture of me in the Gallery shortly, one of an intact 'warranty valid' un-scarred torso. This was taken just before leaving for the hospital.

Soon you will see the results of my operation in glorious colour and Dr Tim Christmas' handiwork.

I arrived at the hospital just after the admissions department had closed, so I made my way up to the ward where I was to be admitted. There was a bit of confusion on the ward, as I was supposed to collect my notes from admissions upon arrival.

I spent the rest of the evening worrying about tomorrow. I had to take some Bowel prep in order to clear me out before the operation. There were plenty of tears, mostly apprehension on my part. This was my first operation ever and a major one at that.

14th April 2005 - Thursday


Woke up early, the Bowel prep from the night before soon did the trick!

I was told that the operation was scheduled for 10:30 and was visited by the Anaesthetist. Apparently pre operation and some time ago, normal procedure is to be admitted to a pre operative clinic. This was something that perhaps the schedule of my operation's urgency did not allow. A lot of things that fortunately I had taken the time to find out about a few days ago were sprung on me. More trauma for my ever depreciating mood to cope with!

It was suggested that I take a tablet to calm my nerves. I'm not a big fan of suppressive tablets or medication, however, on this occasion, I went with the advice of everyone around me and was given a Temazepam. I'm not so sure it worked much but everyone around me said I was a little calmer.

Once dressed in gown and surgical stockings, I was ready to go when called upon. I climbed on to my hospital bed, the bars locked in to place and off I was wheeled.

As you can imagine, I was VERY nervous and very emotional. What felt like my final good-byes were said over the phone to my Mum and by the lift door to my Dad and Ken.

Once on the 14th floor, it was just a short journey and I was in the Anaesthetic room. I was comforted and further prepared for surgery. The last words I remember saying through my tears as the anaesthetic worked its cold way up my arm and over my face were "please look after me..."

Today's entry also written by Ken...

Tim's Dad and I arrived early this morning in preparation for today's events. The Surgeons, Senior Registrar and the Anaesthetist came round early to explain a few things and get Tim's consent signature for the ops. It was a bit up in the air as to whether he would require a Central Line. This is where they feed a pipe directly into a main artery, allowing for easy drug and fluid intake. He was told that he would also be fitted with a catheter as well as being cannulated in his hand for other fluid and drug intake. The final two tubes to be fitted were a tube up his nose and into his stomach to drain off fluid and a tube inserted into his side to drain fluids around the operated area.

He was given a tablet around 09:30 to take the edge off his nerves, basically a 'Chill Pill' and standard pre-op procedure. He was then taken up to Surgery on the 14th floor around 10:30, and we gave our best wishes to an emotional Tim as he was wheeled into the lift stretched out on his bed. We were then told to enquire in around two hours as to his condition; we weren't allowed up to the 14th floor.

The Sister rang up for us at around 12:30 and we were told he was still in theatre and to check again in an hour. We weren't overly concerned as to the amount of time that had passed, as we didn't know exactly when the operations started so couldn't gauge anything. Sister rang again at 13:30 and was told he had been out of theatre for 45 minutes. Also that he was in the recovery area on the 14th floor and was expected back down to us in under an hour.

After some pacing, plenty of clock watching and numerous phone calls to his Mum and brother to keep them updated, he arrived back into the ward and we followed. The curtain had been pulled round as the surgery nurses checked his dressings and handed him over to the ward's care. I peeped through the curtains to find him asleep, looking very pale, pipes and tubes seeming to come out of every part of him, and being fed oxygen through a mask. As if sensing someone other than the nurses was looking on, he opened his eyes. I gave him a questioning thumbs up, and he replied with a reassuring nod. He was back.

He was then pounced upon by the observations nurse and was told he would have his vitals taken every half hour for the first two hours and then every hour for the following two hours. He managed a few groggy words to us just to say he was fine, but in pain. One of the tubes going in to him is a Morphine drip, which is in his control. He has a button that he can push to feed in Morphine as and when he needs it, but it will lock out if pushed too much in quick succession. So far the lock hasn't been enforced. I asked him on a scale of 1 to 10 how bad the pain was, he thought about it, then said 7, promptly followed by a squeeze of the button!

He was complaining that his mouth was very dry, especially as he hadn't drunk any fluid since midnight. I went off to see if he was allowed to drink anything, but the Sister explained that he can't eat or drink for a few days depending on how it goes. What was allowed was to moisten his mouth and lips using ice cubes and sponge sticks dabbed in water; a willing female happily played Nurse. Now able to speak slightly better, Tim made a call to his Mum to reassure her that he was okay.

We then waited for the Surgeon's Senior Registrar to appear to explain how the Ops went. He arrived around 16:30. After asking how Tim was feeling, he told us that the Ops went better than expected as they didn't need to insert the drain tube into his side, and the central line wasn't needed. He went on to say that the tumour from the abdomen and cancerous testicle had been removed without any problems, and to expect pathology results in two weeks. He said the scar on his stomach seems also to be smaller than expected, about 7 inches, so another piece of good news.

He's now in and out of sleep and not really any less groggy than when we first saw him, but apparently this is to be expected for a few hours yet. I'm not sure in his current condition if he's going to be tortured by the smell of dinner about to be served to all the other patients, but based on the past, he's not missing much.

That's about it I think. All in all it's gone really well, the tumour and cancerous testicle are gone, and he's got fewer tubes and shorter scars than expected. Hopefully that's the last piece of treatment he will need, and it's just recovery from here on in.

I doubt Tim will be able to manage more than one day without using his Mac, so expect tomorrow's entry to come from the patient himself.

15th April 2005 - Friday


Thanks Ken for your moving entry yesterday.

Still feeling groggy today, although a bit more with it.

I have so many tubes coming out of me, finding a little difficult to breathe and swallow properly. Movement is severely restricted and uncomfortable.

I'm using my PCA (Morphine) button a fair bit to aid pain relief. This also keeps me subdued and not fully aware of my surroundings.

Still only wetting my mouth with these special sponges today.

16th April 2005 - Saturday


Briefly got out of bed today. Started to sip very tiny amounts of water. My stomach has started to bubble very lightly today.

Mr. Christmas' Registrar recommended that I start drinking a bit more today, I asked if orange squash would be OK and he said yes.

Subsequently I have very slowly sipped two cups of orange squash and they have caused me immense stomach cramps.

NG tube removed this afternoon. Horrible and not very tasty, it didn't hurt coming out but wasn't pleasant at all.

17th April 2005 - Sunday


Briefly got out of bed today. I sat in my chair for a bit this morning. Later on I managed 2 very slow laps of the ward accompanied by my IV machine.

One of my cannulations tissued today. I felt the bandage on my arm swelling and called the nurse to stop the IV machine and check my arm. I am so used to cannulations and what they should feel like this was old hat to me. Although uncomfortable and mildly painful, I waited for the sister to come and re-cannulate me in another vein (as you know, an experience I do not relish).

Once the sister arrived, I discussed the possible use of Emla cream with her, she told me they did not have access to any. Now I wish I had brought my own! She attempted and failed at a cannulation, not willing to take any feedback from me 'the patient' she continued to attempt to flush what I said had failed and my arm swelled further, only commenting to me that if it had failed then she should be the judge of it!

I waited a over 1 hour more for a doctor to arrive and have another go. Although an uncomfortable experience, he was successful.

I went back to sipping water today, as I felt the advice given yesterday was a bit hasty and I didn't want to suffer with the stomach cramps again.

The rest of the day soon passed quickly, I watched a couple of DVDs in bed before getting my head down for the night.

18th April 2005 - Monday


Only today was I able and well enough to quickly post a progress notice here with a little help from Ken.

Mr. Christmas came to visit with his team of Registrars and Doctors very early this morning. He removed the bandages from my scars that I was reluctant to look at, and commented in jest that he had a team of plastic surgeons working on me in surgery to make them as unnoticeable as possible. I must admit my first impressions of what must be some quite angry scars are pretty good indeed.

As I had heard before, the Operation had gone very well. Of course, it was more comforting to hear it come from the person that actually performed it!

Mr. Christmas also commented that the tumour from my abdomen was most likely to be dead now he had seen it and removed it, stating that obviously he didn't have microscopic eyes. Another piece of positive news. The jury will still be out on that one until I get conclusive pathology results back in a couple of weeks' time.

I've had my catheter removed today, a very unpleasant experience. I now have to pee in a bottle until further notice, as my output has to be regularly monitored.

I've had my operation wounds cleaned, sprayed and redressed today although the larger bandages are to be left off in favour of airing the wounds for them to heal better.

Managed another 2 very slow laps of the ward aided only by my IV machine to walk with.

19th April 2005 - Tuesday


Today's entries bring you more up to date from the 15th April onwards. As my memory returns and I can type more info, I'll fill in more detail regarding today and the past few days.

Not much sleep at all last night, what with wanting to go to the toilet, having to 'try' and go in a bottle, constant noise from surrounding patients and an awful back ache from being in bed too long.

When I did finally get comfortable or at least exhausted enough to sleep, I was suddenly awoken by Mr. Christmas' Registrar who rattled off some information and promptly left my bedside. I was a bit put out, considering my condition and that I had just woken up, there was no chance to reasonably question or answer his comments.

Then shortly after it was regimental 'out of bed', 'we want to make it' time.

After hardly any sleep, some pain and a lot of discomfort, it really starts to grind at you.

Managed 1 cup of water and 2 cups of warm milk so far today. Stomach seems more settled. I have been asked to eat some soup this afternoon.

I've had a full wash today, brushed my teeth and had a shave. After which I managed 3 laps of the ward accompanied by my trusty IV machine.

A friend brought in some soup for me to eat this afternoon. I have slowly worked my way through a whole tin so far.

I've certainly been a bit more active and able to move about today.

Soup again for dinner.

My 3 units of blood are due to be transfused tonight. Once under way, it meant for a disturbed night's sleep. One unit transfused at a time, each over 3-4 hours with very regular observation and temperature checks on me. This meant waking up almost every hour of the night.

20th April 2005 - Wednesday


Up early today and out of bed. Thought I'd beat them to it, before I'm instructed to "get up and out of bed", plus I didn't get much sleep either, so I was awake anyway.

Feeling better after my lengthy 3 unit blood transfusion which is just about finished now. I'm due a blood test today then, based on the results, perhaps 1 more unit of blood later on.

Just had a cannulation removed from my hand, so only one left in my arm now. Also I'm free of my IV machine for the first time since last Thursday. Hopefully I will be a lot more active and lively today, although I must make sure I do not overdo it or over stretch myself (I don't want to tear open!).

Going to try some Weetabix with warm milk for breakfast today! Managed to get breakfast down but sending food down in to my stomach still hurts a little. Just a few stomach cramps, I'm sure it'll all be back in place soon.

Had a visit from the Registrars and the ward Sister this morning, much more informed and pleasant to talk to. They've started reading my website, too! I didn't have to say much! Finally I get to pee normally again, no more going in a bottle every time. Now I just have to make it to the loo when I need to go!! Previously, I had tried to make the effort to get to the loo, just from a mental point of view and for the exercise. Going in a bottle wasn't a pleasant experience but far more favourable than using a catheter!

Blood test taken (Full Blood Count and U&E's), noticed it was marked 'urgent' so if things move quickly, I'll know my HB level soon enough and either be totally free or transfused another unit of blood later today.

Had a bit of an anomaly occur down below, apparently something to do with the catheter I'd had. Anyway I called the Doctor to double check.

Had a good informative visit from both the Registrars and, later on, my Surgeon. My HB is back to 11.3 now, so no need for the last unit of blood and the final cannulation in my arm was removed.

All that is required of me now is that I go to the loo... (no.2)! I haven't been since last Thursday morning and it's pretty much a medical requirement after the type of major operation I've had. Thing is... hospitals, toilets and me don't quite go together very well. I cannot strain either!! I might have to opt for one of the 'techniques' my surgeon joked about earlier.

So all being well I should be going home tomorrow. I can't wait.

Late this evening I was due another intravenous antibiotic injection, the final one of a 3 day regime (twice a day). As my cannula had been removed I was reluctant to have this and asked if it could be given in tablet form. Unfortunately, this was not possible so I very nicely refused to take it. A bit of an oversight and hasty removal of the final cannula on the Doctors' part earlier. I just couldn't see the point of going through the discomfort of what I particularly dislike for just one small injection of something that I could probably live without. Especially going on an earlier experience in this ward. (Still, I respect I'm no medical expert so I hope it isn't detrimental to my treatment).

21st April 2005 - Thursday


Slept a lot better last night as no needles or wires were attached to me. Up early today. First thing on my mind: going home today as long as I can get some action down below!

It has been a whole week since the morning of my operation. I remember the feelings of anxiety and trepidation like it was yesterday. Something I don't relish remembering. Hopefully in time the memories will fade, just as the scars will.

07:35 - The gates are open. I had a movement, small but satisfying!! This means I'm now on track for home today without having to undergo any of the 'techniques' that Mr. Christmas said they have here to help make your bowels move!! Phew! Never has such importance been placed on such a sensitive subject.

08:10 - A brief visit from Mr. Christmas' team. Nothing much to discuss. I'm free to go! I'll have to return to clinic in about 3 weeks time for a follow-up on my operation, scars etc.

Had some Weetabix for breakfast.

Time soon passed, despite the morning traffic, Ken soon arrived to pick me up. I had managed to get myself packed and ready to leave.

At home I have done nothing but relax on the sofa and catch up with a few things that needed doing.

I've carefully eaten some solid foods since I've been back home, methodically and in small amounts, being careful not to give my settling stomach too much to do.

Getting around is still difficult and uncomfortable but should ease more and more as the days go on. I am most certainly looking forward to a good solid undisturbed night's sleep in my own bed!

It's all about recovery now; I found a good link on the internet for the basic care of scars, here. I intend to look after them as best I can.

22nd April 2005 - Friday


Finally had what seems like my first good sleep in ages. Although movement for me is restricted (I prefer to sleep flat on my back to avoid the scars), I managed a good 6-8 hours solid sleep.

Haven't done much at all again today. Looks like this will be the order of the day for at least a few more days as I get more and more mobile each day.

Ken very kindly went over to my Mum's house to pick her up as I'm not up to driving yet. This is the first time I've seen my Mum since before the operation. Whilst she was here I had a surprise delivery of some lovely flowers and exquisite nibbles from a friend who has been nothing but very supportive and thoughtful throughout this whole journey of mine.

So it's sofa and good food for a bit.

Looking forward to a good sleep again tonight.

Almost forgot... I'm 33 and 8 months old today!

23rd April 2005 - Saturday


Another good night's sleep. Again a little more comfortable than the previous night (slowly but surely).

I've put a couple more pictures up in the gallery today.

The first, one I know a lot of you have been looking out for, is a first glimpse at my scars just over 1 week on from the operation.

The second showing my hair growth so far and the tiger marks on my shoulder that are still just as prominent. This picture makes me look a bit like I've just been released from a POW camp, which is most certainly how I have felt about hospital sometimes, but NOT all the time!! I'm cutting ALL my hair off tomorrow, only once, just to promote its growth and make it stronger.

Looking at a picture of a rather skinny me leads me to contemplation about all that has happened to me since diagnosis. Certainly a lot indeed. I have been through quite a battle, a battle that for the time being is far from over. I still await results which could mean more operations (potentially disabling) and more hospital visits for sometime to come.

I hope this is not the case as, after nearly 5 months of treatment and hospital visits, I am keen to return to normal life.

The centre of my back still hurts quite a bit and I'm beginning to think that it's related to my insides healing up; there's been quite a bit cut away from inside me as I understand it. It could also be to do with the fact that I'm sitting around a bit more than usual. Also the Tinnitus is becoming more annoying and apparent, especially in the evenings. I hope this subsides soon.

For the back pain last night I resorted to 1000mg of Paracetamol for the first time since I've been home.

With a little help from some more Paracetamol (1000mg), I managed to get out to an important Masonic meeting this afternoon. Something I wanted to achieve and had focused on. Only 9 days after major surgery, a lot of people were very surprised to see me indeed.

On returning home I enjoyed a lovely dinner and soon retired for the night.

24th April 2005 - Sunday


Again last night's sleep was a little better than the previous night.

Movement is a lot less restricted now and standing up/sitting down isn't as awkward anymore. I do feel some soreness from the groin scar occasionally but it's nothing I cannot handle!

Settling down to watch the Formula 1 today, looking forward perhaps one day I will actually go to a race somewhere in the world. I've certainly found recently I've been thinking of a lot of things I'd like to do once this is all over, so perhaps I can see beyond this after all. I hope my future isn't dashed by poor results soon.

My Mum came over to see me at my house and I hobbled around the kitchen to make her a big lunch.

Apart from that I've sat around surfing the internet and watching TV. I'm starting to get itchy feet for things to do, although not always easy I'm beginning to get bored of sitting around!

I'm going to leave the hair shaving ceremony until tomorrow.

25th April 2005 - Monday


Up early today.

I've added some more content to the days surrounding my operation so if you feel like a re-read you can start off on the 13th April.

I've also been hard at work on a comprehensive glossary section for my website. Although mainly related to my battle, I hope it can be of some assistance to those of you that may be embarking on similar treatment or a hospital stay. With a little help from Ken it'll be live soon.

More pictures in the gallery today.

Managed to get out for a bit this afternoon, to Sainsbury's and Starbucks. The walk around Sainsbury's was tiring and I soon noticed back and groin pains coming on. Not sure where the back pain is from but the scar on my groin is still mildly sore. Still I soldiered on and topped up on some food shopping.

I had an interesting call from the hospital this afternoon whilst relaxing in Starbucks.

By the time I got home and had eaten dinner most of the evening had already passed, I spent a lot of time on the phone and before I knew it, it was time for bed.

26th April 2005 - Tuesday


Didn't sleep brilliantly last night, much to do with my back aching although mobility in bed has greatly improved and I can change sleeping positions quite easily.

After having read yesterday's entry in full I wonder if you asked the question, "what was the interesting call about, from the hospital?"

Well, as the results of the Pathology are now in, my Doctor and his Registrar decided not to make me worry unnecessarily until next Tuesday's meeting, hence the call from the Registrar, Dr. Dancey.

The Cancer that was so skilfully removed from my body is ALL DEAD. This means the likelihood of an operation being required on my lungs is now very slim indeed; the chances are practically next to nothing.

The battle is almost and practically over now, a battle that has lasted almost 5 months. I mentioned on the phone that we have won this triumph together, we being the skilled and professional hospital care I have been given under the watchful eye of my Consultant, Dr. Savage, the surgical skills of Mr. Christmas and the care of their respective Registrars. The response was that I too was a very big part of this battle and without a good spirit, a positive attitude and some serious fight in me, it may never have turned out like this at all. Of course thanks goes to all of you who have supported me and kept me in the right frame of mind.

I still have a meeting next Tuesday to discuss this further but a great weight has been lifted from my mind. This is FANTASTIC NEWS. I am not shouting from the roof tops yet but you can probably tell I'm pretty happy about it.

Of all the things that could have happened at this stage, this is the best outcome I could have wished for.

All that should remain now is a CT Scan in the not too distant future. This will hopefully show that the tumours in my lungs (now very likely to be dead as the pathology evidence is in) have shrunk and are slowly fading away.

Then regular blood tests for many years to monitor any (hopefully none of course) future Cancer that may appear in my body.

I will be in remission from Cancer for the rest of my life, a life that soon -once I recover from surgery and come back to full health- I can return to and put this part of far behind me.

My Dad is arriving soon to help me out for about a week. Unless anything else exciting happens between now and next Tuesday (3rd May), I'll leave you all with the good news until then.

Enjoy the rest of your week and have a little smile on your face for me!

This is my personal experience, not medical advice.
