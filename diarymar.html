The diary

March 2005

Scans, recovery and the next decisions

Personal diaryAbout 24 min read

1st March 2005 - Tuesday


Got to Charing X about an hour late for my blood test. My Dad accompanied me to the hospital and joined me for a chat with my Doctor. Routine discussions; my treatment has progressed well, now it's recovery time for my body before any operations are carried out.

Spent a considerable amount of time wandering about on the 6th floor catching up with Milton and the staff while waiting for my blood test results. Once they were finally through, I managed to grab the registrar on the 6th floor to discuss them.

My blood health is low but that is to be expected. I wasn't planning on doing anything apart from recovering at home, so the registrar didn't recommend any further medication or injections and gave me a cautionary chat about looking after myself and allowed me to go home.

A quick visit to the local DIY store en route home.

Once home, managed a little dinner and relaxed in front of the TV before hitting the sack.

2nd March 2005 - Wednesday


Woke up not really feeling refreshed at all. Managed to get myself up and dressed but not much more. A little breakfast and I hit the sofa for the rest of the day.

Often snoozing, I found that if I tried to motivate myself to do anything I would just get out of breath and my heart would beat faster and harder.

If I were to mark my health and how I feel out of 10, I'd say I was at about a 6.

3rd March 2005 - Thursday


Much the same as yesterday; spent the day on the sofa. My little health marker I've made up in my head is still at about a 6.

4th March 2005 - Friday


Still much the same, spent the day on the sofa feeling very lethargic indeed. My little health marker now at about 5.

5th March 2005 - Saturday


Still much the same, spent the day on the sofa. Sleeping lots and getting tired very quickly, even if I attempt to move about too much on the sofa. My little health marker still at about a 5.

6th March 2005 - Sunday


Still much the same, spent the day on the sofa. Sleeping lots and getting tired very quickly, even if I attempt to move about too much on the sofa.

Very frustrating indeed, there's so much I want to achieve and even the smallest things seem like too much of a chore.

One thing I really wanted to do was get to see my Mum today, being Mother's Day. It just wasn't going to happen; I could not muster the energy to even lift myself from the sofa. This made me quite disappointed indeed.

I have been eating, but sporadically, and there really hasn't been much I've particularly fancied at all. Still, my Dad has persevered and managed to get me to eat a decent meal each night.

My little health marker now at about a 4. I reckon I'm most certainly due for a blood transfusion this coming week and said as much to my Dad.

7th March 2005 - Monday


My Dad left early this morning for his home, after spending the whole of last week looking after me. Also fitting in quite a bit of DIY that needed doing too!

I rested and slept on the sofa for the rest of the day.

I reckon health marker probably at a 3 by now!

8th March 2005 - Tuesday


Got myself moving this morning, I really don't know where I found the energy from. Drove myself in to Charing Cross for my blood test.

Spoke with the registrar immediately after bloods were taken and nipped up to the 6th floor to see who was about while waiting for my results.

I knew I wasn't feeling well but once I returned for my results at Clinic 8, I was told that I wasn't going home and was to be admitted - to an isolation room!!!

My blood health was so poor I was in quite a bit of danger. Black outs, internal bleeding, serious infections; to mention some of the possible things that could have happened to me if my blood and I were left alone to get any worse!

It seems the cumulative effects of my chemotherapy had really kicked in and my bone marrow was simply not functioning correctly enough to produce goodness for my blood.

After waiting 6 hours for my room to be allocated (a friend had arrived to keep me company) I was moved up to the familiar 6th floor, this time on the North wing in to a room of my very own.

1 GCSF injection and immediately on Antibiotics (Amoxicillin 500mg).

Cannulated twice, the first time failed, eventually the first unit of blood to be transfused was begun. Following which I was transfused 1 pool of platelets and then a following further 2 units of blood. All this would go through the night and my observations were carried out at very close intervals due to the feverish side effects both blood and platelets can give you. No sleep for me!

Together with some friends, I managed to get some clothes and my usual hospital gear brought to me this evening. Remember I only drove in for a blood test earlier and was not expecting to be admitted!

I was sick this evening. Not sure why, may have been the stress of coming back in unexpectedly or simply just the ongoing effects of my poor state of health.

Being in isolation meant strict hygiene and contact with others was at a minimum.

9th March 2005 - Wednesday


Three months since diagnosis today. It passed without me noticing.

I slept for a fair bit today. Someone came over to visit, I doubt I was much company.

They took another blood test, a very slight improvement. Another 1 unit of blood was required, managed to get this in during the evening and was left alone for a good night's sleep.

10th March 2005 - Thursday


Slept really well. Observation day today, if no improvement is found in the level of my platelets today, another pool of platelets will be required.

Fortunately my bloods had recovered suitably well enough for me to just be observed for the day.

My health had certainly picked up a lot more today and spent most of it in bed watching daytime TV!

No need for isolation hygiene requirements anymore. I was free to leave the room and take more visitors. A few visitors came over this evening after which I got myself another brilliant and undisturbed night's sleep.

11th March 2005 - Friday


Slept really well again and felt so much better.

Stuck the daytime TV on, observations taken. I eagerly awaited my blood test today as the results would determine whether I was to be discharged.

I waited some time for my blood test to be taken. The ward was quite busy today as I think they had a very poorly patient whom I had seen them rushing an emergency crash cart to. During my short stay this week I had seen many distressed relatives. Death is something which you become almost oblivious to, especially spending time on a Cancer ward, you know it's happening but shield yourself from the suffering.

I waited patiently and carried on with my new daytime TV addiction.

Finally the registrar decided to do the blood test, as I had not been seen to for ages. They were marked urgent and I awaited the results.

Everything apart from my Magnesium levels had returned to reasonable levels and I was to be discharged as soon as my cannulation was removed and my TTA (To Take Away) medications allocated. Apparently the TTA's were already ready but due to how busy the ward was today, I waited a further 5 hours to be discharged (I think they had thought I'd already left or had forgotten about me. Instead I was happily laying in bed watching the box and chatting to a friend who had been with me for the whole day).

My Magnesium deficiency is down to the damage that the Cisplatin or the 'P' of BEP chemotherapy has done to my body. This is something that will now have to be monitored carefully during each of my blood tests although the doctors do not seem to be overly concerned. There is so much, outside of killing cancer, that chemotherapy does to your body. Killing cancer cells is the desired result, the rest just has to be dealt with as and when it becomes apparent.

I left the hospital at exactly the time I thought I would, right in the thick of the rush hour on a Friday night heading out of London. It was not good.

Once home, I relaxed in front of the TV, ate a huge 2 helpings of chicken and pasta for dinner which someone kindly prepared for me and soon retired for the evening.

12th March 2005 - Saturday

No entries for today.

13th March 2005 - Sunday


No entries for today.

14th March 2005 - Monday


No entries for today.

15th March 2005 - Tuesday

I'm back!

I've updated some of my missing diary entries as best as I can remember them (more to follow soon), right back until the 20th February 2005 and, after a lot of typing, hope you enjoy the read! Apologies for not keeping you all posted on my progress recently, I have been weak at times, convalescing at others. I was sure you'd understand and know your thoughts and prayers were with me during what has been one of the tougher times for me.

Woke up after a long sleep and readied myself for my hospital visit, hopefully today they'll let me go home!

A friend has been staying at my house looking after me for sometime now, it's nice not to have to spend the time at home alone and to have someone around reminding me to eat and helping me with cooking etc.

A quick breakfast and some orange juice and my friend and I sped off to the hospital.

Arrived at Charing Cross Clinic 8 on the 1st floor for my blood test, whilst waiting for the results made myself busy visiting Milton, some other fellow patients and staff up on the 6th floor.

After about 2 hours, I went down to get my results and had a chat with the registrar. My general blood health is on the up, with most of the individual readings from my blood looking more positive than the previous week. Simply put, that means my blood health is not quite at its best but certainly looking like it's heading in the right direction.

In order for me to be ready for any operation, my blood health must be much better than it currently is. The doctors will simply not operate if there is a chance of me getting an infection or not being able to fully bounce back from surgery.

Day by day now, I'm feeling better, trying to do more and more without overdoing it. I've been out to see my Mum for the first time in ages and have been to the supermarket to top up on my shopping. Although I'm doing things a lot slower than normal, at least I'm starting to do things for myself again after being weak for what seems like absolutely ages.

Once I had struggled home through the traffic, I managed a big cooked breakfast for lunch, including some lovely scrambled ducks eggs!

Relaxed on the sofa until dinnertime!

Occasionally I feel tired during the day and, quite frequently, catch the odd half hour here and there, not always being necessary and hopefully becoming less and less apparent as the days go on. I guess if my body is requesting sleep, I'll give it some sleep, but will be much happier when I can stay awake and alert for the whole day without weakening!

A nice dinner followed by strawberries and ice cream for dessert!

Time for more sleep soon.

16th March 2005 - Wednesday


Got up really late today after a long sleep in.

Spent the day relaxing and eating well. Still feeling better although not doing too much at the moment.

This evening I've felt very tired again, although I have good colour in my face and feel quite healthy. I hope this isn't a turning point to low health again. I really haven't done anything at all today that would make me tired out so am finding it a bit strange.

I'll get to bed and hope that I feel more sprightly tomorrow.

17th March 2005 - Thursday


Woke up early but didn't get up until midday today after a good sleep. Feeling OK, got quite a bit to do today, out and about. I hope I don't tire out too quickly.

Ken should be back from his break soon. I'll have updated X-Ray pictures, HCG results and some more photos for you to see imminently.

After a hectic day out, I was really exhausted and glad to be home. I felt more tired than I normally would but still I understand that it's going to take a while for my body to come back to full stamina. I certainly don't feel as weak as I have done in the past. Straight to bed for a solid night's sleep, although a little troubled and apprehensive about my scan tomorrow.

18th March 2005 - Friday


CT Scan day today. I must eat something this morning to keep me going. As of 12pm I am not allowed to eat anything until after my scan at 4pm. During these 4 hours I have to drink a colouring agent called Gastrografin at 12pm, again at 2pm, then again when I arrive for the scan. I'm allowed to mix it with 300ml of orange squash, however it's not meant to taste that nice, I'll let you know!

The drink wasn't too bad and I hope I suitably coloured my insides for a good clear scan.

Traffic was awful getting to the hospital, got there just after 4pm. Waited in the CT reception until about 5pm, when they started to prepare me for the scan. I think I tired my brain out with stress and almost fell asleep waiting.

At this stage I am frightened about what they are going to find inside my body. I'm being looked at through a magnifying glass and the result will determine what, if any, other operations are to be performed on my body. Apprehensive, nervous and worried; I couldn't think straight at all.

Another small cup of colouring fluid, clear in colour with not much taste, then I was cannulated ready to be connected or 'interfaced' with the CT scanner.

I have some really good pictures of me inside the CT scanner which will be up in the gallery soon. Fortunately the CT operator owns the same digital camera as me and she didn't need any instruction on how to use mine!

The scan took just under 10 minutes to complete, there is a recorded voice that asks to 'hold your breath' and 'breathe normally' during the scan. Halfway through, the CT scanner begins pumping yet another colouring fluid in to my veins, warning just beforehand that 'the injection is starting'.

My only thoughts were to stay as still as possible during the scan, in the hope that the stiller I could be, the clearer the picture they would have of my insides.

It was all over quite quickly, my cannulation was removed almost painlessly and yet another hurdle in my journey has been overcome. The next is this Tuesday, the day I find out the results of my scan and how much cancer they need to cut out from inside my body.

Milton was in at the hospital for another blood test and we managed to catch up with each other. We all went across the road for a Pizza Express. Traffic was quite bad being yet another Friday rush hour, so spending a little time catching up with Milton was much better than sitting in stationary traffic on the way home.

Once home, I felt quite tired again and managed about an hour's sleep on the sofa. These tired spells aren't normal for me at all, however I still feel ok before and after sleeping, it's just pure tiredness.

Spent a little time on front of the TV and managed a big bowl of fruit and ice cream as a late snack. Didn't get to bed too early as I just didn't feel tired enough after my nap earlier; once I did get to bed I soon drifted off to sleep.

19th March 2005 - Saturday


Woke up quite early, but soon went back to bed and didn't wake up again until midday! The weather has been really nice over the past few days, going to try and spend some time outside today.

Keeping my mind off of Tuesday's meeting is of paramount importance. I cannot avoid it and shouldn't worry until then, but it still plays on my mind from time to time. All I can do is hope that the chemotherapy has cleared the cancer cells in my body, enough to require only a minor operation. If my health and blood results are on the up, then the operation will be arranged for a date quite soon after Tuesday's meeting.

Dropped my friend off at Heathrow Airport for a flight that I would have most certainly been on with him, if I hadn't been ill. A little heart wrenching, hopefully I will be on that flight one day.

Went over and spent some time with my Mum. The nice weather today helped her good spirits, which was added to with a fair amount of mobility.

Got home in good time for a big dinner before my first evening outing for what seems like an absolute age. Another friend is having a birthday party upstairs at my favourite bar in Richmond and I really wanted to show my face.

Got there at 22.00, lots of people were pleased to see me after such a long time. It was nice to be out although I did take a back seat from the partying and drank only cranberry juice all night. It was quite tiring being out and, once home, I was glad to get to bed.

20th March 2005 - Sunday


Woke up at midday. Not much planned for today.

Had a really big breakfast, although it was lunchtime!

A few visitors came over to my house and I chatted the afternoon away while watching the Formula 1.

Didn't get much done at all today.

I felt tired in the evening but my mind would not rest. I am constantly going over what is to become of me, despite trying to suppress the thoughts of the upcoming Tuesday meeting and my scan results. Believe me I have thought of every eventuality; if you've been following my story closely then I'm sure you can imagine what thoughts I've been having and how worrying they are. I'm very nervous about the thought of having operations, the easiest of which will be the orchiectomy I have to undergo, the rest, if any, are my biggest concern.

21st March 2005 - Monday


Woke up at 09:00 today, I must stay awake in order to get to bed early tonight in preparation for my Tuesday meeting. Breakfast out of the way, I am going to do a few chores around the house and then set off to take my Mum to an important meeting for her at the local hospital.

Hospital, hospital, hospital, my life at the moment seems to revolve around hospital and will do for sometime to come!

My Mum's test results have also been playing on my mind for quite some time. Thankfully all she needs is some dentistry work, nothing more than that. Phew.

Now it's down to me to get a good result!

After a little potter around at some shops with my Mum, I took her home and then made for home myself. Not much time left before I find out now. I wonder if I will be able to get to sleep tonight!?

Two helpings of dinner and I was soon tired out enough to get to bed. Eventually I got to sleep at around 01:00.

22nd March 2005 - Tuesday


Woke up at about 07:00 this morning. Anxiety about today's meeting no doubt! I felt rested enough so was up and about by 09.00.

Had a big breakfast cooked for me this morning.

I'm 33 and 7 months old today!

Ken arrived back after being away for a few weeks and we all set off for the hospital.

Arrived in good time for my meeting and was soon seen to by my Doctor and Registrar. They had examined my CT scan results and I prepared myself nervously for the results.

I will try to explain what was said to me as best I can, I've just got home and am typing this whilst the news sinks in and I can understand and accept it all myself.

During the course of my treatment for Testicular Cancer, the chemotherapy has had an outstanding effect on what, by all accounts, was quite a serious and large spread of cancer. My blood results (tumour markers) have shown an outstanding response to the treatment, with the cancer showing no signs of recovery for over a month now.

However, I still have tumours showing in my lungs and abdomen. A few very small tumour scars, along with two sizeable tumours in my lungs and one tumour almost 2cm in size on my abdomen. Without an operation, there is no way of telling if any of these tumours, or any tiny part of them, are still alive. All the signs so far show that they have died and may just be shrinking slowly over time as my body repairs itself.

So without an operation the gamble is to wait and find out if they are still alive. This could take some time and could mean that, if they are alive, they would mutate into another form of cancer which could put me at further risk in the future.

With an operation, the tumour would be entirely removed and unable to mutate or grow even if it was still alive.

Bearing in mind that these tumours in my body could just be dead tissue which would fade over time.

The conclusion is that the obvious and most important operation is to remove the testicle that started all of this off; that is a dead cert and relatively easy operation, which will be scheduled soon.

The outstanding decision is the slightly more serious operation on my abdomen to remove the 2cm tumour, a decision that will be taken tomorrow by my specialist Doctor along with another specialist Doctor, a Surgeon and a Radiologist.

As I understand it, my Doctor likes to err on the side of caution and may decide to wait and see if the abdomen tumour shrinks any more before any surgery is required. However, the others in the team will most likely favour the operation being carried out as soon as possible, along with the testicle removal.

At this stage I think there is a 60-70% that the abdomen operation will be carried out along with the testicle removal. Tomorrow, I will know for sure after the experts have had their patient meeting at the hospital.

Operations on lungs are a more serious issue and the general practice is to let them be and keep a close eye on anything that remains. My lungs have shown a massive improvement and, like the abdomen, the hope is that the tumours showing are dead tissue and will recover over time. The likelihood is that I will not be having an operation on my lungs; instead they will be monitored over time with more X-rays and CT scans. If, after some time, they have not improved then my lungs will have to be operated on. This may change!

I went to the hospital today for some news and, on a scale of 1 to 10, 1 being brilliant news, I probably came away with a 6.

I will hear more tomorrow, but the general picture has been explained to me and my interpretation of it is above.

I had a blood test after the meeting and await the results of this over the next few days. This and future blood tests are mainly to monitor my general health and recovery from chemotherapy, along with another tumour test to monitor the status of my cancer (currently non active or dead).

How do I feel? Physically... quite good. Mentally... in shock (a little), nervous, externally; quiet and thinking.

I have a choice and can make decisions about my operations. Given the dilemma, I think that it is best to follow the advice of the experts in charge. I really feel that I cannot make any decisions in isolation regarding my treatment as they are based on odds and are quite simply a gamble!

Managed to get over to my Mum's in the evening to explain face to face what I have learned so far.

Home for dinner and I was fast asleep by 01:00. Tomorrow's another day!

23rd March 2005 - Wednesday


Woke up early today, just one thing on my mind. My first thoughts as I opened my eyes were about my operations and how much of this cancer is actually dead inside my body. I just have no way of knowing. However, all the signs so far (including medical) suggest that it is.

I'm beginning to get very scared, I could really cry at times and am probably crying inside already. My demeanour has changed somewhat, from being jovial to quiet contemplation. I'm certainly not in a chatty mood at the moment.

The registrar at the hospital had a bit of trouble getting hold of me today, a mix up with my actual contact telephone number. Before finally getting hold of me, the hospital phoned my Mum and then a friend of mine, which worried them somewhat. The hospital isn't allowed to discuss anything with anyone but the patient, so calling someone else and saying "it's the hospital, we need to speak to Tim" can easily be taken the wrong way.

My bloods have recovered almost to normal, which is a very good sign that my body is getting better quickly from the chemotherapy. I'm a healthy young man as the doctors put it (apart from the obvious).

The news is that my surgeon has decided to also operate on the abdomen and wishes to discuss this with me before he goes away for a week tomorrow. The hospital simply wanted me to get in for a meeting with him this afternoon to discuss my imminent surgery (within the next 2 weeks). Hence the urgency with which they were trying to get hold of me.

Once I arrived at the hospital, I was soon seen by not only my surgeon but also my doctor, the registrar and the Macmillan nurse. Everyone at hand for me and my questions. The surgeon came across very confident about the operation and explained to me in detail how it would be carried out.

Dr Tim Christmas' reputation precedes him (he's my surgeon). These particular operations are certainly not unfamiliar to him. I have been told that people are sent to him from all over the country and sometimes all over the world. It appears that Charing Cross Hospital and its patients are very lucky to benefit from his expertise.

A somewhat comforting thought.

I am still nervous about my pending operation; who wouldn't be? I will end up with a sizeable scar in my groin and an even more sizeable scar down the centre of my stomach. There will be before and after pictures on here for all to see (no rude bits, I'm afraid)!

The date of my operation is now posted in the schedules area of this website. They have not been confirmed to me in writing as yet, so may be subject to change.

In the mean time, I just have to keep myself busy. My blood health and results have returned to normal now, this was confirmed to me not only on the phone earlier but also in the meeting. I am lucky to have my health and be able to recover quite quickly by normal standards. Not so much in terms of fitness but general health I am in pretty damn good shape apart from the obvious!

On the way home I was still trying to soak up all that I had been told, hopefully I will be able to expand on it more once I have digested all the information myself.

Very bad traffic on the way home; went to Sainsbury's to top up on some food shopping.

After I had my dinner, I popped over to spend the latter part of the evening with my Mum to expand further on my call to her earlier in the day. As you can imagine, each and every time I get more news I am immediately passing it on to my Mum & Dad by phone.

Once home, I finally got to sleep at about 2am.

24th March 2005 - Thursday


Woke up at 11am. A good deep sleep.

Soon after I had eaten breakfast the registrar called to confirm the last part of my blood results. My HCG tumour marker still remains at below 2 or untraceable. So good news that potentially none of this cancer showing inside me is alive and what isn't going to be removed will hopefully disappear over time.

A question that I have been meaning to ask for some time about my cancer was answered today. As I have shown particularly good recovery so far, does this mean that my cancer will also recover too? After all, the cancer was created in my body, I did wonder if it would recover as well as my body has. The answer is no. Generally in healthier people the battle is hard but the clear up rate is much better than with people who have poor health when they are admitted with cancer. Another lesson, look after yourselves and make sure you are in good health!

Keeping myself busy today, trying not to think about things too much. I have a bit of time away from hospital now before any further tests are carried out.

Got to bed quite late again. I must get back in to a normal sleeping pattern.

25th March 2005 - Friday


Easter weekend! Woke up feeling good. Nice to see the sun shining. Not much to report. I'm feeling in good health and eating well.

Apart from the obvious stress of my pending operations I'm going to continue to lead as normal a life as possible and feasible under the current circumstances.

I'll not bore you with mundane daily reports of my movements!

If anything happens, I'll be back here to tell. Otherwise see you all again on the 5th April 2005.

This is my personal experience, not medical advice.
