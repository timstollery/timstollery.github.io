How it began

My story

The symptoms, the questions and the events that led to my diagnosis in December 2004.

Personal diaryAbout 4 min read

On the 9th of December 2004, I was diagnosed with Stage IIIb Testicular Cancer.

On the 10th December 2004, amongst obvious discussions about my forthcoming and required treatment, I was told that if no treatment were to be given, then I would have...

"just less than a year to live, possibly only months"

This website covers my journey throughout the fight with my Cancer, not only my initial suspicions but my day to day battle both in hospital and out, it covers the complete story.

If you're in doubt about anything that might be wrong with you, don't hang about, make sure you get seen to and seen to properly, not once but time and time again until you are happy with the result. As you will see from my experience, things can be easily missed in our busy lives...

The Chemotherapy treatment I am to receive will most likely drain my energy, so I'm using this as an outlet for you all to find out how I am and save me telling the story a hundred times, I hope you understand. Throughout the site I have links referencing particular sites to explain things further, these will be underlined.

Thank you for all of your support and kind wishes, it is much appreciated.

The story begins here...

June 2003

After having my suspicions of a slight irregularity down in my nether regions I decided to pay a visit to my GP for a physical examination, my GP being quite dismissive claimed that a physical examination would not be necessary and instead lifted my arms and legs and asked if I had any pain anywhere???

I am not a doctor but surely after asking for a physical examination I should have at least been given one and after my peculiarly fashioned GP examination I was left with no answer to my original suspicions. So I pondered on this for 2 weeks and decided to go to my local walk in GUM Clinic (Genito-Urinary Medicine) to request a physical examination, which I was promptly given. The lady that examined me deliberated for a bit and finally said all felt fine and my initial fears were laid to rest and I worried no more.

January/February 2004


A slight swelling in the right hand side of my nether regions had occurred and I thought that I had simply knocked myself as it wasn't that painful or uncomfortable, after a week and a bit it had subsided and no difference in shape or size was noticed than from before. I did wonder if I should go for another checkup to be sure and either became too busy or I simply wasn't that concerned as my fears of a problem had previously been dispelled.

October 18th - 22nd 2004


I had a weird pain in my back on the left had side around my rib cage, almost like I had pulled a muscle it went away after a couple of days and I really thought nothing of it.

Late October 2004 through to the end of November 2004

I slowly developed a cough, dry but irritable and producing no flem. A few more of the strange pulled muscle feelings popped up around the front and back of my ribcage both on the left and right which were a little painful at times in terms of general movement and coughing/breathing. Again they subsided but I certainly thought something was up. The most noticeable symptom of illness was an intense sweating at night, so much so that from the base of my head to the bottom of my back both front and back would literally leak like a tap and soak my sheets so much so I could not sleep on that side of the bed anymore.

November 29th 2004 - Monday


I decided that a visit to the GP was needed for at least some antibiotics to attack what I thought was a nasty infection or flu that needed to be cleared. I was seen by the same dismissive GP and prescribed over 2000mg of antibiotics to take a day. I asked as I left that I thought perhaps a chest Xray would be needed if the antibiotics did not work and was told very quickly that it "might be considered" if there was no change. I went straight to the chemist that same afternoon.

The following 7 days I took my antibiotics religiously, ate extra healthily and drank no alcohol to get over what I thought was a nasty flu.

That's everything leading up to my diagnosis. Continue with the diary to read about my treatment and recovery.

This is my personal experience, not medical advice.
