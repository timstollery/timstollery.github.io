The diary

August 2005

Follow-up tests and raising awareness

Personal diaryAbout 4 min read

5th August 2005 - Friday


Routine blood test and meeting with my Consultant today at Charing Cross Hospital.

Everything looking good so far, my markers remain at normal and full blood count results now fall within the normal values although still at the bottom end of the spectrum.

Physical examination carried out, all good.

After the meeting I was sent for an additional blood test to check my testosterone levels, this is a new one for me. I told the Phlebotomist to be careful with the tube of blood he took as it may blow the testing machine up if they are not careful. I haven't noticed any of the low testosterone symptoms as yet, if anything I'm worse than before!

Have you noticed I don't mention the endless needle worries anymore, I have mentally got used to them and the sites used for taking blood from me have become immune to needle pricks. Either that or I haven't had a bad experience recently!!

9th August 2005 - Tuesday


8 months since initial diagnosis today. It passed without me thinking of it at all, I rarely think of the diagnosis anniversaries anymore.

Talking of anniversaries I'm going to make it to another birthday very soon, on the 22nd August 2005 I will be another year older. In the past -like many of you out there I'm sure- I didn't like to think of getting another year older, but now at each birthday I'll be grateful I've been given the opportunity to live another year! Let's face it, luckily I'm a survivor and hopefully many, many more birthdays will now pass me by!

Strangely, on this day today, fell a talk on Testicular Cancer at an Army base not too far from where I live. Given by the inimitable checkemlads.com crew, Mick Riley and Phil Morris (new pictures in my gallery) to a large audience of first year recruits, a perfect target audience. I was introduced and applauded for all that I had been through, something I'm sure no soldier however 'hard' would like to confront.

I've also committed myself to a tandem sky dive with an Army team, on the 2nd of September 2005 with Mick and hopefully Phil. Jumping out of a plane at 13,000 feet was not something I had in mind to do in my life, but now I jump at every opportunity given to me (pardon the pun).

12th August 2005 - Friday


Blood test day today, popped in to my local hospital for it. Again the department was a ghost town, I caught the nurse in charge and was soon given my test.

Each time I go along to these tests at another hospital I have a special letter and dispatch sticker to hand over. This makes sure that my blood tubes reach the right place.

Only 10 days to go and I'll make it to being another year older!

26th August 2005 - Friday


Blood test day today, popped in to my local hospital for it. This time the department was full with people. I had to wait an hour before I was seen and tested, even then the nurse (a REED temp) did not know what to do with my paperwork, I had to explain how to take my blood, how much to take and what to do with the letter and sticker that I had brought for him.

Quite easily the worst experience of a blood test I have had to date, short of dragging me around the room whilst holding a needle which was stuck in my arm, the test was very uncomfortable indeed and it took 2 attempts at getting anything out of me. I was glad to get out of there! Still suffering with a few of those lasting Chemotherapy symptoms, which could possibly go on for a long time to come.

Here's an update...

Tiredness (occasional)
Peripheral Neuropathy (less apparent)
Tinnitus (apparent at all times, got used to it)
Tiger stripes on skin (still apparent, should fade with time)
Lung twinges/pains (occasional) I have noticed that on the day after having had a few too many alcoholic beverages, the above symptoms are far more apparent. Think I'll go steady on the juice for a while!

My birthday passed relatively quietly, I had many well wishers and a few cards/presents to boot. All in all and in my case especially I suppose I kind of hoped for a big surprise on the auspicious day, making it another year alive was most certainly the celebration. Perhaps I'll have to pull my finger out and organise my 'Ball'. Still, I'm running hard away from a 6-8 month hole in my finances, which is catching up with me and hurting bad.

This is my personal experience, not medical advice.
