The diary

June 2005

A milestone and a new chapter

Personal diaryAbout 8 min read

2nd June 2005 - Thursday


I had been given the news by phone yesterday but essentially today was the final hurdle as far as my Cancer was concerned. Very nearly 6 months to the day from my diagnosis, which was on the 9th December 2004. This Cancer had been inside my body and spreading for quite sometime before that, there is no knowing exactly for how long either. With my symptoms and lead up in mind, possibly up to almost 2 years before I was diagnosed!!

I set off for the Hospital a little behind time today and even though it's a school holiday this week the traffic was horrendous. It seems that everyone visiting Clinic 8 today had suffered the same traffic fate as me, the place was empty.

Luckily I was seen to very swiftly and my bloods (Full Blood Count, U&E's & Tumour Markers) were taken almost immediately as I walked through the door.

It wasn't long before I was seen to by my Consultant Dr. Savage.

We went over my recent CT Scans and compared them to the previous ones taken almost 2 months earlier. A marked improvement was obvious and my lungs appear to be clearing up, although it's a slow process, it's exactly the progress that I could have hoped and wished for...

EXCELLENT !!

All that remains now are regular monthly blood tests, physical examinations and alternate chest X-Ray's which will become more and more spaced out as the years go by... yes years! I will be blood tested and checked up for the rest of my life, hopefully in about 5 years from now I will be given the 'ALL CLEAR' and then further blood tests will be taken annually for the rest of my life.

I'm off the production line now and handed over to quality control!

Amongst discussions about side effects that I have been suffering from, I reported a creaking sensation that I've recently noticed in my lower back, hips and legs. I haven't had any pain or discomfort, just this weird sensation like a creaking. My Doctor seemed to be a little worried about this and said it may be something of nothing, certainly not related to Cancer, more a side effect of chemotherapy and it would be best for me to go and have a Pelvis X-Ray immediately. The results of which would be discussed at the next patient meeting when the Doctors get together (most likely this coming Monday).

I'm not too worried and hope the sensation will pass given time. Hold that thought!

Also whilst looking over my CT Scans my Doctor spotted some 'clips' that showed up. It did make me wonder but his mention of them was very cursory.

We said our good-byes and off I went for my Pelvis X-Ray. Again I was seen to very quickly and was X-Rayed within minutes of arriving at the reception. I asked very nicely if I could see the final images on screen, which the Radiographer kindly agreed to. Again there was mention of these 'clips' and that's not what the X-Ray was for!

Now it was getting me thinking, the Radiographer couldn't really shed any light on it for me so I set off to find out some more.

Essentially and at first I was quite mad/worried as nobody I asked seemed to know anything. There I was, left thinking that some foreign objects had been left in my body for no apparent reason and further surgery may be required to remove them, what do I know I'm not a surgical expert!

At no point do I remember being told that metal 'clips' would be left inside me and now it has got me wondering how many of them there are, where they all are and exactly why are they there.

I haven't really been given a detailed answer as yet but it seems common practise to have these clips left inside you after some surgical procedures. I would have loved to have been told this before hand, wouldn't you?

Surgery is scary enough being out of your control. I think it would be nice to be put in the picture much more before you are cut open, especially when it is planned surgery!

Hmmm, not exactly sure what I am going to do about this but I need to find out more to put my mind at rest.

That's all I can think of for now. I'll be back in a few days time to have a natter about the following...

Note to watch TV... 21.00 on the 9th June 2005 on CH4 UK.

There is a documentary called "Bollocks to Cancer", I'll be very interested to see how close this documentary depicts itself to my individual experience.

Apart from the above I am in good health and rapidly returning to full strength. I've been out and about filling my lungs with fresh air and eating well, watch out I could be knocking on your door soon for dinner!!

For those of you who are avid readers out there, you'll have made it this far with me and I thank you tremendously for that. Unless anything exciting happens now, entries will be very few and far between, although I do plan to keep the site well updated for the foreseeable future. There are new reference features coming to the site very soon and a few more pictures to boot.

I am also going to be planning a party... aptly named "Tim's Ball" primarily for us all to celebrate the life we have been granted however difficult at times, please email if your interested in coming along! I haven't come up with a venue or any dates yet, I'm thinking August 2005 sometime to coincide with my birthday!

7th June 2005 - Tuesday


Currently I'm attending a Cancer Support Group and my second meeting was this morning. Essentially we talk about our experiences with Cancer and how we and all around us have/are dealing with it.

Mostly what is discussed is pretty private stuff and individual to that sufferer, most of what I say is already here for you all to see! One of the subjects that was brought up today was the constant hurdles that face you during treatment, it's just one thing after another...

Which brings me to some new information I have been given today. My recent Pelvis X-Ray results are in. All appears fine so far but my Doctors (whatever they are looking for) have been advised by the Radiologist that an MRI scan would be more suitable.

I am now awaiting my appointment to come through the post, apparently it will be quite quick and I will have a follow up meeting to discuss the results.

Now I'm a little nervous as to what this could be.

Still no concrete information on the 'clips' that are inside me. I have had some very comforting emails from fellow patients who also have them so it's a little less worrying. Still I would like to know how many of them there are, where they all are and exactly why are they there! After all it sounds very much like they are there to stay and although I don't think I can notice them it would be nice to know exactly what I'm carrying about inside my body!

Ok I'm going to creak off in to life as best as I can make it, back on Thursday.

9th June 2005 - Thursday


23.30... Today is 6 months to the day since I was diagnosed with my Cancer, a disease that had spread throughout my body unbeknown to me apart from a niggling cough.

This time on the 9th of December 2004 I was lying in a medical assessment unit in West Middlesex Hospital, riddled with disease. My friends had gone home and I was left to wonder, alone in my room, what the future would hold for me.

So, half a year later, I have been through 4 cycles of extensive BEP chemotherapy followed by major surgery and today I'm in remission from a Cancer that almost killed me. My story has been told and you've all been here to share it with me.

Strangely on this anniversary, a documentary was aired this evening called "Bollocks to Cancer". A documentary about a young Testicular Cancer sufferer and survivor. Although a different person and a short production to pack a lot of experience in to, it really brought back some vivid memories and experiences for me.

It certainly did a good job of portraying what it's like to lie in hospital on BEP chemotherapy, the thought processes and feelings. My treatment was the exactly the same, I had an extra cycle. The wards were similar in layout although I did not have other Testicular Cancer sufferers around me. At the beginning, in one of my earlier male ward stays, all five of my fellow patients died. That was tough.

I have a different perspective on life now and have learnt a great lesson in mortality. A gift if you like, but not exactly the best way to receive it. It's difficult to describe the feeling, you have to experience it in one way or another for yourself, something which I do not wish on anyone.

My website lives on, as I do. I will be back soon with more information on further scans and tests that I am to have.

This is my personal experience, not medical advice.
