The diary

December 2004

The diagnosis and first chemotherapy

Personal diaryAbout 31 min read

6th December 2004 - Monday


Antibiotics course finished and no real change noticed.

8th December 2004 - Wednesday


Growing concern, booked another appointment with a different GP at my surgery for Friday 10th at 5pm.

9th December 2004 - Thursday


After yet another sweaty night (these had become such a regular occurrence now over the past few weeks) I awoke with what felt like yet another pulled muscle pain in my ribcage area, this time around on my back on the left hand side.

I was becoming increasingly fed up with my symptoms and decided that the GP would only prolong any further treatment I may need, and a visit to the A&E for a routine check-up and X-ray would be a better option. So, without further delay or cancelling my GP appointment, I paid a visit to West Middlesex Hospital's brand new A&E department.

I waited just under an hour at A&E to see someone, a brilliant waiting time for an NHS A&E.

Some routine checks were carried out and a cautionary X-ray was ordered immediately without any real concerns or suspicion attached.

I was sent for an X-ray some 20 minutes later. The radiographer asked after zapping me if I had ever had a chest X-ray before, I noticed an air of concern come over her face and I answered candidly "no".

Upon my return to A&E I was given a blood test. I noticed several doctors looking at a computer screen and then looking over at me in amazement. Now I was really starting to sense something wasn't quite right, but kept my cool.

A doctor approached me and led me into a curtained cubicle. He pulled the curtains closed after our entrance; the look on his face told me he had bad news before a single word had passed his lips. "We've found some dark patches on your X-ray" he said cautiously. I asked for a straight explanation and, after an initial shock and a cup of water, the facts started to sink in.

It was explained that I had up to 8 or 9 lesions the size of 20p pieces on each of my lungs and the chances of it being an infection was 20% versus the 80% chance of it being CANCER!!!

I was admitted to West Middlesex Hospital that evening for an overnight stay in the Medical Assessment Unit (MAU), a CT scan and Ultrasound scan scheduled for the morning.

10th December 2004 - Friday


The morning came and my fears worsened as a plethora of doctors entered my room and each and every one of them looked as worried as the doctor the night before. What was dawning on me was that this was something quite serious. More bad news was given to the effect that if it was a Cancer, as they now suspected, then what was showing on the chest X-ray was not its primary source and was likely to have started somewhere lower in my body, eventually reaching my lungs.

The CT scan results confirmed that the Cancer was also in my para-aortic lymph nodes located by my abdomen, and gave a much clearer picture of my lungs with depth and clarity to confirm its spread.

A further Ultrasound scan on my testicles also confirmed that the most likely cause and primary location of the Cancer was in my right testicle.

Something I had suspected some 18 months back!

I was transferred to Charing Cross Hospital by wheelchair in an ambulance as an emergency case (no blue lights, though!).

All this time, I still looked as healthy on the outside as I had before, a young man with a nasty cough and absolutely no other symptoms whatsoever from any single part of my body.

I called my GP's surgery to inform them that I wouldn't be turning up at 5pm!

Once at Charing Cross, I was wheeled in my chair to the 6th floor. There was an immediate difference in newness between West Mid and Charing Cross but comfort was coming from the fact that I was now going to be treated in a Hospital that had years of experience and expertise - over and above any of the other hospitals in the area.

I waited a while to be seen by my consultant and, to put a 40 minute conversation very briefly, I was told that my prognosis was good, a 4 out of 5 chance of a complete and full recovery from Testicular Cancer. Some 4 months of Chemotherapy in and out of hospital and getting ill to get better lay in wait for me, but it's my only choice.

I had to ask a final, but obvious, hypothetical question...

"As I feel fine and could walk out of here now, what if I didn't want any treatment whatsoever?"

"You'd have less than a year to live"

So that really kicks the facts home I guess, Chemotherapy treatment or die, a simple one to work out!!! So, whatever it takes, however long, and whatever side effects I have to put up with, they pale into insignificance compared to the alternative.

A room was allocated to me and I decided to stay in hospital to try and keep the room as my Chemotherapy treatment would start the following week. Although the consultant was happy for me to retire home until the following week, I felt more comfortable being in this environment, considering the news I was still trying to take in and believe.

11th December 2004 - Saturday


I had to return home to refill my Mum's Parkinson's medication schedule to cover her whilst I was in hospital. Leaving the hospital was all cleared with the powers that be, so I had a shower and a good freshen up and left for Hounslow in order to be back to Hospital for dinner at 6pm. Being out of Hospital after the news I had been given was a weird experience, almost like I shouldn't be out of my comfort zone in case anything was to happen to me, so I felt a little anxious to say the least. Although sorting my Mum out was a priority, I now also had my own welfare to be concerned about.

Upon returning to hospital at 6pm I discovered that dinner was served at 5pm so my best friend Ken Clark who, so far, has been through this whole daunting experience with me, popped over to Pizza Express which I can see from my window, to pick up some dinner.

12th December 2004 - Sunday


The day passed with relative calmness although anticipation was building for my Sperm Banking, EDTA and MRI tests over the next couple of days.

13th December 2004 - Monday


A visit to Hammersmith Hospital over lunchtime for some Banking (I'm not going into detail here!) and discussions about how, when and who I consent to using it in the future. All consents are far from set in stone and can be changed as and when needed, but if something happened to me and I hadn't changed them, the laws surrounding consents can be very strict and would stick to the last consent I had given.

14th December 2004 - Tuesday


Down to the 1st floor for my EDTA test at 11am which requires me to be back at 2 hour intervals, 1pm and 3pm which gave me a brief chance to get over to Hammersmith between 1pm and 3pm for another session of Banking! Increasing my future chances of having a family is a nice insurance policy to add to but on the scale of things my recovery comes first.

Back to the ward for my dinner at 5pm then a short wait for my first round of Chemotherapy, this was all explained to me in full before treatment began. Due to the lateness of its start, and the schedule of drugs they feed me intravenously, it made for a disturbing night with visits to the loo dragging a machine along with me and getting out of bed to be weighed in the early hours.

15th December 2004 - Wednesday


Chemotherapy didn't finish on time this morning today, so I had to break away from it to rush down for my MRI scan. I was fast asleep and luckily Ken arrived early to wake me and get me down to the 1st floor for the scan, something the nurses should have done!!

The MRI was pretty straight forward, a small injection in my arm and into the scanner I slid. I wasn't afraid; it was a little noisy and I stayed as still as possible so the resulting image would be as clear as it could be.

Back to the ward for the final blast of Chemotherapy and then I was free for the day. After one night of Chemotherapy I didn't feel much different.

The night came quickly and I was soon connected up to the Chemotherapy drip again. They were very busy on my ward and when it came to changing my dosage bag I was left for quite some time with no through flow, this caused quite a bit of discomfort in my hand and a burning sensation.

Another disturbing night with dosage changes, wee'ing and weighing! I did sleep better though, probably fatigued from the night before.

16th December 2004 - Thursday


Morning came and the Chemotherapy treatment again finished a little late. Today I was to be free of any lines or taps in my hand, just another blood test, that's all.

Again I was feeling fine and ate everything that was put in front of me.

I got a look at my X-ray and MRI scans today. Thankfully the MRI was clear - still to be officially confirmed by my consultant - and the X-ray was an obvious indication that this disease was inside me and meaning business.

The day went swiftly, although I tired towards the end, I still found the energy to set up Milton's (my ward buddy) new iMac G5, which gave him great pleasure. He's now even busier with his crusade to offer a positive approach to dealing with serious diseases like ours.

17th December 2004 - Friday


Slept well, albeit a little sweaty during the night.

Today a brief chat about my test results with my consultant confirmed that the MRI test was clear, the blood test clear, the kidney test (EDTA) was good and all in order. My X-ray was alarming but the CT showed I still have plenty of lung space to breathe with comfortably and fight this disease.

Discharge day today, home for the weekend. A bit of a wait around for my medication to take home and I was off the ward and home in no time, a surreal experience since going in to A&E over a week ago!

18th December 2004 - Saturday


Slept well in my own bed last night, felt sweaty again during the night.

Ate breakfast, ate lunch, went to see my Mum briefly then back home for dinner.

I was asked by the doctors to monitor my temperature, matched with how I am feeling whilst at home. I didn't feel any different and my temperature was reasonable at all times (normal should be 36.5 C).

19th December 2004 - Sunday


Slept a bit more awkwardly, woke up wide eyed, sweating, with a slight pain in my left lung during the night and couldn't sleep again for a bit for thinking about all that has gone on so far. Bounced myself off the walls in the dark and found the loo, back to bed and I was asleep in no time.

This morning I'm OK, had a bit of a lie in (what's new!). The Sun is shining brightly out there today.

Relaxed all day at home, away from all the Xmas bustle outside that I keep hearing about from my friends that have phoned or visited.

Back to hospital tomorrow. I'll be in for a while for my first phase of full blown BEP Chemotherapy. Getting a bit nervous.

20th December 2004 - Monday


Slept really well although doing the usual sweating thing.

It's hospital day today, not so daunting as I think the reason I'm going in today is just for NHS bed logistics, either that or 2 days away is all they are going to give me, considering the condition inside my body.

I almost don't want to go back to hospital, but know that I have no choice.

Back at hospital now, a new Ward (6 West, F7). Nervous at first, but now settled, oh and of course they had to give me a blood test didn't they!!

Big day tomorrow, as far as I know. See you then.

21st December 2004 - Tuesday


Slept well although didn't go to bed until 3am as I was chilling out with Milton. This new ward I'm on doesn't even bring you breakfast, cheek!

My 2 registrars (Dr's) came round to see me (they are under supervision by my consultant, the only one from whom I will take medical orders/instructions). They seemed pretty upbeat and happy to see me, and my initial blood results from yesterday all looked good. It seems my body is as ready as it's going to be for this Chemotherapy and they are preparing to give it to me this afternoon. They reiterated that it would be a much bigger dosage than I was given last week. Let's hope I can tough the effects out, I'm going to have a bloody good go.

Breakfast done and they are taking my blood again, although the experience is becoming easier as my new Dr's assistant/phlebotomist (blood sucker), Sylvia, is a master at taking blood and does a sterling job with me - 'the big baby' when it comes to needles.

Another blood test this afternoon, what are these people after? My life and soul? I must admit I might be getting used to these now, either that or my mind and body has given up worrying about it so much.

Chemotherapy time is fast approaching and the nasty experience of having a Cannula fitted to my arm has to be overcome first.

Well it took them 3 goes to get the big Cannula needle in my arm; I hated this experience. Now I'm connected up and have a full 15 hours of intravenous drip Chemotherapy ahead, this will take me well into the morning tomorrow.

Had a few visitors tonight, been sitting in the TV room relaxing and chatting. Chemotherapy still going strong and making me wee a lot!!

Off to bed soon, catch up with you all again tomorrow.

22nd December 2004 - Wednesday


Slept quite well considering 15 hours of Concorde fuel has been pumped in to me (thanks Rog). I'm awake and feeling fine, just waiting to be disconnected so I can go for yet another pee and get myself some breakfast.

Breaking news... I'm potentially moving wards today - I pulled some strings ;) - back to South ward where I started all this. I think they miss me!! I'm 33 years and 4 months old today, exactly. Yup, it makes you think about your age too!

Getting some great inspirational emails from you all, even from people I have never met, from the furthest corners of the earth, saying it's making them go get themselves checked out. What a surprise that my site is not only being used by those of you that wish to keep up to date with me, but also by people who may benefit from my experience.

Moved wards now, back to the original place where everyone knows me. Although I might be annoying at times, I think they like me!

Back on the Chemotherapy now, another 9 hours dragging an IV machine about, but feeling fine. Hearing more and more of people's Cancer stories is quite frightening, especially people who have a similar Cancer to myself. My only consolation is that EVERYBODY IS DIFFERENT and I have to keep saying that to myself to stay positive. Good test results are a good indication too, I'm chasing after some blood test results at the moment, tell you all about it later...

The Consultant, with both his Registrars, found me loitering on the ward. They asked if I could follow them in to a quiet room (you know the feeling I was getting now, one of anticipation and worry). Sat me down and asked all about this website, which they'll be looking at soon. Also, mentioning the results of my HCG blood test almost in passing, a test they will be taking regularly throughout my treatment cycles. The HCG markers in my blood are an indication of my Cancer and its current level. When I first came in to the Charing Cross Hospital before any treatment, the reading was 3,900. It has now lowered itself to 3,300 which suggests an improvement and my body's positive reaction to the treatment so far. Although the Consultant would have been equally happy if these figures had mildly risen, although at a slow rate. No parties yet, however much it is good news; these levels can go up as well as down.

Originally I was told that 80 to 90 percent of the Cancer in my body should be an easy conquest, it's the last 10 percent that is the hard part to eradicate. So, all in all, so far we are heading in the right direction.

Ate double dinner tonight, one hospital meal and then a takeaway Pizza from Pizza Express across the road, appetite not gone yet, in-fact it's a little more furious at times.

Feeling a little more fatigued tonight, especially in the legs, possibly an indication of some Chemotherapy side effects. Still on the Chemotherapy now, should be finished by midnight and then I can get an unrestricted night's sleep.

23rd December 2004 - Thursday


Awoke early today at 6am to have my blood pressure checked etc. Usual routine but I awoke with a startle and nearly karate chopped (like Inspector Clueso) the nurse. I warned her to be a little kinder when waking me or her life might be on the line!

Got back to sleep for a bit, despite a lot of activity on my ward. Breakfast out the way now, I usually have 2 Weetabix with warm milk. Got my Christmas dinner menu today, hospital Christmas dinner whoopee! The doctors and nurses really pity you when then hear that you are in for the Xmas period, I'm so not bothered they cannot believe it. Honestly, when faced with something like this, nothing else should matter but your recovery (I was never really an Xmas person anyway, I'll save all my Xmas joy for the day when and if I'm given the chance to have my own family).

Third day of Chemotherapy today, another 9 hours connected to a drip machine. That should be around 2pm I guess so making the most of my unattached freedom is a must.

Ahh, I saw the blood test trolley come round just now and yes they wanted some... I must be getting used to these now as it went OK although I still didn't look!

The Registrars came through to have a quick word, nothing major just a friendly 'how you feeling' and I think they can see I'm doing just fine so far.

Lunch out the way, a quick bath, shave and dressed to impress. I'm on top form for another 9 hour round with the Chemotherapy IV machine.

Today marks 2 weeks since I was first diagnosed with Cancer. How about that?

I've just witnessed the inception of Milton's Xmas single which should be available on his site for download tomorrow or Xmas day. Please support him and go get yourself a copy from his site. He's a fine musician indeed.

Ken went out for Kebab Kid for us all tonight (another 4th meal, appetite is still good). I've really fancied one since being here and being not so far away from my favourite kebab shop (very unlike your traditional drunken kebab shop, this has been voted one of the best in London by Time Out). Regular takeaway food is not advisable while on Chemotherapy but I would say KK passes the tests! It certainly went down fast enough with a bag of chips to follow.

A friend is here taking pictures of Milton and I, for our web-sites (providing his Kebab with chillies stops repeating on him!); they'll be up on the website soon.

Only a few hours of Chemotherapy to go tonight; running a bit behind and should be finished tonight by 1am. I'm sure I'll be suitably tired by then.

Signing off for now... until tomorrow...

24th December 2004 - Friday (Xmas Eve)


Chemotherapy day 4 today, should start around 2pm'ish!

Didn't sleep brilliantly last night, not for want of it though! Bleeps and beeps from other patients' machines kept me in a light sleep and also a few visits to the loo. I keep mentioning my visits to the loo because it's actually a good sign that my kidneys are flushing correctly and quickly, especially with all this Chemotherapy I am having, it's all fluid given by IV and if I retained it all I'd probably drown!!

Breakfast all done, I had the usual. Just going to relax a bit and reply to a few more of the many supportive emails I've received from you all.

One of the nurses was humming "Here comes the Sun" by George Harrison whilst making my bed, a song close to my heart. It was like a little message to me as I've always come across it at emotional or special times in my life. I'm guessing she heard it in Milton's room earlier and now I cannot get it out of my head (I'm not really trying to either!).

I hope you enjoy the new images section (thanks Ken), I'll be adding to them frequently now, mainly throughout each phase of my Intense Chemotherapy. IF there's a change in the way I look over time, which there may well be, then I don't want you to become alarmed - it's all part of the healing process, I'm suitably informed (or at least the side effects of Chemotherapy). Mind you, for those of you that don't know me, I'm not that good looking anyway ;-) !!!

Had a visit from my consultant and one of his registrars again. They're still happy with my progress and will be taking another blood test today! I also have to have an injection whilst at home called a G-CSF which should help me stay healthy over and above any side effects of Chemotherapy. Can you see me injecting myself, I don't think so! Any volunteers, a nice nurse perhaps?

I've just completed a massive update in the Links section of my site for those of you that are interested to read more detail about some of the tests and medical names I have mentioned.

Blood test done, I'm definitely getting used to these or I have found the best nurse there is at doing them; I want to give her a hug.

Finally back on the Chemotherapy, it's been manic here this afternoon with patients being discharged and new patients arriving. They just haven't had time to get to me, so I had to go and beg them at the ward reception for my treatment to start. 4pm start time means I will not be free of the IV machine until just before 2am tonight, another late night!

Some of the patients around me are in quite a bad way, a permanent reminder that Cancer is a potential killer and not to be ignored in our lives. A vicar has visited the patient opposite me, it really opens your eyes when it comes to that.

Chemotherapy schedule gone a bit haywire tonight, I'm still waiting for my final 4 hour bag at 22.45 which means I will not finish until 3am now. Another disturbed night, I'll not be rested well for Santa. Couldn't resist a Pizza Express blow-out tonight so Ken went down to get the takeaway order for us all.

Definitely tiring for bed now and not looking forward to my noisy and delirious neighbour (a new addition to the ward), been seriously thinking about sleeping in the TV room under the Xmas tree!

I hope that tonight Santa brings you all you wished for.

25th December 2004 - Saturday (Xmas Day)


Merry Christmas to all of you. I don't expect many of you will be checking in on me today with all your presents and festivities. Not so much for me, as I'm doing just fine and Xmas doesn't bother me, but please spare a thought for those who are less fortunate than yourselves today. After all, Xmas is a time of giving, so a little kind thought wouldn't do you any harm! As I haven't had much time to organise Xmas cards for you all, Ken has kindly decorated my site for your Xmas enjoyment! What a cheapskate I am, one Xmas card for you all!!!

Well I did manage a couple of hours on the sofa in the TV room whilst my Chemotherapy drip finished which, as I suspected, finished around 3am, the bleeper alarm woke me up. This also gave ample time for some medication to take effect on the delirious and noisy man that had moved next to me in my ward. I had to ask the nurses to do something; although I did feel sorry for him, I needed my sleep time too!!

They've moved my noisy neighbour just now and the chap on the other side is not doing so well, in-fact he's just been sick so I'm putting off my breakfast until I get up to go get it, I'm having a cup of tea for now. This morning I feel rested but I hope to get an early night again tonight.

Waiting for my friend Ken to arrive before I open any of my presents, I expect something Xmas related will be going on in the ward today. Humbug!

Noisy neighbour is back. I'm going to have to spend the day away from my bed, not such a bad thing I guess.

My consultant popped round to say hello, although not dressed in a Santa outfit... he's very happy with my progress so far!

Xmas lunch out the way (in-fact they brought me two by mistake, the first one wasn't mine but by the time they realised I had eaten it and then they gave me another!). Chemotherapy is due to start again soon, it looks like it'll be another late one tonight. Milton has launched his alternative Christmas Single today "That's Christmas". Produced in 2.5 days from his hospital room whilst undergoing Chemotherapy, the (alternative) Christmas Single is all about what's crap about Christmas but great about people!

At last Chemotherapy has restarted at 15.50, which means approximately another 9 hours on before I'm finished tonight. The nurses bought me a gift of some shower scrub but no nurse to go along with it! The main present I really wanted for Xmas was my Chemotherapy; after all that's why I'm here, for treatment, let's get this whole thing in to focus again!

On the final 4 hour stint of my first major phase of Chemotherapy now, managed to get ahead of schedule, looks like I'll be finished by 2am. Hopefully they will have suitably calmed my neighbour so I can get to bed and get some decent shuteye.

Still eating more, I've enjoyed a Hamper full of goodies tonight that one of my friends kindly brought over for me. There's been talk of me being discharged tomorrow, we'll see what that brings, I really don't mind either way, it depends on how I feel and how a new set of blood test results look in the morning.

Sleep well with all your Xmas tummies!!

26th December 2004 - Sunday (Boxing Day)


So much for the sleep, although what I did manage to get was reasonable, managed the 2am bedtime and went off to sleep almost immediately, only to be woken be the moans and screams of the man next to me at around 6.45am. I pressed my nurse call button to get him some attention but I think that there is nothing more they can do for him, he is delirious, confused, rambling and most of all sounds scared that he will be left alone to die.

It may sound harsh but I cannot do anything for him apart from call the nurse, I have detached myself from the situation now and have taken up residence in the TV room to spend the rest of my early morning in the peace and quiet of my own company (and a little TV!). It hurts quite a bit when I see others suffering around me, mainly terminal in my section of the ward. I must stay positive about my own condition, so some isolation in this room by myself was my only option.

It's starting to get busy now out in the corridors, it'll be breakfast time soon.

The usual breakfast out the way and it turns out I did need some more sleep as I dozed off on the sofa in the TV room. Woke up about an hour later.

Just had a Blood Test, definitely easier to cope with but I'm still not watching! The result of which will determine both my requirement for the HCG injections and my current HCG counts.

Lunch approaching and I'm getting hungry, methinks it'll be quite a wait today before they give me their decision on my discharge and or injection schedule.

Discharged at 15.45 without the need for an immune boost (HCG) as my blood test results have shown that I tolerated the first phase of Chemotherapy very well. My body is still able to fight off infections by itself, although possibly mildly dampened, so I have to be careful of my exposure to germs etc.

So this ends phase 1 of my particular Chemotherapy regime. I'm not sure if the strength of the Chemotherapy in the following 3 major phases will be increased in strength (this entry is going to serve as a reminder to me to get this answer as soon as possible). I will need more power and strength to overcome greater doses.

At the beginning of each major phase, I will be X-rayed to have some visual results as to the increase, or more positively, and what I will be hoping for the most... the demise of the Cancer that is showing on my lungs.

Home for dinner and an undisturbed night's sleep.

27th December 2004 - Monday


Good morning! I managed 8 hours of good sleep, I don't want to overdo the lie in as I still need to stick to a regular eating regime, so I'm about to go have my Weetabix but this time at home!

It occurred to me last night that my consultant and doctors may be occasionally looking in on this diary, so not only is it acting as a means of communication for me and a means of awareness for others but also as a kind of Bio Monitor for my doctors, as they can just check in to see how I'm feeling!!

Well this morning I feel fine, apart from a little bit of a throat (probably from snoring after my comfortable sleep) and a snotty nose, can't explain that! I'll stick to my given tablet regime 1 Allopurinol 100mg in the morning along with 2 Domperidome 10mg 3 times a day. I also have a mouth wash to keep any nasty germs at bay, although my immune system result was good and I should be able fight off infection myself, I'll try not give it too much work to do. No feeling of sickness at all.

Wouldn't it be a good idea for temporarily discharged patients to wear a bio monitor to analyse statistics, the results of which would be available securely online to the doctors, and if anything went wildly wrong alarms would be raised etc. They put curfew monitors on prisoners these days, why not use similar technology? I'll shut up before I get carried away.

Been keeping myself busy all day; healthy eating and drinking very well indeed!

Tiring now, ready for another good night's sleep!

28th December 2004 - Tuesday


Another good night's sleep, although the 3 litres of water I drank during the course of yesterday had me up a few times! I didn't wake up until 11am this morning.

Anticipation is building for my hospital visit tomorrow. I have my Emla cream (which numbs an area of skin like a local anaesthetic) at the ready to make the Cannulation (don't look at that link if you are squeamish) experience a little easier for me.

Busy all day again, not eaten as well as other days, not through want of trying!!

Today I have noticed a slight noise in my ears, more of a very high pitched tone than a ringing (which could be a chemo side effect), I thought I heard it yesterday but ignored it, the same happened today so I'm letting you know! Changes are afoot inside me, there's a full scale war going on and I AM GOING TO WIN.

Not so sleepy tonight, may be the anticipation of tomorrow's events, plus I have to be up early in the morning, back to hospital regimes.

29th December 2004 - Wednesday


Not much sleep last night, managed about 5 hours and awoke with a terrible cramp in the calf muscle of my right leg. I've never suffered from cramp apart from in my feet on a couple of rare occasions.

Got to the Hospital on time and checked in with South 6 to find out where I am to go for my outpatient treatment. Outpatient Chemotherapy is given on the same floor in the East wing of Charing Cross Hospital.

Had my blood test taken, I didn't like this much today. My Chemotherapy has arrived in a special drug case and I'm due to start very soon.

I had the Emla cream applied to my right arm at the time of my blood test in readiness for the Cannulation which I am still not looking forward to.

Briefly spoke with the doctor and he's confirmed that the dosages of Chemotherapy are to stay the same throughout my treatment, so here's hoping that I can tolerate the rest of this as well as I have so far.

Well what can I say, the Cannulation experience was a revelation, I didn't feel a thing. Not wanting to speak too soon, I might feel a sting when the effect wears off, but by far that must be the lesser of the two evils. The Emla cream worked a miracle and will now in future be my saviour.

Chemotherapy started at 11am, that means I should be finished at 5pm tonight. They will not let me move from this ward so I am a prisoner for 6 hours! I can see me watching quite a few DVDs on the laptop over the next few months of my outpatient visits.

Ok I've watched Collateral with Tom Cruise on my laptop, it's over and I'm bored. Feeling a little run down like I'm catching a cold; this is to be expected I'm told.

Looking forward to going home soon and relaxing.

Once home, I started feeling a chill and slightly feverish. Checked my temperature and it was just within limits, a bit of cramp in my feet this time and I got myself off to sleep on the sofa for a couple of hours.

Couldn't eat my dinner tonight, just didn't feel hungry.

I've woken up now and feel a little better.
A good night's sleep is ahead.

30th December 2004 - Thursday


A solid sleep, interrupted by a few visits to the loo. I normally sleep right through, so I can only imagine this increased water works activity is due to the extra fluid I have taken on during Chemotherapy.

I've had a lazy morning, didn't get up until 10.30am and certainly feel a whole load better than I did last night. So far last night was the worst I have felt in a long while. No sickness, but lost my appetite completely and had a fever/chill.

Hopefully back on track today after a light breakfast and some orange juice.

I will definitely be taking it very easy today.

I've driven a few places in my car, mainly for shopping, the first time I've been out properly now for almost 3 weeks. I'm now rubbing my tummy ready for dinner!!

Eaten loads tonight, all healthy I might add! Not feeling too tired but will try to get my head down soon.

Goodnight everybody!

31st December 2004 - Friday


Good morning. I didn't sleep much, probably went off about 03.00 and I've woken up at 08.30, not through want of trying but I just wasn't that tired for some reason. I'll try to get a little snooze during today, but I'm not too good at daytime napping. I haven't mentioned my night time sweats for a while, but they are still ongoing, every single night, they are a better experience in my own bed over the hospital beds as they are water proofed underneath.

It's not contagious, just not very attractive! Besides there isn't any girlfriend in my life at the moment so I'm not bothering anyone!! I'm hoping as I get better inside the sweats will subside; the doctor did explain that it was due to increased hormone levels due to the Cancer. Did I tell you all that if I took a pregnancy test at the moment it would show up positive? Weird; I'm so tempted to try!!

Well it's the last day of a horrible year and a bit for me (see thoughts section). I hope to put it all to rest tonight and look forward to a thriving and healthy future for us all, starting tomorrow!!

I'm thinking of going out to celebrate the New Year tonight, strictly no alcohol but I don't see what harm one night out will do. I'm a little worried about my energy level and lungs breathing in a lot of smoke, it's why I popped in to a pub yesterday for a trial run. I just don't want to give my body too much extra work to do at the moment. Although so far I have been fine out, have plenty of energy and my breathing seems totally normal. As you can see, I am concerned about the idea, but think I will regret it if I don't go to my second home in Richmond (most of you know where I go, part of the furniture, it wouldn't be the same without me).

Ate a big breakfast and a hearty lunch, now in need of a snooze.

A few of my 'friends' have changed their minds with regard to the tentative plans they had made with me earlier this week and I was so looking forward to having a few buddies around me tonight to see the New Year in. Now I will make other plans for tonight, I might even just go to bed!
Cheers...

Managed to get out and enjoy my New Year! Although not with my usual gang I had a good time (I was glad of the company) and was happy to act as cabby, I know from many years first hand experience what a nightmare it can be to get home on New Year's Eve. Obviously I stuck to the strictly no alcohol regime I have put myself on.

Now I'm peckish, so will have a night cap (food) and retire for a nice long new year's sleep!!

This is my personal experience, not medical advice.
