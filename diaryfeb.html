The diary

February 2005

Continuing chemotherapy and its effects

Personal diaryAbout 30 min read

1st February 2005 - Tuesday


Very disturbed sleep last night, as I expected really. Still tired and groggy this morning, so I'm not up to much. Feeling hungry, so I should get up and go get some breakfast.

I'll only have a short break from chemotherapy today. Not the 'required' 24 hours between treatments, just 4 hours. If I work it this way, at least my body will be given a 'reasonable' amount of time to recover between treatments each day and I will get a relatively undisturbed sleep with the mornings to myself, free of the IV machine. In theory this sounds great, let's see if I can make it work this week. I'm only trying to make it better based on my previous rushed chemotherapy experience.

I managed some Weetabix and a brief visit over to Milton's ward.

The day went in a blur, had a quite a few visitors but was not feeling my best and spent most of the day in bed feeling poorly. I was sick a tiny bit, too.

Chemotherapy started at 5pm.

By the evening I'd had no lunch, no dinner and was still feeling sick and weak.

I fancied a bowl of mixed fruit and Ken kindly went in to the kitchen and prepared a load for me. This went down really well and I soon perked up. Watched a DVD (Paycheck) to while away the early hours while chemo finished and then got my head down for a reasonable sleep.

To give you an idea of how poorly I felt today, I hardly touched my computer and haven't updated my diary for some time.

2nd February 2005 - Wednesday


A good night's sleep, feeling a little better today although still a little dodgy. I'm not sure what it is, maybe sickness, maybe hunger, maybe fatigue, I cannot quite put my finger on it.

My Consultant paid me another brief visit today, not much to say really. I mentioned I was a little concerned about this news that I may have to have an operation on my lungs, but he kind of reassured me that in most cases they do not expect to carry this out and he pointed out again that I was only half way through my treatment.

Still it all has to be said 'just in case' and the abdomen operation was mentioned as well. I've just got to keep fighting mentally to get rid of this Cancer from my body and just have the one operation to remove the affected testicle.

Well, if I feel up to it later today, I'll update you all a bit more, until then...

Chemotherapy started at 15:00 today. All being well I should be finished around midnight.

Had an aromatherapy back massage this afternoon, a much looked forward to complimentary therapy which takes me away from the stresses and strains of hospital life.

Feeling a bit better, I can smell dinner and will try to get some of it inside me now. Managed most of my dinner.

Quite a few visitors came tonight which tired me out somewhat. Chemotherapy carried on into the early hours and the ward was noisy tonight so sleep will be difficult.

3rd February 2005 - Thursday


Woke after a very disturbed sleep. Managed to get up for some breakfast.

The rest of the day I spent in bed, feeling very nauseous. On occasion, mostly when I went in to the very 'unclean' and smelly patient toilet on my ward, this would cause me to heave and sometimes to be sick.

Couldn't face any lunch.

Chemotherapy started at around the same time as yesterday, possibly a little earlier in order for me to be free earlier for sleep. I wasn't aware of much, I had my good moments, mostly after being sick but it soon turned bad again and I was in and out of sleep all day.

This week so far has been much harder than previous weeks. A female friend has been to see me several times this week, the female company with a little added TLC has really made all the difference.

Couldn't face any dinner. Ken prepared another plate of fruit for me, which I ate most of and managed to digest before being sick again later on!

Developed a mild headache and was given 500mg of Paracetamol.

Got free of the chemotherapy drip at a reasonable hour and got my head down for as best a night's sleep as I could muster. During the night I woke feeling nauseous, I hadn't taken my anti sickness tablets from earlier, so stayed awake for a while until I felt like I could manage a sip of water and got the tablets inside me, 2 Domperidome and 1 Ciprofloxacin.

4th February 2005 - Friday


Woke up feeling a little better and managed a bowl of cornflakes. This feeling didn't last long and I retired to bed feeling much the same as the previous day.

Couldn't face lunch.

The day went slowly, chemotherapy started late although I had agreed to have it started earlier.

Couldn't face dinner.

Finally got away for home at 22.00. Once home I had a bath and went to bed!

5th February 2005 - Saturday


Woke up after a long and undisturbed sleep. Felt much better than any of the mornings last week.

Managed some breakfast and took my correct medication, 1 Granisetron and 2 Domperidome tablets.

A friend popped over and I decided (after a little convincing) to go out locally for a Pizza Express. Ken, my friend and I hit the Hampton Court Pizza Express for a little while. I managed a bit of a starter and the majority of my main course. Straight home after.

Just been relaxing for the rest of the day, haven't really been hungry but feeling much better. Managed to update this diary with entries for the past few days.

This evening I've had another mild headache, so I've taken another 500mg of Paracetamol which seems to have done the trick.

Not sure if I'll have a snack or not tonight. I'm going to concentrate on trying to eat a little something before I go to bed. I think my body is looking forward more to another long and undisturbed sleep instead of food!

Until tomorrow...

6th February 2005 - Sunday


Another long sleep and a very lazy morning lying in bed. Felt OK when I woke, although a little nauseous, or perhaps hungry, I couldn't determine which.

I've taken my medication (1 Granisetron and 2 Domperidome tablets) late today (about 14.00) and haven't eaten as yet.

Not planning anything today apart from possibly going over to see my Mum later on this evening if I feel up to it. Apparently she's been invited out at last however, if she cannot make it, I'm sure she'll be left indoors alone with no family visits. The only local family she has apart from me is my brother and it's really been playing on my mind on how much he has been looking out for her whilst I have been unable to. Not a lot by all accounts.

Had a little something to eat for an early dinner.

Went over to see my Mum this evening for a few hours. I arrived to find her well and downstairs but her cheek was all swollen on one side. This has happened before, possibly an abscess. It took me a while to convince her to make sure she again calls the Doctor about this as soon as possible, despite an upcoming X-Ray appointment, she has been waiting three months for! I have seen so much varied Cancer at the hospital on both young and old, male and female, it frightens me to think what the outcome of my Mum's X-Ray will bring.

Despite my treatment going very well, this weekend is the first time I have had thoughts such as "will I make it to see the end of this year", stupid I know but I want to be totally honest here in this diary. Guess I'm just scared and this past week has taken its toll on me mentally as well as physically.

I returned home, made myself a little more to eat and relaxed in front of the TV until the early hours. During the course of the evening that mild headache crept back up on me for what must be the third night running, I'm not sure what's causing them? Another 500mg of Paracetamol and I went to bed.

7th February 2005 - Monday


Woke up feeling fine and hungry this morning. Quite early (09:30) considering I didn't go to bed until 02:30. Hopefully that'll mean an early night tonight, in readiness for treatment tomorrow.

Managed some cleaning around my house, had a visit from a good friend and then a bite to eat, off to the supermarket and briefly to my Mum's house again.

I feel almost normal today! Unfortunately it doesn't last long, I'm getting very tired quickly these days and it's treatment day tomorrow.

Had a blow-out dinner this evening which Ken kindly prepared for me.

Off to bed soon, dreaded hospital day tomorrow.

8th February 2005 - Tuesday


Felt really exhausted when I woke up today. Blew my nose before getting ready and developed a nose bleed, this is a worry especially when my platelets could be low. I managed to stop it quite quickly but it worried me somewhat. After a quick breakfast, Ken and I braved the traffic to the hospital, it wasn't very easy today, the roads were full.

These problems I had this morning caused me to arrive at the Hospital quite late. My blood test was taken almost immediately, but due to my unavoidable lateness there was insufficient time to squeeze in receiving my blood results along with my 6 hours of chemotherapy. Therefore it was decided that it would be best all round to have my chemotherapy treatment postponed until tomorrow.

One day off schedule will not however make any difference to my health stakes.

Once my blood test results were back it appeared that my earlier worry of a low platelet count was in fact unfounded. It turns out they have risen back to completely normal levels for the moment. However, and confirming my tiredness of this morning, my red blood count and haemoglobin levels are starting to fall in to the "we're concerned" range. A possible blood transfusion was mentioned! I will now be watching these levels carefully along with all the other counts from my blood tests that I have so far learned about.

In order to maximise my time at the hospital, it was arranged that an upcoming EDTA test be moved forward to today. This meant a bit of necessary hanging around and I was out of the Hospital by 16:30.

Straight home for a huge dinner kindly prepared by Ken and several pancakes for afters! I've been quite tired all day and once dinner had suitably gone down I got myself an early night in readiness for hospital tomorrow.

9th February 2005 - Wednesday


Awoke still feeling tired. Although it was hard, I managed to motivate myself to get up and get ready for hospital. I had a clear 8 hours sleep last night but I'm still tired.

Got to the hospital, no time for Emla cream today, straight into cannulation which took 3 attempts to take this morning.

Once my treatment was under way I was feeling quite a chill and asked the nurse to cover me with blankets. Managed to doze off for practically the rest of the day until my 6 hour treatment was finished.

So much sleep! Has to be down to the fact that I have a very low red blood count and haemoglobin level.

Once home, hit the sofa for more sleep! I haven't managed much to eat tonight and will soon retire to bed. Hopefully tomorrow I will be a little livelier as I have a plane to catch in the evening.

Night.

10th February 2005 - Thursday


Woke up after a good night's sleep and feeling much better than yesterday. A lot of things to do today.

Yesterday I left the hospital in a daze tired and exhausted; and forgot to pick up my GCSF injections from the hospital pharmacy. I need to start these injections today so another visit to the hospital is required.

Managed to complete a little work on a laptop this morning, it's ready to drop off to the owner now so I can fit that in today along with the visit to the hospital, dropping in to see my Mum and catching the plane tonight!

Ken came along with me today although I did most of the driving; managed to pickup my injections, drop off the laptop (took my first injection at this point) and get home just in time to eat and get ready for my flight.

Picked up some more clothes from my Mum's house en route to the airport.

Arrived at Heathrow in good time for my flight only to find out it had been cancelled (supposed technical problems)! They offered to fly me to Manchester and coach me to Leeds/Bradford (my original destination). I called my friends in Leeds and it made little difference to their travel time to pick me up from either airport, so Manchester it was.

Didn't expect any problems flying, although the walk to the gate tired me out. I had a brief wait to catch my breath as the plane was a little late in departing.

Domestic flights are so quick, as soon as you have ascended it seems that you begin descent and final approach.

My friends were waiting for me as soon as I appeared through the arrival doors and we were on our way to Leeds in a flash.

Relaxed and chatted for a bit, got to bed about 01:00.

11th February 2005 - Friday


Had a really nice sleep and a lazy morning.

A nice breakfast and some really tasty soup for lunch. My appetite seems to have returned quite adequately.

Went for a drive in and around Leeds for a while to get out for a bit. I am still feeling quite tired and not really motivated for much at all, one of the reasons I haven't updated my diary in a couple of days.

Returned back to base for the second of my GCSF injections which I did myself! Chose the stomach location and managed to stick myself with the needle. It wasn't too pleasant to do but needs must!

Had a wonderful dinner, watched the box, relaxed and chatted for most of the evening.

After a bath I got to bed around 00.00.

12th February 2005 - Saturday


Another good sleep and a lazy morning. I've had breakfast so the appetite is still there.

It's the Cirque du Soliel show night tonight in Manchester, going to try to conserve my energy all day today, so I can feel good for the journey to Manchester and enjoy the show.

Arrived at The Trafford Centre in good time for the show, feeling OK. I got a sneak look at the Trafford shopping centre, never been there before; quite impressed! I'll have to pay it a visit when I'm better.

The show was fantastic (as expected), I enjoyed every minute of it and was glad that, although tough, I had made the effort to visit my friends and go to the show (with a little help from them of course).

Once back to Leeds, I was soon ready for bed. I found the whole thing quite tiring.

13th February 2005 - Sunday


Woke up at 06:00 feeling nauseous; managed to ignore the feeling and get some more sleep. Once I got up, it wasn't long before I was sick. Strange, as I had only been feeling tired over the preceding days. I think it might be something to do with the fact that I ate a whole box of popcorn the night before. Oh and half a bag of liquorice!

Once up and about, I didn't really fancy any food at all and just curled up in my chair and watched the box.

My Aunt and Uncle paid me a visit and saw me change from feeling quite sick to perking up quite a bit. I was sick again in the afternoon; felt a lot better after. Soon after they left I started to feel tired again and had some doubt whether I would make my flight home.

Had a sleep for an hour or so and pushed myself to get packed and ready for the flight. All day I had only managed some water, Lucozade and a sliced apple, as I just didn't feel right at all.

I had a great but relaxed weekend with some very caring and close friends. It was hard for me to say good-bye at the airport, I could have cried easily. I had such a nice time and also did not want to face what I knew I was coming back to.

I was soon up in the clouds and headed for Heathrow. Ken was there waiting to pick me up and I was ready for and waiting to hit my bed. I was exhausted.

14th February 2005 - Monday


Woke up feeling OK. Lazed around for a bit then got up and got myself some breakfast. At least my appetite has returned.

Still lazed around, taking it very easy as I'm still getting tired out quite quickly. Tomorrow's blood test will most likely confirm my red blood count and haemoglobin levels are still low. This could possibly mean a requirement for a blood transfusion sometime this week. I'm not really keen on it, not sure why, it just doesn't appeal to me!

Time to reflect on my weekend away today, like I said a brilliant time but I certainly pushed myself to get there, be on my best and get back! I still think I needed to get away, so I'm really glad I did it.

It also occurred to me today that the 2 month marker - since I was diagnosed - escaped a mention in my diary on the 9th of this month. Time has passed so quickly recently; I've been tired, fed up and I have so much ahead of me with extra operations being mentioned and blood transfusions, my mind is all over the place.

The day soon flashed by, once I'd had a long bath I was ready for an evening in front of the box.

Had a light dinner of soup and bread, I haven't really eaten as well as I should have but my tiredness doesn't allow me much energy to do anything else.

Ready to hit the sack soon, hospital tomorrow and the apprehension is building already. I'm getting fed up with it, but I have no choice, so soldiering on is my only option.

It has been a hectic weekend and I've not been feeling great, apologies for those of you that missed out on my regular diary updates. Oh, and in case you were wondering, I didn't get any Valentines, nor did I send any!!

15th February 2005 - Tuesday


Woke up feeling OK, although still tired. After a palaver in the bathroom with a nose bleed, a painful experience on the loo and feeling exhausted, I finally got myself together for the hospital.

Once I arrived at hospital, pretty much the same happened as the previous Tuesday. I was late and told that my treatment could not be carried out until tomorrow. A blood test was taken and I waited just over an hour for the results to come through.

Everything at an all time low today: neutrophils, platelets, red blood count and haemoglobin levels. This means 3 more GCSF injections at the end of the week, beginning Thursday. Bleomycin chemotherapy treatment tomorrow and, more significantly, I am to take on 2 units of blood on Thursday.

Made my way home feeling tired and washed out. Not looking forward to tomorrow.

I wanted to go and see my Mum this evening but was just too tired to make it over to her house.

Watched the box for a bit and retired to bed with my heart beating fast if I so much as moved a little.

16th February 2005 - Wednesday


Woke up feeling OK, although still tired. After another nose bleed and, feeling exhausted, I finally got myself together for the hospital.

Once I arrived at the hospital, I was soon connected up for treatment. I didn't feel too bad and managed to stay quite lucid today, a big difference from last week's treatment.

Had a big Subway sandwich for lunch, didn't fancy any of the hospital stuff and Ken collected it for me while he was out in Hammersmith. It was certainly a large sandwich so I made it last throughout the day. Experienced a bit of a stomach ache late afternoon. Not quite sure what this could be related to, could be the chemotherapy, could be the anti-sickness drugs or could be the sandwich!

By the end of the day, I was really starting to feel the side effects of the chemotherapy. Shivering and chills were the order of the day. Once I managed to get to the car, my teeth were chattering despite me being wrapped up really warm. The stomach ache was quite chronic at times.

My heart beat was very noticeable, too. I could hear every thrust of my heart and feel it all over my body. Due to my reduced red blood count and haemoglobin levels, my heart was compensating for the lack of oxygen being carried around my body by pumping harder and faster.

Reaching home, I was in a terrible state and hit the sofa, still with my coat on and covered myself with a duvet. After some sofa sleep I managed to get myself up to bed, my heart still racing. I could hardly breathe or see properly.

I think some anxiety was mixed in to my state and I just couldn't get to sleep with the beating noise and rushing of blood in my body. Ken checked my stats (with a heart rate monitor I have) and my temperature; all seemed within tolerable limits and things settled a little for me to get off to sleep.

17th February 2005 - Thursday


Woke up heart still beating fast; no time for anything this morning, so I just got ready in my poorly state and set off for the hospital. En route to the hospital today, I could hardly hold back the tears as I have just about had enough of all of this treatment, needles and hassle. Totally fed up is the feeling. I can see the light at the end of the tunnel but I'm rapidly nearing the end of my tether.

So many ailments to cope with, along with the constant battering of treatment, is certainly dragging me down. I hope it will soon be over and I will be given a break to get back to full strength. My body has been ravaged by this cancer, now beaten into submission by the chemotherapy. The benefits of the chemotherapy far outweigh the side effects, it's just becoming a lot harder to deal with these days.

I never anticipated or had been told at the beginning that there was a possibility of having a blood transfusion, despite it being a common treatment with chemotherapy patients. Typical of me to have a rare blood group, in fact the third rarest in the country according to the National Blood Service. I am AB+, this doesn't cause any problem, it's just there's not so much of it around as other more common blood types.

Arrived at the hospital, still very washed out, heart beating fast, stomach mildly aching, exhausted and feeling quite upset. I managed to hold back the tears and got on with the events of the day.

I was soon hooked up and the blood transfusion begun. No IV machine is used (it can damage the blood), the blood is kind of free flowed straight from the bag but slightly restricted to about one drip every two seconds. I had two bags of AB+ (about 600ml, just over a pint) specifically cross-matched. All arranged blood transfusions are. It took around 4-5 hours and I certainly felt a lot better at the end of it.

If I am to suffer any side effects, they would be apparent quite quickly. My temperature and stats were checked regularly and all went smoothly.

Ken and I visited my favourite juice bar at Planet Organic in north London afterwards for a healthy smoothie and a shot of wheat grass! My stomach ache is still apparent but seems better.

Now I'm home and looking forward to my final three days break. I'm feeling hungry so I'll have to conjure something up tonight. My Dad was due to visit but is not too well himself, flu, and I cannot be exposed to that.

Over the past few days, and especially since my Wednesday chemotherapy, my chest seems to ache quite a bit, possibly my lungs reacting to the recent chemotherapy or the fact that my blood levels were at an all time low.

The transfusion today should perk me up quite a bit so, thanks to some kind soul giving some of their rare blood, I am to feel better quite soon and now understand the National Blood Services advert when they say "thanks to this person my relative's life was saved during their chemotherapy". Thanks whoever you are!

18th February 2005 - Friday


Woke up after a weird night's sleep. Got to bed at about midnight. My back and chest (lungs?) were aching quite substantially last night.

After dinner I managed to pop out briefly to surprise my Mum as she was out in a quiet local pub with my brother (thanks Martin). She was really surprised to see me turn up to have a quick soft drink with her and it made all the difference to me and I'm sure her spirits, too.

Last night was weird. When I went to bed my back was aching quite severely, right at the bottom of my spine and it felt like it was deep inside the bone. I could hardly get off to sleep and awoke at 02.30 in excruciating pain. I had to take 2 paracetamols and, once the pain was suppressed, I drifted off to sleep. Woke up several times after that, sweating badly. All in all, not a brilliant night's sleep, however I am feeling reasonably OK today.

The blood transfusion has obviously done a good job as I certainly feel a bit closer to normal again, certainly versus the last few days. I've had a very lazy morning and enjoyed a large bowl of cereal for breakfast.

I'll take it easy today, I fancy getting out a bit later so will eat well and have a long overdue relaxing bath before I decide what to do.

Didn't feel like doing anything today so lounged around after my bath. Ate some lunch then some soup for dinner because I just didn't have the energy for anything else.

Just have to postpone what I wanted to do today until tomorrow. As long as I can conjure up the energy!

Going to bed very soon!

19th February 2005 - Saturday


Last night's sleep wasn't bad although I did wake up once, suddenly, in the early hours. No idea what that was about.

A bit sluggish this morning, but managed to get moving and arranged with a friend to take a drive to visit the Masonic shop in Great Queen Street, London. Quite a journey for me! Picked up some bits for an important ceremony on the 26th, one day after my upcoming treatment. I hope to be there!!

Made my way back, with a quick visit to B&Q and McDonalds. Sat around at my friend's house for a while then head for home briefly before setting off to see my Mum for the evening.

A hectic day by my current standards, but I achieved all I needed and should sleep well tonight. I haven't managed to eat a very healthy dinner tonight, I'm out of energy and am home quite late. Soup again!

All in all, a good day, although tiring. I just don't seem to have the energy I used to have and am certainly feeling weaker in my arms and legs. No wonder, when I haven't really done much at all in the last 2 and a half months!

20th February 2005 - Sunday


From today the following entries were not made in this diary until the 15th March 2005, due to circumstances that will become apparent as you read on...

A good sleep and once up I made arrangements to have Sunday lunch with my friend at his Mum's house.

Time soon passed and I was at my friend's Mum's for dinner, she's 75 and certainly cooks a mean Sunday roast which I thoroughly enjoyed along with a massive fruit salad for afters, yummy.

Over to my Mum's to spend time with her before my 5 day stay in hospital beginning tomorrow. Picked up some clean clothes I needed and headed home apprehensively, fighting hospital thoughts from my head!

21st February 2005 - Monday


Arrived at hospital in good time today. I know the routine, so got my general admission out of the way, blood test and X-Ray.

Met up with Milton and nipped over the road for a Pizza Express with him and Ken. Straight back to hospital afterwards and awaited my cannulation and treatment start time.

Confirmation came today about my final treatment cycle, the Bleomycin chemotherapy drug was to be cut out of my schedule. My lungs have shown significant improvement (the cancer cells have shrunk to almost non existent). Any more of the 'B' part of my treatment would only damage my lungs further. So this is my FINAL week of chemotherapy, no more required, no outpatient chemotherapy visits anymore. A sobering thought and certainly something to focus on as a positive landmark in my overall treatment. Having goals, however small, has helped make things easier going throughout and almost sped up the times I've wanted to forget about so quickly!

Treatment began quite late however, due to the 'B' part of my chemotherapy being cut out, it would only mean 9 hours of treatment each day.

Not much sleep tonight, being a veteran of chemotherapy, 3 full cycles previous, I was used to this and handled it in my stride!

22nd February 2005 - Tuesday


Free of my IV machine in the early hours of this morning, I managed to get up and about for a bit today. I notified the nurses of a time that would suit me to begin my next treatment. They were happy to do this and I made best use of the time I had to myself.

This evening, I started to get nauseous and the chemotherapy was starting to take its toll on me a lot earlier than I had remembered from previous weeks.

23rd February 2005 - Wednesday


Woke up free from IV again, a slightly better night's sleep but chemotherapy still did not end until the early hours. Sleep certainly does not seem to be important in terms of treatment in hospital, I always thought that it was the time that your body rejuvenated and recovered best!?

Still feeling extremely nauseous today.

I was asked if I could be a dummy patient, like an actor in a given situation. As this helps to pass the time of day in an interesting manner, I readily agreed. I was to be told -hypothetically of course- that I was Hepatitis C positive by a trainee doctor who needed to practise their patient communication skills before an exam.

It was fun (I don't think in reality it would be much fun) playing the part and to be honest the news was broken to me a little bit abruptly and could have been a little smoother. I asked all the questions I think someone in my given situation would do in order to make it as real as possible for the trainee.

I retired to bed shortly after. I haven't eaten much at all so far, Monday was the last day I really ate a decent meal. The sheer thought of treatment, the environment, the smells, everything makes me feel sick!

Ken has visited from late afternoon onwards. Having someone around makes a great difference to time spent in hospital. I am very lucky to have had people I know around me most of the time. Other patients look lonely and left without visitors for days, perhaps it's what they wanted; I know I would have certainly felt a void if faced with the time on my own.

chemotherapy started a little after the time I requested. Another "into the night" treatment schedule ahead.

Stayed in bed for the rest of the day and, in the evening, managed some water melon Ken had brought along. It didn't stay down for long, I will not go into detail!

24th February 2005 - Thursday


Another late chemotherapy finish (early hours). Nausea much, much more apparent today and retching with sickness was the order of the day. The anti sickness drugs are not working so well for me this week, most likely to do with the fact that I cannot keep even them down for long.

Stayed in bed all day, getting up only to visit the loo. Being sick wasn't pleasant with nothing inside me to actually be sick with, my only comfort being that I didn't have to jump up and run for the loo; I had stocked up on the cardboard throw away hospital sickness bowls!

I did manage 2 bowls of cornflakes without milk tonight. It was really the only thing I could think of that I fancied and they actually stayed inside me somewhere never to be seen again!

Late start on the chemotherapy again, slightly later than I had requested but earlier than previous days, my first decent night's sleep was due. This slightly comforting thought was soon demolished as chemotherapy bag changes and delays (the nurses were extremely busy today and low in numbers) took my chemotherapy completion in to the early hours again.

25th February 2005 - Friday


My last chemotherapy day. A wonderful thought but I had no energy and felt so sick I could hardly break a smile.

Another day in bed, my friend came early today and kept my spirits up although I did sleep quite a bit so wasn't much company in return. In fact on reflection sleep was definitely a regular occurrence during the days this week, catching the odd hour here and there whenever I could.

Chemotherapy started a lot later than I would have liked. 6 West was a very busy ward this week and rumours were rife that it was to stay open for the weekend. Well I never, what a turn up for the books, I remember a time when I was the only patient left on the ward and they couldn't wait to get rid of me. Today I was even offered an extra night in the hospital if I wanted. What do you think I said to that? This was possibly to be the last ever day I had to spend in hospital and I couldn't wait to get away!!

Finally, chemotherapy finished around 7 or 8pm and after a struggle I was dressed and almost ready to go. One last big heave and I was sick for the last and final time in hospital. I was sick what must have been a record number of times this week, I really do not want to remember how I felt.

Ken took me home, I fell asleep on the sofa and eventually got up to bed in the early hours.

26th February 2005 - Saturday


Ken came in to my room to say farewell early this morning as he was off on a short break to see his family for a while. I slept in for a while afterwards.

So I was up around 12pm and a friend had arrived to look after me and keep me company until Sunday.

Masonic ceremony day today, I really wanted to be able to attend as this was an important goal for me to achieve following my treatment. After all, I had bought all the bits I needed the previous Saturday and somehow had managed to learn some important lines in between being sick and chemotherapy!

Found the energy from somewhere, got myself smartly dressed and left my house around 2.30pm.

I took it very easy indeed, was extremely glad I had made it out, completed my part of a lengthy ceremony word perfectly and even managed to stay for the meal afterwards. I picked at the food but made a good effort to get some of it inside me.

When the meal had finished, I was very tired after a long day and soon went to bed once home.

27th February 2005 - Sunday


Woke up quite early today but soon retired back to bed and didn't get up until about 3pm!!

Although in and out of sleep for most of the day I managed to watch some TV and, more importantly, eat a little food!

My friend decided to stay at my house another night and leave in the morning. My Dad is due to arrive late morning to stay with me for the week ahead.

Despite sleeping for a good majority of the day, I still felt really tired tonight. Once I made sure my friend had everything she needed and was comfortable I retired to bed for another full night's sleep.

28th February 2005 - Monday


Shortly after getting up, my Dad arrived.

Spent the day catching up with my Dad and, although weak, managed to eat a little and start to plan my week ahead, starting with a blood test tomorrow.

There is so much I want to do but just cannot motivate myself to do it, the truth is I just don't have the energy yet. It annoys me that there are things I want to do but just cannot muster the energy to do them. This chemotherapy in my system is going to take some serious flushing for me to get back to anywhere near normal.

This is my personal experience, not medical advice.
