The diary

Life afterwards

Follow-up appointments, family and later updates

Personal diaryAbout 25 min read

14 February 2025

Looking back at Charing Cross Hospital

Some of you might remember this chapter of my life… 20 years ago, I walked out of this hospital after completing some gruelling chemotherapy treatment for Stage IIIb Testicular Cancer. I didn’t know what the future held or if I’d make it another 20 years - but I hoped.

Recently, of all days, on Valentine’s Day, I walked out of those same doors for the last time. I paused, feeling a little emotional, looked back and took this picture.

Life is a gift, and sometimes the hardest journeys lead to the most meaningful milestones. Here’s to the next 20 years and whatever lies ahead! x

On 14 February 2025, I was discharged from the care of Charing Cross Hospital, twenty years after my original treatment. My HCG results continued to be below 2 (normal) at my annual February follow-ups.

See the HCG follow-up results

January 2015

Very nearly 10 years clear.

So I'm about to reach the 10 year clear milestone, but with the saddest news.

After my enduring experience with Cancer and brush with the edge of life itself, my precious and beautiful daughter, who is almost 4 years old, has just been diagnosed with a brain tumour. She's just about to undergo many years of chemotherapy, radiotherapy and proton beam therapy.

This is totally unrelated and in no way connected to my illness (genetically or otherwise), just complete and utter rotten luck. In fact I am finding it extremely hard to believe both mine and my partners luck, and of course that of my daughter. Who's child deserves such rotten luck? I have no words to describe how devastated we are. I can honestly say I would happily go through my illness all over again to take this away from my precious little girl.

You can follow her story from her website and on twitter at: www.eleanorstollery.com


Below in chronological order...


2005


September 2005 1st September 2005 - Friday


Routine blood tests, X-Ray and meeting with my Consultant today at Charing Cross Hospital.

Battled with the usual bad traffic in to London today, which meant I was slightly late for the meeting with my Consultant. Once I'd arrived and the blood tests were carried out, my Consultant had gone to an important meeting so I was seen by his Registrar instead.

I was soon sent for an X-Ray and came back to the Registrar for the results. All good. After a brief chat and confirmation of my next check-up date I left the hospital.

27th September 2005 - Tuesday


Blood test day today, popped in to my local hospital for it. Again the department was quite busy. I had to wait an hour before I was seen and tested.

My favourite vein isn't working too well anymore! It's gone a bit hard. Guess I'll have to find another good one which gives limited pain, whilst the other recovers!

The blood test only takes a maximum of 5 minutes and I was soon released with a couple of attempt holes covered by cotton wool and tape!

I've got some more questions for my consultant to answer. Hopefully on my next visit to Charing Cross Hospital I'll get to see him. See you back here on the 7th October.

October 2005 7th October 2005 - Friday

Routine blood tests and meeting with my Consultant today at Charing Cross Hospital.

I had made a late appointment with my Consultant today as the plan was to go for a bite to eat with him after my routine check-up in order to discuss a web-site he would like me to look at developing.

During my 'check-up' conversations with my Consultant I always try to give a very good indication as to how I have been feeling since we last spoke and any symptoms that I might still have or have developed.

One symptom recently has been a few mild but odd headaches in the left side of my head, my Consultant didn't seem too concerned as my treatment has been very successful and consequent blood tests/scans have shown no signs of any new activity whatsoever, nonetheless he booked me in for a low priority MRI scan on my head!

My body has been through quite a battle over the past year, I would like to -try to- think that any or all of the pains I get from time to time, sometimes daily, are just part of recovery or even old age! As my Consultant once said to me, "each and every new pain I get from now on will come as a renewed and far more heightened worry to me". The likelihood of it being anything as sinister as before is pretty slim especially as time goes on.

I have heard Cancer called the silent killer before, now I know what that really means. However, there are many positives I can turn to, one of which being that I am regularly checked up on at the hospital, so I must not ponder on what could be and just get on with my life, a plentiful one with a new perspective!

Here's an update of symptoms...

Tiredness (more apparent)
Peripheral Neuropathy (less apparent)
Tinnitus (apparent at all times, got used to it)
Tiger stripes on skin (still apparent, fading)
Lung twinges/pains (occasional) Head aches/sudden mild pains (occasional)

I have to remind myself before going out for a few too many drinks that the day after brings on the above symptoms much more! No sympathy expected there!

I managed to grab a business related chat with my Consultant following my check-up and hopefully will be working with him on a revised and informative web-site related to a very rare Cancer. I will of course post the link when it's launched!

A few days back I went to lunch with my good friend and fellow survivor Milton Mermikides someone I'm sure you are familiar with if you have followed my story or are a recent reader of the entire story! We sat in a restaurant in a totally normal environment eating totally normal food, a surreal experience for us both after what we have been through. I hadn't seen Milton for sometime and he's looking really well as I think I am too (compared to what we both looked like in hospital anyway), in fact we hadn't known anything different of each other! Both Milton and I felt like shouting from the rooftops the elation we felt about being back in the normal world doing normal things and feeling normal again! Something which to external perception no one would have any idea of, we were just two blokes sitting in a restaurant eating!

November 2005 4th November 2005 - Friday

Routine blood tests, X-Ray and meeting with my Consultant today at Charing Cross Hospital.

Breezed in to Charing Cross, no traffic today.

Saw my friendly Phlebotomists, I mentioned to them that they have now known me almost a year, as on the 10th December it will be 1 year since I was first at Charing Cross Hospital. Doesn't time fly when you're having fun!

I was soon seen by my Consultant. Not much to say today really, I collected some up to date printouts of blood results, had my physical examination and collected my X-Ray form. He wasn't happy with how the 'NHS system' had allocated a date for my MRI head scan, so this may now be prioritised a little higher and brought forward to as early as November or December 2005.

Other than that he is very happy with my progress, the treatment has been very effective and I am well and truly on my way to making it back to 100%25 health - my blood results over the past few tests show that I am still just out of the normal ranges. It takes approximately 1 year for the effects of BEP Chemotherapy to fully dissipate from the human body, in some cases longer.

Bumped in to a fellow patient in the waiting room, I met him along with his parents some time back during my treatment days in hospital and he is still battling hard it seems. I can only wish him and his family well in the face of Cancer which I am sure Charing Cross is doing everything it can to defeat.

Off for my X-Ray, as usual a quick and painless experience with what looked to my eye like a good result (the Radiographer kindly showed me the resulting scan), however it takes a few days for a Radiologist to check it out properly.

Once again I was free to leave the hospital, back in to the open world and on with my life. See you again on the 2nd December 2005.

December 2005

2nd December 2005 - Friday

Routine blood tests and meeting with my Consultant today at Charing Cross Hospital.

Arrived behind time today, been rushed off my feet this week what with arriving back from abroad later than I expected.

Blood tests carried out very quickly, I pretty much walked straight in and before I knew it I had given 4 test tubes of blood. One and most importantly for my tumour markers (HCG), two to monitor my general health (U&E's & FBC) and finally one for my testosterone levels.

Had to wait for my Consultant for a bit which was totally understandable as Clinic had all but closed for business and he had left to continue with his work elsewhere. Clinic reception kindly called him to see if he was free to come back for me.

Everything still looking good as far as my recovery is concerned, testosterone levels are within UK normal ranges, last months X-Ray results have been studied and the -some would say, rather awkward- physical examination checked out all fine.

Comments on last months X-Ray even stated that there is now little or no evidence of any damage in my lungs, something I am astounded by. A CT scan would most likely show up some scarring, but there is no need for a CT at this stage.

Certainly all signs of a fine recovery in progress.

My MRI scan has now been brought forward to the 19th December 2005 and my next routine check-up is scheduled slightly earlier on the 23rd December 2005, as I will be away when it is actually due around the beginning of the new year.

I'll put pen to paper (or keys to the web!) on my first diagnosis anniversary which is fast approaching on the 9th December 2005. I will not be celebrating as I don't think celebrating the diagnosis of a life threatening illness is very apt.

9th December 2005 - Friday


The 9th of December 2005 passed relatively quietly for me and was certainly a day I choose to reflect on the year that has passed. Although it's been tough at times, I'm most certainly glad to still be here and to have out lived the "less than a year to live" statement.

This website has been a great help to me, as an outlet for my thoughts and fears during my treatment, surgery and ongoing recovery. It now stands as a testament, a message of strength and positivity to others. Others not only suffering with a similar illness or disease to mine, but for anybody who faces a challenge of such enormity in relation to life's twists and turns. You can win through, you have to just keep on going, keep looking forwards and stay positive. I did and look where it got me, still here and living life to the full.

Not many people remembered or reminded me of this day just a few close friends. I made a note of it in my diary, although I doubt I'll ever be able to forget what happened a year ago today, I have the scars to remind me forever.

Has it changed me? Well, I have certainly been given the opportunity to see life with a different perspective completely, a gift if you like or simply a second chance in the face of death. At no point before in my life before this happened would I stop to think that a certain day would be the last day of its kind in my life, ONE LIFE, so as the 9th of December 2005 passes I will never see another day the same in my life. There is no ground hog day. So we only get one shot of this existence, make the most you possibly can of it.

What are you going to do today and with the rest of your life?

You never know what life has in store for you, what's around the next corner... this disease crept up through my body for over a year and a half, practically unbeknown to me, scary isn't it!

People ask me if I am 'cured' or 'recovered'. My understanding of being 'cured' of Cancer is there is NO cure, if you survive it and the treatment, you live in remission of the disease for the rest of your life, your body has shown the potential for Cancer so your chances are slightly higher than most (of it coming back), however there are many other health statistics that will beat this in to a minute possibility.

Am I fully recovered? NO, I am still very tired; my body is recovering from the effects of chemotherapy, which will take sometime. Although to all external appearances I might appear fit and healthy, I just do not have the stamina or the tenacity I did before. It will come with time.

All this affects my work and my finances substantially enough to reverberate for a long time to come. There is no possible way you can imagine that you would have to plan financially for a major illness in your life, you just don't think it will happen to you.

This Christmas should certainly be a better one; at least I'm not going to be eating a hospital Christmas dinner attached to a Chemotherapy IV drip!

I'm not sure how many of you out there still follow my story and have done so from its inception back in mid December 2004. It's been a long year for me, a life changing experience, how has yours passed?

19th December 2005 - Monday


Arrived early for my MRI appointment. The MRI department is normally a pretty busy place so I was kept waiting about 45 minutes for my scan.

Usual procedure, remove all metals, credit cards and pretty much everything from my person apart from my clothes. You are given a little locker to store it all in.

Once in the scanner it takes about 30-45 minutes in total to complete an MRI brain scan, half way through there is an injection of a special colourant to further highlight areas of the brain.

Whenever I go for these 'scans', be it X-Rays, CT's or MRI's I always try to judge the operators reaction once its complete, after all, I was fairly accurate in my judgement of the radiologists reaction to my first ever X-Ray over a year ago!

23rd December 2005 - Friday

I was quite late for my appointment today and I paid for it (not intentionally I might add). Today's blood test was a painful one, the blood was taken from a not often used vein. The sister was chatting to me whilst the test was underway so my mind was taken off of the pain somewhat, still, I hate it when it hurts like that.

My Consultant was called back from his staff Christmas lunch break which had almost begun (sorry!), just to speak with me. This was my last meeting with him before I was due to go abroad for a considerable length of time, a first since I had been ill.

Everything checked out fine. Results from my previous tests were discussed. Nothing showed up on the MRI, bloods returning to normal values, all looking good so far. I am in the very high 90's in terms of percentage for making a complete return to normal health. I was given an X-Ray form as it was X-Ray time again and an appointment for almost immediately after I was due to return from my trip.

Once at the X-Ray department I had quite a wait to be seen. I normally take most of the day out to do a hospital visit so this didn't concern me too much and after all my welfare is what is most important to me otherwise I would be no good to anyone. X-Ray done and I was free to go.


2006


January 2006

27th January 2006 - Friday

Back from abroad. I was quite ill whilst away, towards the end of my trip, probably the illest I've been since treatment. It's a scary place to be when you're over 4000 miles away from your home comforts. I was glad to have been given the opportunity to get away for a well deserved break but at the same time I was glad to be back within my comfort zone.

Routine blood tests and meeting with my Consultant today at Charing Cross Hospital.

Good and relatively painless blood test (I like it when it goes smoothly). If you ever wondered what goes on at a blood test have a look at Milton's recent video here, this is something we have both become pretty much accustomed to over time!

Was swiftly seen by my Consultant, again everything in order, physical examination out of the way and off I go!

My next appointment will be the last of my monthly check-ups, after which it goes to every 2 months with X-Rays on alternative visits as before.

February 2006

8th February 2006 - Wednesday

A mental note... ONE YEAR CLEAR OF CANCER IN MY BLOOD!

Did anybody really notice, not a chance, "life goes on", something to always remember!

24th February 2006 - Friday

My first official blood test since my landmark one year clear date. Today's test will eventually confirm 'officially' if I am to make the one year of untraceable HCG markers in my blood. The chances of my Testicular Cancer coming back are diminishing as time goes by and passing the one year mark just makes it all look that much better!

Arrived at Hospital in a rush today. Got a lot of things going on in my life outside of hospital now, it's a comfort to come in and get checked up but sometimes feels like a chore. However, I wouldn't miss it for the world and my appointments can be made somewhat flexible if required. So despite the drag of driving to Charing Cross Hospital, it comes pretty high up on my agenda!

As soon as I walked through the door I was greeted by my friendly Phlebotomists. Another landmark today, I WATCHED for the first time, every detail, a big deal for me, but I was feeling strong so I went for it. The needle went in and I hardly felt a thing, 4 test tubes of blood taken (it amazes me how fast it spurts out!). One and most importantly for my tumour markers (HCG), two to monitor my general health (U&E's & FBC) and finally one for my testosterone levels.

Spoke with Dr. Savage who also seemed happy the year had so far been clear for me! I showed him some amazing new images I have had created from an old CT scan, you can see them in the 'Results' section of my site, of which he was suitably impressed (thanks go to Antoine Rosset of OsiriX fame) and the Apple Mac of course!

Got the all important physical examination out of the way, collected my X-Ray appointment form, arranged my next appointment and I was off.

Once at the X-Ray department, I had to wait a little while for it to be taken. My name was called and it was all done in seconds, had a quick peek and all looked good to me. I collected quite a crowd of radiographers around me as I showed off the CT imagery and as with every time I visit them and tell my story they are amazed and pleased at my progress from what they can see was quite a nasty spread.

That was my last monthly check-up. I now move to every two months with chest X-Rays on alternate visits as before, obviously more spread out. A radiation break for my chest!

I do hope this website is helping someone out there. Please feel free to email me to ask questions... don't be afraid.

Unless anything changes, see you back here on April the 14th 2006, one year on from that scary operation (there will be new scar pictures)!

April 2006 14th April 2006 - Friday

One year on, since that daunting day.

I remember it well, in retrospect I was totally distraught with worry, fear and anxiety. I could have filled buckets with the tears I shed. An operation that I was certainly not looking forward to.

I went through a lot, although at the time it's happening to you it seems that you just have to get on with things and take each day as it comes. Dealing with the unknown seems easier in comparison. Looking back at the whole thing, it was massive and I'm very lucky to be here writing this!

Having friends and family around me, supporting me was a blessing. Reading through the 14th April 2005's diary entry really touches me. It took a fair chunk out of my life to overcome the Cancer, which the effects of, particularly financial, are still with me. My body has been through a lot, only time will tell if I am to get rid of some of the lasting symptoms... the scars will be with me forever.

21st April 2006 - Friday

Routine blood tests and meeting with my Consultant today at Charing Cross Hospital.

I watched my blood test again, every detail, I'm getting used to this now! 4 test tubes of blood taken. One and most importantly for my tumour markers (HCG), two to monitor my general health (U&E's & FBC) and finally one for my testosterone levels.

My Consultant diagnosed something new for me today! It was mentioned briefly and has most likely been with me all of my life. The diagnosis is made most often following routine screening blood tests which unmask 'hyperbilirubinemia'. It's named Gilbert's Syndrome and affects between 3 to 7% of the population, with males predominating over females. I'll have to do some more research but this may tick a lot of boxes for me and answer a lot of health related issues. If I wasn't already trying to live a healthier life, I'm going to have to try even harder now!

My lungs are still looking good, X-Ray results show scarring and gristle left in my lungs from the Cancer's demise, some of which may stay with me for the rest of my life. There were some pretty big tumours in my lungs, some the size of apples! Got the routine physical examination out of the way and off I went!

22nd April 2006 - Saturday
Today I had my Willow Foundation special day. A fantastic day out, driving exotic sports cars! A small group of us (Ken, my Dad and me) went down to a track in Oxfordshire and all had a go, courtesy of Willow. They even gave us some money to have snacks and a dinner afterwards. It was also nice to see Wendy Gough (Testicular Cancer Awareness campaigner extraordinaire) who had kindly written to the Willow Foundation some time back in order for me to be granted this special day. Sponsor the Willow Foundation... they provide fantastic and very special days for young adults who are going through horrible experiences with their health. Having something special to look forward to in the light of serious illness can make the world of difference. A VERY honourable cause.

June 2006

23rd June 2006 - Friday

Routine blood tests and X-Ray today at Charing Cross Hospital.

I watched my blood test again, every detail, I'm an old hand at this! 3 test tubes of blood taken. One test is for my tumour markers (HCG), two to monitor my general health (U&E's & FBC) and finally one for my testosterone levels. The Phlebotomist forgot to take a 4th tube today so instead of sticking me with a needle again he said he would split one of the tubes of blood in to two!

I saw my Consultants understudy today, a Registrar, usual routine, all looks OK so far and the physical examination went OK. The Registrars change from time to time it seems like they do around 6 months stints at different hospitals. My Consultant Dr. Savage still had time to say hello which is always nice, I much prefer to see him over a stranger who really hasn't had anything to do with my case. When you mention something to a new Registrar they scrabble through your file unwittingly to find the answers!

I managed a look at my X-Ray, it's a daunting thought each time I have an X-Ray, waiting for the image to appear on screen and hoping that it looks -in my limited capacity as a patient- almost clear.

Here's an update of recent symptoms...

Tiredness (stamina back to normal now)
Peripheral Neuropathy (gone)
Tinnitus (apparent at all times, got used to it)
Tiger stripes on skin (still apparent, fading)
Lung twinges/pains (occasional)
Headaches (nothing out of the ordinary)

Not much to moan about in the light of things and of course I have my life!

Financially it is still a huge struggle. I had my garage broken in to last month, lost two mountain bikes amongst other possessions. The insurance company dashed my hopes of them being covered so there's another dent in my ability to get fit again.

August 2006

18th August 2006 - Friday

Arrived promptly and got the routine blood tests out of the way. After speaking with my Consultant and complaining of some chest pains that were worrying me he recommended an X-Ray, although he was quite positive that there was nothing to worry about. So if I don't hear anything and there are no further updates on the subject then take that as good news... no news is good news!

It seems these days that any little pains or niggles can sometimes worry me on a large scale. I tend to keep the worries locked up inside too, feeling that others around have probably had enough of me complaining!

This test in particular I was almost "looking forward to" and for a long time I haven't felt as worried as I did preceding this test. My Consultant was very re-assuring and had pretty much laid my mind to rest by the time I left his room.

Bumped into a lovely couple in the clinic waiting room who I hope I will see again one day. I was recognised from this website! They had sat up and read the entire diary the night before. I stayed and chatted for a while and hope I was of some encouragement to them both, especially Theo.

Theo, I wish you all the very best in your continued treatment and recovery. Stay as positive as you can and keep in touch.

I chose not to look at the X-Ray today, it wasn't a routine one, normally I always try to sneak a peek at it. It was just how I felt.

31st August 2006 - Thursday


No news at all from the Doc. So that must be good news!

I'm abroad at the moment and awaiting Hurricane John to hit, just on the coast of San Jose Del Cabo, Mexico. Reports are that it is a Category 4 storm which can be life threatening. The eye of the storm is predicted to touch down very close indeed!

Well I've dealt with a life threatening illness let's see how I cope with this, a different experience indeed, but I have a very positive outlook.

Back soon! I hope.


2007


February 2007 9th February 2007 - Friday


Today it was confirmed that I am officially 2 years clear of Cancer. Every year further away from it all, the better. What fantastic news for me.

I feel my diary must come to an end now and I must move on with my life.

Has it changed me. Honestly? Yes and No! Life has not changed one bit, just the way I perceive things have. If anything life is a little harder but I now have a coping mechanism to help guide me through the bad times. I must say though when the good times are good, they are even more appreciated than before.

There probably isn't a day that goes by that I don't see something in the world that lights up my day, mostly it is the person closest to me and other times it's something in the world that you have come to appreciate, more so since the illness.

Every single day I think about the fact that I have had Cancer, the tinnitus I get reminds me of all of the chemicals pumped through my body and the twinges and occasional -normal- coughs remind me of the Cancer more so than anything else. The scars I have are fading, like memories fading in to the distance.

The times I remember the most whilst in hospital are the days of my illness that will stick with me forever and are some of the most intimate in my life, those that I shared them with will certainly not forget.

Something I never spoke about in my diary was the fact that the patients I met in the first male ward I was admitted to, either passed away around me or shortly after I had made their acquaintance. That was tough for me to deal with as at that stage I did not fully understand the likely outcome of my illness, so being surrounded by other people dying of a disease similar to mine was terrifying. I have come to understand Cancer is a very broad term!

I think the night and morning before my operation were the most terrifying moments for me, I could not control my emotions and they were laid bare for all to see. The moment the lift doors closed to take me up to surgery was quite honestly one of the hardest things I have ever experienced as I thought I would never see anyone ever again.

I still get upset now if I think about it too much.

Financially I still find things hard, scrambling back to financial health has been a battle in itself and has certainly pulled me down. Testosterone levels and low feelings at times also have affected the way I handle my life. In fact right now I am going through a sudden and difficult personal time and finding it very hard to deal with. Sometimes we just cannot understand what awaits us in life.

Still, I'm a survivor, am thankful for it and you can be too!

2012

February 2012



7 years clear!

Personally it has been very hard to reach this point. I have been financially ruined by my illness, people and events surrounding it. Getting back on my feet has been quite a struggle, further compounded by events that continue to haunt me.

It brings to mind a famous quote by William Shakespeare…

"Love all, trust a few, do wrong to none"

Despite this, I have soldiered on regardless and could never have imagined reaching this point in my life.
I have a beautiful partner and the miracle gift of a little baby, naturally conceived. It's so true when they say money cannot bring you happiness, it may solve a lot of 'problems' and make them go away, but for what
I have now, is truly priceless.

This is my personal experience, not medical advice.
