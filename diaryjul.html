The diary

July 2005

Check-ups and getting on with life

Personal diaryAbout 5 min read


Arrived at the hospital very much on time this morning, the traffic was a breeze.

As instructed by phone earlier in the week I went straight to Clinic 8 for my X-Ray form and then to the MRI reception to register my attendance in good time for the MRI scan.

After registering at the MRI reception I went for my X-Ray. A very jolly bunch of nurses were operating the X-Ray machine today, I had a bit of a laugh and showed them my website. Although a serious condition I am monitoring with all of these X-Rays and tests, it's nice to have a bit of a laugh, normally at my expense... just to keep the spirits high, which is just as they should be!

Straight after the X-Ray I went through for my MRI scan. My second MRI ever and I had the pleasure of putting a new scanner through its paces. I took a photo of the scanner (check the gallery soon), apparently it's worth 750,000 pounds sterling! I could certainly think of a few things to buy with that amount of money! However in this instance I think it's probably money well spent!

The X-Ray was to check up on my lungs and the MRI was a followup from my report to the Doctor that my hips/legs were giving off a creaking sensation which comes and goes at no particular time.

After my thorough zapping this morning it was off to Clinic 8 for my regular blood tests. This was quite quick and relatively painless (I am really getting used to these).

Following a short wait I was seen by my Consultant, Dr. Savage. He had by that time had a chance to go over the scans from earlier today and was pleased to announce that they were all in order. Whatever they had suspected might be up with me as a serious Chemotherapy side effect was not the case and everything looked normal. I'll just have to put up with the creaking sensation and hope that it goes away sooner rather than later. Just as with the other side effects I have accepted and am dealing with on a daily basis.

The X-Ray was also as clear as expected, there is damage to my lungs which is more apparent on a CT scan, however nothing nasty shows and in time they will hopefully get a lot better. I cannot really say that I notice any difference but then I haven't put any great amount of pressure on them yet. Just a couple of mountain bike rides out along the river which I seem to cope with very well, perhaps even better than before... but back then I suppose I was riding with developing tumours in my lungs that I didn't even realise I had!

I then paid a quick visit to the various wards that I have been treated in over the past months. It's nice to see the people who have treated me and give them a healthy smile, it makes them happy to know that 'the person' or 'the number' they treated is well again as quite often the case is that they hardly ever get to see patients again whatever the outcome. It also gives me good vibes to see them so happy that I am better and looking so much more healthy again.

All in all quite a busy day at the hospital today. A small error in my blood test schedules has been rectified and I am now on a fortnightly regime as you will see from my updated schedule. Soon it will switch to monthly.

Unless anything exciting happens, I'll be back again on the 12th to report on my findings with the surgeon's team.

In the meantime I'll be making some changes and updates to the site so watch this space.

12th July 2005 - Tuesday


I came in to the hospital today for a pre arranged meeting with one of the Surgeon's team. Something that I had arranged for once, to clear up some outstanding queries.

The scars are healing well although it'll be sometime indeed before they fade from the red lines that currently show. I have some numbness around and below my groin scar site (the Orchiectomy scar), this is totally normal and to be expected.

The clips... well this is something that I feel has still not been put to bed properly. For one thing I was told I cannot physically have one (just so I can show you all and for a reference as to what I have inside me). The other and more important factor is that there are no notes on exactly how many of these clips were used or their locations within my body.

In fact and according to the doctor I saw today, there are no notes on my surgery or the procedure whatsoever, I find this odd.

I did manage to find out that the clips are made of a type of plastic called Silastic with titanium hinges, but even then the doctor was vague about this.

15th July 2005 - Friday


My first blood test in another hospital.

I had a meeting today at the Mulberry Centre so I thought it best to get my routine blood test carried out at West Middlesex Hospital as it is right next door and very local to where I live!

An all new environment to me so I asked at main reception where the outpatients blood test department was. I was swiftly and accurately directed.

Most of West Middlesex Hospital is now relatively new buildings and the blood test department appeared clean and new. The nurses were chatty, they were so quiet I had to knock on the door and butt in on their gossip in order to get my blood test done.

All in all a nice experience in what was a foreign environment for me. I'll be coming back for more blood tests here in the future!

This is my personal experience, not medical advice.
